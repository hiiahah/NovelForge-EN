# Chapter 30: The Fourth Heroine Appears (Distant Thunder)

---

I did not sleep.

This was not strictly true — I slept the way a man on death row sleeps, in shallow, miserable intervals interrupted by my own brain screaming *three routes, three routes, THREE* like a corrupted skip counter.

But now it was Tuesday evening, I was walking back from the Decryption Session, and the System's audit was still tattooed on the inside of my skull.

*[You have exceeded the designed limit.]*

Yeah. Thanks. Noted. Filed under "things I plan to die of."

---

The session itself had been... fine.

'Fine is the wrong word. The word is *hostile atmosphere with snacks.*'

Iris had spent the entire two hours doing that thing aristocrat girls do where they're angry at you but too proud to say why, so they just weaponize courtesy instead.

"Another cipher, Lucen. Do take your time. You seem *tired* today. I wonder what could possibly be exhausting you."

'You. The sword saint. The reality-bending little sister. Pick one.'

"It was a long day," I'd said.

"Mmm." Sip of tea. Eyes like surgical instruments. "Long days do tend to accumulate around you. Rather like *people* do."

Fifteen-year-old girls should not be allowed to be that menacing. There should be a law. A registry.

Alice, perched in her corner with her collection of half-attached shadows, had just watched the two of us with those too-knowing violet eyes and eaten a biscuit with the serene confidence of someone whose affection level was safely humming at 5.

Then, as I was leaving, Iris stopped me at the door.

"Lucen."

"Yes?"

A long pause. The kind where a smart man defuses something and a tired man just stands there like a dumbass.

"...The harvest ball is in three weeks," she said, looking at the window instead of me. "The Aurelia delegation expects a partner for the opening dance. I am informing you of this as a *matter of scheduling*."

'She just — did she just —'

"I... see."

"Do you?" Her silver eyes flicked to me. "Good. Dismissed."

And the door closed in my face.

'She asked me to a dance. The Ice Courtess just asked me to a dance and rejected me preemptively on her own behalf. That's not a flag, that's a whole flag FACTORY—'

I stopped walking and stood in the middle of the path, breathing.

'No. No. We're not doing this now. One disaster at a time. Save file is corrupted enough.'

---

The campus was quiet.

Stellaris Royal Academy at night was genuinely beautiful — and I say that as a man who had spent 347 hours staring at it in 4K on a monitor. The game hadn't done it justice.

Stone pathways lit by mana-lanterns that burned with that soft blue-white flame. Old oaks lining the walkways. And above it all, the twin moons — Lume and Sere, the big one silver-white, the small one faintly amber — hanging over the academy like two coins someone had flipped and never caught.

'World detail #847: Ezralia has two moons. Dragons breed during the double full moon. This will never be relevant.'

Narrator voice: *it will be relevant.*

Anyway.

Most students were in their dorms. Curfew wasn't strict for upper years, but Tuesday nights meant most sane people were catching up on sleep or homework or both.

I was doing neither. I was doing my nightly ritual of walking between buildings while calculating my odds of surviving to graduation, which the System had helpfully stopped displaying because, and I quote, *'the number was becoming demoralizing.'*

My mana channels ached faintly. 0.009 out of 100,000. An enchanted wooden training dummy registered 0.01. I was, officially and systemically, less magical than a practice prop.

The only thing in my channels at all was that warm little ember of pale-blue — Alice's cookie trace, humming away like a refrigerator in an empty house.

'At least someone's happy in there.'

I turned the corner past the astronomy tower, heading for the shortcut along the academy's outer wall.

And I stopped.

Mid-stride. One foot off the ground. Full system halt.

Because there was someone sitting on top of the wall.

---

Twenty meters up.

The outer wall of Stellaris was twenty meters high, mana-reinforced, warded against climbing, and topped with iron spikes because this institution believed in *committing to a theme*.

No student should be able to sit on it.

And yet.

A girl. Small. Maybe fourteen in appearance, slight build, sitting on the very highest point of the wall between two spikes like the spikes were a *suggestion*. Legs dangling over the edge, kicking slowly, heels tapping stone.

Chin resting on her fist.

Golden hair — and I don't mean blonde. I mean *gold*, metallic, catching the moonlight and throwing it back like the hair itself was doing the moons a favor by reflecting them.

She was staring out at the mountain range beyond the academy grounds, utterly still except for those slowly swinging legs.

And my heart, my treacherous, speedrunner-trained, pattern-recognizing heart, did the one thing I begged it not to do.

*It recognized her.*

'Oh no.'

'No no no no no.'

---

Golden hair. Small frame. Ancient posture. Sitting at impossible heights, staring at mountains, radiating the specific brand of stillness that only comes from a creature that has watched *centuries* pass like commercials.

I knew this character.

God help me, I had *romanced* this character. Route-completion #4 in my final playthrough. Seventeen hours, eleven retries, one bad end that made me uninstall the game for a full day out of sheer respect.

**Lyra Silverclaw.**

The Hidden Route heroine. Unlockable only after completing two other routes and triggering a specific midnight exploration event in Year 2.

The Golden Dragon.

'That's not a girl on a wall. That's a geological event wearing a school uniform.'

Ancient. Centuries old — the game never gave a number, but lore text implied she'd watched the academy's founding dynasty rise *from an egg*. Polymorphed into a human child's body because, quote, "humans are easiest to observe when they think you're smaller than them."

Enrolled as a junior cadet, two years below my class, to hide among humans and study them for reasons the game didn't fully explain until Act 3 of her route.

The single most powerful being on the continent.

Not the most powerful *student*. Not the most powerful *mage*. The most powerful *thing*. The final boss of the final boss fight, the creature the other final bosses referred to with phrases like "we don't go to the western mountains" and "please don't."

And her bad end—

```
[Bad End No. 89: The Golden Reckoning]
```

I'd triggered it once. On purpose. Completionist brain, couldn't help myself.

The moon turned gold. The wings came out. And the mountain range she was currently staring at — that exact one, the one visible from this wall — stopped existing over the course of a single cutscene, because the world had failed to be worthy of the person she loved.

The epilogue text was four words: *"She chose the sky."*

I did not sleep well that night on Earth either.

---

'She's not supposed to be here.'

That was the thought circling my skull like a vulture with tenure.

Lyra Silverclaw enrolled in **Year 3**. Not Year 1. Her arrival at Stellaris was a scripted, fixed, immovable event — the game's lore was explicit that she'd spent the first two years "traveling," whatever a dragon considers travel.

*Two years early.*

She was sitting on a wall she wasn't supposed to know existed yet, at an academy she wasn't supposed to attend yet, on a Tuesday.

'Why. Why. WHY. Actually, don't answer. I know why.'

I knew why the way a man standing in a lightning field holding a nine-iron knows why his hair is standing up.

*Me.*

Alice's node activated early — because of my void. The timeline around me was warping — because of my void. And now a creature whose entire sensory apparatus was built to detect *anomalies in reality itself* had caught a whiff of the only true void on the continent and come to investigate, two years ahead of schedule.

'I am a glitch in the matrix and the matrix keeps sending increasingly powerful women to check the error logs.'

My mana void wasn't just a stat deficiency. To something like her, I was a black hole in a star field. A hole in the shape of a boy. The one thing in her centuries of existence that read as *nothing* — and nothing, to a creature that has seen everything, is the most interesting thing in the world.

'I need to leave. Calmly. Quietly. Like a man who did not just make eye contact with extinction—'

---

She turned her head.

Twenty meters up, against the moonlight, and I saw her eyes.

Gold. Molten gold. Vertical slit pupils that no human had, no human *could* have, pupils built for a hunting dragon's skull and currently installed in the face of a fourteen-year-old girl like a software bug the developers left in on purpose.

She looked at me.

I looked at her.

Time didn't stop. It did something worse — it *stretched*, thin and transparent, like the whole world was holding its breath to see what the small god on the wall would do.

My body, traitorous veteran of 347 hours, did the thing I hated most: it displayed my knowledge. My weight shifted to my back foot. My shoulders angled — subtly, professionally, *speedrunner-ly* — toward the archway that represented the fastest escape vector. My heart rate spiked into a pattern that anything with dragon-grade hearing could read like sheet music.

Not generic fear.

*Targeted* fear.

The fear of a man who knew exactly what he was looking at and had already calculated that running was pointless and was doing it anyway.

'Damn it. DAMN IT. Min-jun, you absolute bronze-tier idiot, she can SMELL recognition—'

And Lyra Silverclaw smiled.

Small. Slow. Feline. The smile of a cat that has just watched a mouse explain, in exquisite body language, exactly which drawer the cheese is in.

She held my eyes for one more heartbeat.

Then she turned back to the mountains, chin on her fist, legs swinging, as if I were a mildly interesting footnote in a very long evening.

Dismissed.

By a dragon.

'You know what? Fine. GREAT. I accept the dismissal. I have never been so honored to be beneath someone's notice. Please continue not noticing me for, oh, the rest of my natural life—'

I walked away.

Quickly. Casually. With the relaxed, unhurried gait of a man power-walking directly toward his dormitory like it was the finish line of Any%.

---

No system windows popped up on the walk back.

That scared me more than anything.

The System was usually *johnny-on-the-spot* with its little notifications, its cheerful astronomical death predictions, its garnish of sarcasm. Silence meant it was thinking. The System thinking had never once led anywhere good.

I got inside. Locked the door. Checked the window — wall not visible from here, small mercy. Sat on my bed. Did not turn on the lamp.

Then, and only then, it spoke.

'Eight hundred kilometers.'

Eight hundred kilometers. My void — my pathetic, embarrassing, sub-training-dummy void — had been felt *eight hundred kilometers away*, because it turns out being the only true nothing in a world of something makes you the loudest thing on the continent.

'I'm not invisible. I'm a lighthouse. A lighthouse that only apocalypses can see.'


I put my face in my hands.

"Break down the odds for me," I mumbled through my fingers. "Go on. Do the math."

"Why her," I said to the ceiling. "Of all the heroines. Iris, fine, manageable, human-scale manipulation. Rhea, fine, stabby but honest. Alice, catastrophic but she's *nice*. Why did it have to scale all the way up to — to — *geological.*"

'Because of course it did. Of course the difficulty curve scales. That's how games WORK, Min-jun, you absolute walnut. You maxed survival in a romance game and the game interpreted that as a *challenge.*'

And then, because the universe hates me personally and has a scheduling department:

```
[Message from Management]

[Goddess: 'I felt that.']
[Goddess: 'All the way from here, I felt the little dragon girl notice you. I dropped my tea. I do not have tea. I manifested tea specifically so I could drop it.']
```

"Hello, Goddess. Nice of you to check in. Loving the support."

```
[Goddess: 'DO YOU KNOW WHAT SHE IS.Of course you know what she is, you cleared her route, you SAW what she does to a map.']

[Goddess: 'AND NOW THERE'S A DRAGON.']
```

"I didn't DO anything," I hissed at the glowing window. "I walked home! That was the whole action! Walked! Home! She was already ON the wall!"

The window flickered. The next line appeared one character at a time, which was the single most theatrical thing the System had ever done, and I hated that I noticed.

```
[Goddess: 'Here lies Lucen Gray.']
[Goddess: 'He survived being born dead, only to be loved to death.']
```

I stared at it.

'Mother of God.'

"...It's good," I admitted, because honest criticism was the least I owed a deity who'd stuck around this long. "It's *mean*, but it's good. Scanlon is strong. The twist lands."

```
[Goddess: 'Thank you! I think the second line'— ]
[Goddess: 'WAIT. No. I am not accepting compliments on your TOMBSTONE from YOU.']
[Goddess: 'Go to sleep. Train at dawn. Smile at the sword saint, flatter the Ice Courtess, keep the little void sister tethered, and for the love of ME, do not do anything interesting within the dragon's sensory range.']
[Goddess: 'Which, as we have established, is roughly the size of a kingdom.']
[Goddess: 'Goodnight, Lucen Gray. Tomorrow, the real year begins.']
```

The window dimmed.

I sat on my bed in the dark, listening to my own heartbeat, while 0.009 mana of absolutely nothing hummed with one pale-blue cookie ember and, now — I could feel it, faint as a memory, distant as the mountains — something gold.

Attention. Warm. Patient. Far away.

Like sunlight on the back of my neck, from eight hundred kilometers every direction at once.

'She noticed me.'

'The ceiling noticed me.'

Ilay down. I did not sleep. The twin moons crawled across my ceiling like two coins someone had flipped and never caught, and somewhere out by the wall, the most powerful being on the continent was thinking about a hole in the shape of a boy.

*'I am so, so screwed'* didn't even begin to cover it anymore.

We needed a new word.

---

*On the outer wall, high above the sleeping academy, Lyra Silverclaw watched him go.*

*She did not turn her head again. She didn't need to. Her hearing followed him across the grounds, up the dormitory stairs, into his room — the quick double-beat of a heart that knew precisely what it had seen.*

*That was the interesting part.*

*Humans who glimpsed her true eyes felt a fog of unease, animal and vague, prey sensing a shape in tall grass. They never knew why. It never sharpened into anything.*

*This boy's fear had been sharp. Surgical. His feet had charted an escape route. His pulse had spoken a word no pulse should be able to speak.*

*Her name.*

*Lyra stretched, arms over her head, small spine popping. For half a heartbeat, the moonlight caught her silhouette wrong — too large, too wide, the shadow on the stone behind her folding wings that weren't there, against a body that had never fit inside human skin.*

*Then it passed, and she was just a girl on a wall again.*

*Three hundred and eleven years ago, give or take, she had decided she understood the world. The mana of it sang in patterns — mountains hummed low, oceans hummed deep, people hummed in brief bright sparks that opened and closed like day-flowers. Everything was something. Everything had a note.*

*Until tonight.*

*A void. A blank measure in the music. A boy-shaped place where the world's song simply... stopped.*

*'What* are *you?' she thought, and the thought was warm.*

*She knew what the Abyssal resonance node was — she'd felt *that* wake early too, three days past, which is what had pulled her off the road and toward this little stone school two years before she'd intended. She had come to investigate a crack in the world.*

*She had not expected the crack to have homework.*

*He was afraid of her. He knew her. And he had left — not fled, not worshipped, not attacked. Just... left, politely, at high speed, like a man declining an invitation from a hurricane.*

*No one had ever declined her anything before.*

*Lyra smiled at the mountains. Her mountains. The ones that would look so lovely gold.*

*She had centuries of patience. She had outwaited kings, religions, and one extremely stubborn glacier. She could outwait a boy.*

*But first — she yawned, cavernously, a jaw-unlocking yawn with entirely too many teeth involved — there was orientation tomorrow. Junior cadet induction. Human school. Roll call and uniform fittings and little ceremonies with little songs.*

*How tedious.*

*How* delightful.

*She swung her legs over the wall and dropped twenty meters into the dark, landing as softly as a falling leaf, as lightly as a landslide.*

*'Sleep well, little hole-in-the-world,' she thought, padding barefoot toward the junior dormitories, moonlight gilding her hair.*

*'I'll see you soon.'*
