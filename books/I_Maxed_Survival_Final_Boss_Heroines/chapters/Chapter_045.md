# Chapter 45: The Shadow Banquet: Arrival

The Crystal Ballroom was not a ballroom.

It was a war crime committed by an interior designer with unlimited funding.

I stood at the top of the entrance staircase and looked down at a ceiling that wasn't a ceiling — it was the night sky. An actual, enchanted, astronomically accurate night sky, complete with constellations I recognized from the game's loading screens. Floating candelabras drifted between the tables like lazy golden jellyfish. A string quartet played something elegant enough to make my high school years feel personally attacked.

'Three hundred and forty-seven hours of gameplay,' I thought. 'And the banquet scenes were always the ones I skipped.'

I pressed X through a lot of dialogue in my past life.

That was coming back to bite me now, because I had absolutely no idea what the etiquette was for this event, and there was no skip button in real life.

"Lord Ashford—"

"Young Master Lucas, your father sends—"

"I heard the Sword Saint himself remarked on your—"

And in the backwash of his gravitational field, utterly unnoticed, one (1) unemployed Korean man in a secondhand suit slipped down the last three steps and began speed-walking toward the perimeter.

'Perimeter run. Classic strat.'

In every stealth game I'd ever played, the edges of the map were where you lived. Corners were furniture for cowards. I had thirty seconds, maybe, before anyone registered I existed, and I intended to spend them securing a position near the western terrace doors — maximum exit options, minimum sightlines.

I made it nineteen seconds.

"...Just to confirm, I would be worth exactly one candy."

"Devastating," Finn whispered, hand over his heart. "Devastating negotiation."

The ballroom's ambient noise changed before I saw anything.

There's a sound crowds make when something important happens — a hush-parting-ripple thing, like a stone dropped into a pond full of overdressed people. Conversations dipped. Heads turned toward the main doors.

The string quartet, somehow sensing their cue, shifted into something with more gravitas.

The doors opened.

And Iris Aurelia arrived.

She didn't *enter*. Entering is what you do to a room. She *arrived*, the way weather arrives — with inevitability and an implied threat to your crops.

Formal Imperial dress. Deep midnight fabric with copper threading that caught the candelabra light every time she moved. Her hair was up — actual, deliberate, engineered elegance — and her golden eyes swept the ballroom with the calm of someone auditing it.

The crowd parted.

It wasn't fear, exactly. It was respect mixed with fear, which is functionally the same thing in aristocratic gravity.

'0.3 seconds,' I counted. 'She'll find me in 0.3 seconds.'

Her eyes crossed the room.

Passed over nobility. Passed over faculty. Passed over Lucas Ashford himself without pausing.

Found me leaning against a marble column, holding cider like a shield.

'0.2. She beat my estimate.'

She had been watching since I walked in.

I knew this because our eyes met for exactly one second before I broke eye contact like a man defusing a landmine.

But in that one second, her gaze had gone to my feet.

Her boots.

On my feet.
