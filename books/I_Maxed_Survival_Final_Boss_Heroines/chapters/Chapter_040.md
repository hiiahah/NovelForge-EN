# Chapter 40: The Quiet After the Storm

---

'Achoo.'

I sneezed.

'Achoo.'

I sneezed again, wrapped in an infirmary blanket older than the kingdom's founding charter, and glared at absolutely nothing.

"Right," I muttered. "Somebody's talking about me."

Scratching my cheek, I stared up at the ceiling.

In a world like this — a world inside a game — superstition wasn't superstition. It was *mechanics*. Sneezing twice for no reason meant a flag had just snapped shut somewhere.

Somewhere out there, a quill had just signed my name onto a piece of paperwork, and my death flags had just updated like a software patch I never agreed to install.

'Wonderful. I love being scheduled.'

---

Day 29. Tuesday.

Evening.

After the infirmary staff finally got tired of my face and discharged me with a grumpy *"Don't come back unless you're actually dying this time,"* I decided to walk.

Not back to my dorm. Not yet.

Just... walk.

The festival was over. The stalls had come down. The banners that had screamed cheerful nonsense all week were being folded away, and the academy grounds had returned to their usual state — quiet, enormous, and faintly smug about it.

Stellaris Royal Academy, post-calamity, smelled like cut grass and cooling stone.

My face was still raw from the frostbite. My mana — what little the concept of 'my mana' even meant anymore — was declining in a way the healers kept calling *"clinically interesting,"* which is healer-speak for *"we have no idea what you are but please don't die before we can publish."*

I shoved my hands in my pockets and walked the outer perimeter wall.

'Somewhere in a tower somewhere, a kind old woman is probably watering her plant right now and thinking about me fondly.'

I suppressed a shiver.

Don't think about Voss.

Thinking about Voss was like thinking about the ocean while standing in a boat — technically fine, spiritually catastrophic.

---

I found a bench near the outer wall. Sat. Leaned back.

The sky was doing that thing where it couldn't decide between orange and violet, so it just said *screw it* and did both.

Two moons. Already visible.

One pale gold. One pale blue.

'Right. Still not my world.'

Twenty-nine days.

Day 1, I'd been a nameless servant boy with a broom, a mana index of 0.009, and a life expectancy measured in chapters, not years.

Day 29, I was a festival champion of exactly one duel that lasted three seconds, an accidental collector of three final boss heroines, a cosmic anomaly some priesthood somewhere probably had a flowchart about, and — if the System's alerts were anything to go by — the personal mentee of a woman who believed sincerity could file paperwork.

'I speedran this game in 347 hours. I've seen every ending. Every route. Every bad end, all one hundred and eighty-two of them, catalogued in a spreadsheet because I have a problem.'

'And not a single one of them had a route flag called *0.009 Mana Index Speedrun%.*'

"I've glitched the game," I told the moons.

The moons offered no comment.

Off in the distance, someone was laughing near the main plaza. Crickets or their fantasy equivalent were tuning up for their nightly shift. The wall behind me hummed faintly with the academy's defensive enchantments — 0.01-league mana, most of it. Even the *wall* outclassed me.

'And yet the final bosses keep showing up at my bench.'

Even the System had gone quiet tonight. It had been quiet all day, actually. Ever since the infirmary, ever since that alert about Elara Voss and *The Shepherd's Lamb*...

No snark. No notification. No sarcastic blue windows.

The Evil Goddess was being *subdued.*

Which, if you've ever had the misfortune of being watched by a snarky omniscient entity, you know is approximately ten thousand times more unsettling than her talking.

'She's being quiet. That's worse. That's the *movie trailer* version of quiet.'

I exhaled slowly and let my eyes close for a second.

Just one second.

A whole entire second of peace —

"Excuse me."

— annnnd there it was.

---

I opened my eyes.

I looked up.

A girl was sitting on the wall.

My bench backed against the academy's perimeter wall, and she was sitting *on* it — directly above me, legs swinging, close enough that her shadow had been draped over my left shoe the whole time.

The same perch as before.

The plaza. Last week. When I'd felt something staring at the back of my skull with the weight of a mountain range taking a casual interest in a bread roll.

A junior cadet uniform. Brand new. Fold lines still visible, meaning it had been issued *very* recently.

Golden hair that caught the sunset like the sunset was just doing its hair for it.

And the eyes.

Golden eyes.

Pupils like slits.

'Ah.'

'Right. So that's happening.'

My heart rate did a little drum solo and my soul briefly packed a suitcase.

Every alarm bell in twenty-nine days of acquired survival instincts started ringing in multiple languages, but the loudest one was simply this:

*Dragons do not swing their legs off walls unless they are feeling friendly.*

"You're Lucen Gray," the girl said.

It wasn't a question. Dragons didn't ask questions they already had answers to. That was actually in the game lore — a dragon considering you *worthy of a real conversation* was listed in the epilogue trivia of her hidden route.

Which I knew. Because I had a spreadsheet. Because I have a problem.

"...Yes," I said.

My voice came out admirably stable.

My soul was screaming, but my voice — professional, dignified, the voice of a man who had already been terrorized by a demon princess, an eldritch emergency, and a sword saint — was completely under control.

"1.58 grade point on the composure scale," I murmured.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She tilted her head.

"I'm Lyra," she said. "I'm new."

'You are not new. You are* ancient.* You are older than the kingdom, older than the academy's founding charter, older than the *concept* of paperwork — probably.*

"Nice to meet you, Lyra."

Her feet swung. They didn't reach the ground from atop the wall, and she was swinging them anyway, heels occasionally tapping stone. It was aggressive, weaponized cuteness.

'Calculated. She's performing being a child. She has had literal centuries to study what humans find non-threatening, and she chose* this.*'

"You were in that duel yesterday," she said. "The fast one."

"That's one way to describe it."

"Three seconds."

She leaned forward, elbows on her knees, chin in her palms. Her golden eyes reflected the sunset like coins at the bottom of a fountain.

"The whole festival is talking about it," she said. "A boy with no mana who stood perfectly still while something much stronger tried to kill him, and the whole thing was over before anyone's heart skipped a beat."

I said nothing.

"I've been alive a very long time," Lyra said, "and I've never seen anyone stand that still while something tried to kill them."

She paused.

"That's either very brave or very broken."

She said it *casually*.

She said *a very long time* the way a child might say *I got promoted to the next class*, like it was an unremarkable Tuesday fact, like the temporal weight behind those words wasn't the equivalent of a cathedral landing on your chest.

And the worst part —

She was doing it on purpose.

Not a slip. Not carelessness.

A *test.*

'She's checking whether I flinch at the word count of her life. She's watching to see if a normal first-year would just nod past that sentence, or...'

'Or notice that no child says that. Ever.'

"Both, probably," I said.

Lyra blinked.

Then she laughed.

It was a bright, delighted sound, the kind of laugh bell-makers probably spend their whole careers trying to bottle. Genuinely, horrendously, irritatingly cute.

And underneath it —

A note.

Just for a second, buried in the harmonics like a bassline under a lullaby, there was something else. Something resonant. Something that had stood in the sky over burning continents and heard ten thousand human laughs and somehow, inexplicably, was still not bored of them.

'Hearing her laugh is like hearing a antique bookstore tell you a bedtime story.'

'Centuries in there. And she still finds us funny.'

I was so, unbelievably, catastrophically screwed.

---

She dropped.

Not climbed. Not jumped.

*Dropped.*

From the top of the perimeter wall — which, I remind you, was enchanted and tall — to the grass in front of my bench.

Silently.

No thump. No rustle. Not even the academic courtesy of displaced air.

A child's body, hitting the ground like a rumor.

'Right. Yes. Because dragons don't obey gravity. Gravity is a courtesy they extend to the mortals around them when they're in the mood.'

She walked up to me.

Golden hair catching the last of the sun, little cadet shoes silent on the stone path, slit-pupil eyes bright with what a normal person might mistake for childlike curiosity.

'It is not childlike curiosity. It is* draconic *curiosity. The difference is that when a child is curious about an ant, the ant gets poked. When a dragon is curious about an ant, the ant gets studied, categorized, named, and possibly adopted.'

She stopped directly in front of me.

Even standing on tiptoe — which she immediately did — the top of her head only came up to my chin.

She leaned in.

And sniffed.

"Wh—"

She sniffed again.

*Sniff.*

"You smell interesting," Lyra announced.

My life flashed before my eyes. Not because I was about to die, but because I was about to be *perceived*, and with this woman those were functionally the same hobby.

"That's... not something people usually say to me," I managed.

"I'm not people."

She said it without a single gram of shame.

She grinned. A wide, sunny, absolutely-mammalian grin that had absolutely no business being on a creature whose true form could be seen from multiple postal codes simultaneously.

"You smell like nothing," she said. "Like a hole in the air."

My blood attempted to flee the country.

"But the edges of the hole," Lyra continued thoughtfully, tapping her chin, "smell like fire."

*Iris. Iris's study room. The warm room. The reader of shameful poetry.*

"And ice."

*Alice. The eldritch entity's cold. The frostbite on my face that was still healing, right now, at this very moment, audibly obvious to her nose.*

"And old books."

*The library. Three weeks of research into things no first-year should have noticed, fossilized in my coat.*

"And something sad."

She tilted her head the other way.

"That part's nice. The old things I meet usually stop being sad. They forget how. It's good that you still have it. It means you're young and therefore not boring."

She was reading my entire recent life.

My entire recent twenty-nine days — every room I'd stood in, every crisis I'd survived, every heroine's gravity I'd been dragged through — smelled, to her, like a *library book with a tragic ending.*

'She's not reading your mind. She's reading your past, through your scent. Twenty-nine days of plot, decoded through a nose.'

'The mana void doesn't hide you from her. It* frames *you. You're the only blank space in the whole world, and the edges of the world cling to you and tell her everything.'

The System had once said that 0.009 wasn't a weakness, it was a *signature.*

Standing in front of Lyra Silverclaw, watching a dragon in a child's uniform smell my biography, I finally understood exactly what kind of signature it was.

It was a *footnote.*

And she was a *scholar.*

"You're very strange, Lucen Gray," Lyra said.

She didn't say it like a threat.

That was the bad sign.

Monsters said things like *"You're very strange"* as the prologue to eating you. Dragons — *this* dragon, anyway, the one whose hidden route I'd played through three times including the bad ending where she leveled four mountain ranges and a moderately-sized lake — said *"You're very strange"* the way an entomologist says it when they find a new species.

*Oh? You? You shouldn't exist, and yet. Here you are. Enthusiasm.*

"I like strange things," she continued. "They're more interesting than normal things."

"There's been a running theme in my life lately," I said weakly.

She held out her hand.

Palm up.

In it sat a small piece of candy, wrapped in golden foil. Butterscotch, by the deep amber color of it. The foil had no branding, no maker's mark. It looked handmade.

'...In her route. I'm actually processing, in real-time, that her route has begun, in her act of offering me candy.'

'Because in Eschaton Hearts, in Lyra's hidden route, this was the first bonding event. The MC accepts the candy from the strange new cadet without knowing anything, and the game:

> **Flag Updated: Lyra Silverclaw — Trust +1.'**

And I'd always found it a little cute? A little quaint? The game framing a kid offering candy as the inciting romance event of an ancient dragon route?

Now I was standing inside it.

Now I understood the *mechanics.*

Dragons don't give things.

Dragons *hoard.*

A dragon's treasure is the axis of their soul — the literal, magical, load-bearing architecture of their psyche. Their hoard *is* their identity, their lair, their history crystallized into objects.

And when a dragon gives something *away?*

From their own hand?

It means —

'It means they have decided the recipient belongs in the collection.'

"Well?" Lyra said.

Her eyes were pure, sunlit gold. In the fading light, there was nothing in them but cheerful, inquisitive, utterly innocent expectation.

It was the best-acted innocence I had ever seen and I had once watched Iris Ashvert pretend to be bad at lying.

'Don't take it. Obviously don't take it. The flag, the bond, the moment a dragon —'

I took the candy.

I took the candy because I am Korean and speedrunning is my religion and when an ancient dragon offers you a piece of butterscotch at dusk on Day 29, the correct play, from a pure *optimization* standpoint, is to not insult her by refusing.

'Also I didn't know how to refuse a dragon.'

'Nobody does.'

'Nobody in history has.'

Lyra's smile widened.

It was lovely. It was genuine. It was the expression of someone who had just, politely and legally and with full paperwork, taken ownership of an interesting thing.

Something old stirred in the air for half a second — satisfaction, heavy as the sea, delighted as a child — and then it was gone.

"See you around," Lyra Silverclaw said.

She gave me a little wave.

Then she turned and skipped away into the evening, golden hair bouncing, cadet shoes tapping the path, humming something under her breath that might have been a children's tune or might have been a lullaby from an empire that fell eleven hundred years before the founding of this kingdom.

I watched her go.

The sun was almost down.

And as she got further away, as the last light caught her from behind —

Her shadow on the path stretched out in the wrong shape.

Too large.

Spread away from her shoulders along the ground in two enormous, geometry-defying sweeps that didn't correspond to any part of a small girl's anatomy.

Wings.

'Yeah.'

'Yeah. That's a dragon.'

I stood there for a long moment, holding one piece of handmade butterscotch like an idiot, watching a five-hundred-plus-year-old apex terror skip away into the campus twilight.

---

I unwrapped the candy.

I put it in my mouth.

It was good.

It was *very* good. It was — and I am a man who has eaten at forty-two different convenience stores across three neighborhoods of Seoul and has opinions about tuna triangle kimbap — the single best butterscotch candy anyone has made in the history of candy.

Of course it was.

She had probably perfecting that recipe for six hundred years.

She probably invented butterscotch.

'I just accepted a gift from a dragon.'

'In draconic custom, I have basically co-signed a social contract I cannot read, with terms I do not know, written in the blood-oath calligraphy of the species that invented hoarding.'

'...It tastes really good though.'

A familiar, terrible *ping* echoed in my head.

Blue light unfolded in my vision, and the Evil Goddess's particular flavor of snark — withheld all day, stockpiled all evening, and now deployed with the enthusiasm of a professional — painted itself across the dusk sky.

```
[System: Contact Event Detected.]

[Lyra Silverclaw — Hidden Route: The Golden Dragon — has made direct contact.]

[She scanned your complete mana signature through olfaction.]

[She identified your void-nature in approximately 3 seconds.]

[For reference: The office of the Vice-Headmistress requires paperwork to identify what your teacher's hidden route figured out in one sniff. Humanity is doing great.]

[She offered you a gift: 1x Handmade Butterscotch.]

[You accepted.]

[In draconic custom, accepting a dragon's gift initiates a social bond.]

[You just accepted a social bond with a dragon.]

[Congratulations. You are now marked as "hers to observe." This is the draconic equivalent of a bookmark.]
```

"Perfect," I said out loud.

I chewed the candy.

"That's perfect. Long-term-planning apex predators collecting bookmarks now. Incredible species."

[That is three final bosses and one ancient apex hoarder.]
```

"You don't have to say it like a leaderboard."

```

I stared up at the two moons.

The dragon-etched snark floated there in the evening blue, glowing gently, and the wind moved through the festival-worn grass, and somewhere behind me the academy hummed its 0.01-grade enchantments with more magical presence than my entire soul.

I finished the candy.

"Evil Goddess," I said quietly. "Am I... already dead?"

```

[The last person she offered it to was her first rider.]

[Her* dead *first rider.]

The wind went through me.

Somewhere in the distant evening, a girl was still humming.

A song older than the country, probably.

'She chose you, a little bit, already. Not for romance. Not yet. Just as an* interesting *thing on the shelf.'

'And dragons... do not get rid of their interesting things.'

I stood alone on the perimeter path of Stellaris Royal Academy on Day 29, flecks of golden butterscotch foil folded in my palm, and watched the world — four heroines deep and one hidden dragon deep into chaos — glimmer quietly under the two moons.

Tomorrow, Rhea would be waiting.

Tomorrow, the sword saint would want answers I didn't have.

Tonight, though.

Tonight there was only the wind, the moons, and a piece of draconic candy dissolving on my tongue like a small, sweet verdict.

```

[The Evil Goddess means that sincerely, for once.]

"...Yeah," I said.

I put my hands back in my pockets.

I started the walk back toward my dorm, leaving the sunset and the dragon's shadow and the first night of the rest of my terrible life behind me.

Back to my room. Back to morning. Back to whatever came next.

'Survive.'

'It has only ever been: survive.'

I just hadn't expected the world to start collecting *me* in return.
