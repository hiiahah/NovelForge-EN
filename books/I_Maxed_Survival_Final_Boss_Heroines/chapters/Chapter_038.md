# Chapter 38: The Morning After: Everyone Reacts

---

**[Scene 1 — The Cafeteria, 7:42 AM]**

Lucas Ashford had been staring at his eggs for eleven minutes.

Not eating them. Staring. The way a swordsman stares at a diagram of a technique he can't wrap his head around, looking for the angle that makes it click.

Three seconds.

That's all it had taken. Three seconds, one wrist rotation, and Luther Brightmore — fifth-ranked, arrogant, insufferable, legitimately powerful Luther — had gone from "cast completed spell" to "unconscious on the arena floor with his own magic detonating in his face."

The Void Counter.

Lucas had fought Lucen. Personally. In the training yard, with real intent. He knew, better than anyone in this academy, exactly what that boy's defense felt like from the other side — like swinging at a river, like the concept of "hitting him" had been filed for a permit and denied.

But the counter was different.

The counter was offense. Decisive, surgical, elegant offense. Perfectly timed to the hundredth of a second. Executed by someone with the mana reserve of a wet matchstick.

'That wasn't luck. Luck doesn't repeat. Luck doesn't look like *practice*.'

"You're doing the thing again," Cedric said.

"What thing."

"The thing where you stare at food like it insulted your family."

Across the table, Mariel didn't even look up from her tea. "He's been reliving the duel since six this morning. He woke me up doing footwork drills in the common room. Barefoot. Humming."

"I wasn't humming."

"You were *absolutely* humming."

Lucas set down his fork. Which was, for the record, the first time it had left his hand without food on it all morning.

"He's hiding something," Lucas said.

Cedric grunted. "Obviously. Everyone's hiding something. I hide the fact that I don't understand half of what you two talk about."

"No. I mean —" Lucas leaned forward, and there was that look in his eyes, the earnest one, the one that made Mariel sigh because she knew exactly where this conversation was going. "He's carrying something heavy. And he won't let anyone help him carry it."

Mariel finally looked up.

"You sound like you want to be his knight in shining armor."

"Maybe I do."

He said it simply. Honestly. Without a single shred of embarrassment, like he'd just commented on the weather. Because that was Lucas Ashford. The protagonist. The boy who ran toward things.

Cedric choked on his bread.

Mariel closed her eyes. "Lucas. You dueled him in a training yard three weeks ago and he treated the whole thing like a dental appointment."

"And afterward he gave me pointers," Lucas said. "Real ones. Ones that worked. A nineteen-year-old nobody with no mana, no house, no patron, and no reason to help me — and he spent an hour fixing my guard. Then he tried to leave out the back door like he'd committed a crime."

"The back door was *locked*," Cedric said. "He stood there rattling it for a full thirty seconds pretending that was his plan all along."

"The point," Lucas continued, steamrolling over the laughter with the unstoppable momentum of a true protagonist, "is that everyone in this school is asking the wrong question. They're asking 'who is Lucen Gray?'"

He paused.

"I think the better question is, 'who hurt him so badly that he flinches every time someone tries to be his friend?'"

The table went quiet.

Even Cedric stopped chewing.

Mariel looked at Lucas for a long moment, then down at her tea, and something in her expression shifted — softened — into something dangerously close to respect.

"...You're going to adopt him, aren't you."

"I'm going to be his friend whether he likes it or not."

"That's the same thing, coming from you."

Lucas Ashford smiled, picked up his fork, and finally ate his eggs.

Somewhere across campus, in an infirmary bed, a sleeping Korean gamer's eye twitched violently, for no reason he would ever be able to identify.

**[Scene 2 — Training Grounds, 5:58 AM]**

Rhea Valtor had been training for two hours.

Alone.

The Ashenveil Retreat unfolded beneath her feet like a prayer she'd been reciting since she was twelve — step, pivot, yield, flow. The Seventh Legion's lost footwork. The ghost-dance of a dead army. She had performed it alone in cold dawns for seven years.

Seven years.

Nobody had ever watched. Nobody had ever *seen* it. It was a thing she did the way other people breathed — privately, automatically, without witness.

Then a nobody with bandaged hands had looked at her form for eleven seconds and said, *"Your third transition bleeds momentum. Try shifting weight to the back foot earlier."*

And he'd been right.

And now he wasn't here.

Rhea completed the sequence. Held the final posture. Let the cold morning air fill her lungs.

He wasn't here because he was in the infirmary. Frostbite. Mana depletion. Carried in past midnight, barely conscious, with half the dormitory frozen to the walls behind him and Alice Rimehart's fingers locked around his wrist like a shackle.

Rhea started the sequence again.

Step. Pivot. Yield. Flow.

'Standard protocol: assess, adapt, continue. The incident is resolved. The individual is receiving medical care. There is no operational reason for deviation from schedule.'

Step. Pivot.

'The schedule has not changed.'

Yield.

'I have not changed.'

Flow.

Her hands were shaking.

Rhea stopped dead in the center of the training ground and looked at them.

Not from exertion. She could run the Ashenveil Retreat forty-one consecutive times before her form degraded. She had counted. She counted everything.

No — her hands were shaking because she had run.

Last night. When the alert bells rang. When someone in the corridor had shouted *the north dormitory* and *Gray's in there* in the same breath, Rhea Valtor had run.

Across campus. In the dark. Over a hedge. Through a flower bed that had not survived the experience. She had run like the hounds of the underworld were at her heels, and more importantly, like there was something — someone — at the other end that she could not afford to be late for.

She was Rhea Valtor.

She did not run *for* anyone.

People ran *from* her. That was the natural order. That was physics.

And when she'd arrived, shoving through the cordon of panicked staff, the first thing she'd seen was him — pale, blue-lipped, unconscious, carried between two healers — and Alice Rimehart walking beside the stretcher.

Holding his hand.

Refusing, in front of approximately forty witnesses, to let go.

Rhea examined what she felt about that with the same clinical rigor she applied to everything.

It was not jealousy. Jealousy was an emotional inefficiency, a resource drain, a tactical error. What she felt was clearly an analytical assessment of a competing entity who had established positional advantage in—

'I want to freeze Alice Rimehart to a wall and take her spot.'

Rhea closed her eyes.

'...That was jealousy.'

She stood there in the empty training grounds, in the grey dawn light, a prodigy of the Seventh Legion's last bloodline, and had a realization so structurally devastating that it took a full forty seconds to load.

'Somewhere along the line, the schedule changed, and nobody informed me.'

She resumed the sequence.

Step. Pivot. Yield. Flow.

Her hand no longer shook.

But she left the final posture early, and the Ashenveil Retreat — which she had never once, in seven years, failed to complete — stayed unfinished at her back like an unanswered question.

**[Scene 3 — The Imperial Study, 8:15 AM]**

The notebook was open.

Iris Aurelia was not reading it.

The notebook contained, at last count, forty-one pages of observations on the anomalous entity designated *Subject: Lucen Gray.* Timestamps. Behavioral patterns. Technique analyses. A provisional threat assessment that she had revised upward four times and then stopped revising, because the number had started to feel silly.

She was staring at the wall instead.

Her study was perfect. It was always perfect. Books shelved by subject and then by height, because disorder offended the Empire. A fire laid but unlit, because the morning was mild and Iris Aurelia did not indulge in unnecessary comforts.

A teacup, steaming, at her right hand.

She picked it up.

She set it down.

She picked it up again.

'Sera,' Iris thought, watching the steam curl, 'has been standing there for six minutes pretending to sort correspondence because she can tell something is wrong.'

Iris replayed last night for the thirtieth time. Frame by frame. Intellectually, she told herself. Analysts review footage. That was all this was. Review.

*The duel.* Three seconds. The Void Counter — her technique, taught in her study, performed on the grandest stage the first-year curriculum offered, executed flawlessly by a boy with a mana index that shouldn't technically allow him to cast a light cantrip. She had stood in the faculty box and felt something unfamiliar move through her chest, and she had labeled it *pride in her own instructional methodology* because the alternative label was unacceptable.

*Then the bells.*

She had watched him — and this was the part she could not stop replaying — she had watched him receive the news. From eighty meters away, she'd seen his face change. Not confusion. Not alarm.

*Recognition.*

He had known. Instantly. What was happening, where it was happening, and what it meant. She had watched panic and knowledge war across his face for roughly half a second, and knowledge won, and then Lucen Gray — who had just spent everything he had on a duel, who had nothing left, who moved like an apology on his best days — had *run.*

From the arena of his greatest triumph.

Straight to the girls' dormitory.

Straight to Alice Rimehart's room.

He'd arrived before the faculty response team. Before the wardens. Before *Iris,* and Iris had been airborne within forty seconds. The tactical implications were staggering: intelligence-gathering capability exceeding Imperial surveillance, pre-positioned response protocols, predictive threat modeling of a caliber that suggested either a classified gift or resources she could not account for.

Any of that should have been what kept her up all night.

It was not what kept her up all night.

'He ran to *her.*'

The thought arrived uninvited, and Iris, who could silence court ministers with a raised eyebrow, could not silence it.

'He knew someone was in danger. Of course he ran. He runs into things. That's documented. It's his single most reliable behavioral trait — he walks directly into situations that a person with his capabilities has no business surviving, and then survives them out of apparent spite.'

'But it was *her* he ran to.'

She picked up the teacup.

She set it down.

Alice Rimehart. Seventeen. First-year. Scholarship student, no house affiliation, impeccable if unremarkable academic record. Notable only for a mana signature that read *wrong* on any instrument sensitive enough to notice, and for the fact that every form of scrying Iris had quietly turned on her over the past semester returned nothing.

*Nothing.* Not surface thoughts, not residual emotion, not even the faint psychic outline that every living thing casts.

A girl-shaped silence.

And now, apparently, the gravitational center of Lucen Gray's universe.

Iris was the Third Imperial Princess of the Aurelian Empire. She had been trained since birth in the acquisition and application of power. She understood, at a cellular level, that information was the first weapon drawn in any conflict, and she understood exactly what impulse was currently marching through her in polished boots.

She understood it. She permitted it anyway.

"Sera."

"Yes, Your Highness?" The maid straightened as though she'd been waiting for the words.

"Find out everything there is to know about Celestia Rimehart."

Iris's voice was perfect. Level. Cool. The voice of a princess requesting minor intelligence, nothing more.

"Everything. Where she was born. Who raised her. Her family line. Her coursework. What she eats for breakfast. Who writes to her and who she writes back to. Every sealed record, and the names of everyone who sealed them."

"And the scope of discretion, Your Highness?"

"Maximum. If anyone asks, I am vetting a talent for possible patronage."

She picked up her teacup one final time and drank. The tea had gone cold twenty minutes ago. She did not react to this, because she was Iris Aurelia, and she did not react to things.

Sera bowed and withdrew. The door clicked shut.

Iris set the cup down on its saucer with a tiny, precise sound.

Her knuckles, where they'd been wrapped around the porcelain, were white.

'It was *her* he ran to,' she thought, one final time, and then she opened the notebook to a fresh page, wrote **C. RIMEHART** at the top, and underlined it twice.

Outside, the morning brightened over Stellaris Royal Academy, and somewhere in the deep architecture of fate, a counter that had been holding very still for a very long time ticked upward by one.

```
[Bad End No. 47: Sunrise Eternal — Probability rising.]
[The sun's jealousy is patient. It has all the time in the sky.]
```

**[Scene 4 — The Infirmary, 10:03 AM]**

I woke up to the smell of herbs and the distinct sensation that my hands had been replaced with oven mitts.

Both of them. Bandaged. Again. The infirmary staff of Stellaris Royal Academy and I were, at this point, basically in a long-term relationship. They had a shelf with my name on it. I'm not even joking. A *shelf.*

'Let's run the checklist. Fingers: present, wrapped, status unknown. Face: feels like I lost a fight with a blizzard, because I did. Dignity: filed for bankruptcy three weeks ago, no change.'

'So. Day 29.'

I stared at the ceiling. It stared back. We've been doing this a lot lately, the ceiling and I.

'Twenty-nine days ago I was Kang Min-jun, twenty-three, unemployed, 347 hours deep in a completionist run of a dating sim, and my biggest problem was that the convenience store near my apartment stopped stocking my brand of ramyeon.'

'Now I'm the talk of the academy.'

That was the truly diabolical part. I could feel it. Even from in here. The *ambient attention.* Like a debuff that doesn't show up on your status screen but you know it's ticking.

I won the duel.

I saved Alice.

I defused the calamity.

Three simultaneous crises, all resolved. Flawless execution. Any speedrunner would weep at the run. Frame-perfect inputs, zero deaths, no continues.

And the reward for my perfect run?

'Visibility.'

The one currency I was desperately trying to keep in the negative. I had a build, okay? The build was called *background character.* The build had one skill and the skill was *nobody looks at me.* Twenty-nine days in, the build was in the trash, on fire, and the fire was trending.

Lucas Ashford wanted to be my friend. The *protagonist.* The chosen sun-boy of the entire narrative. You don't befriend the protagonist. Befriending the protagonist was step one of every "tragically dies to motivate the hero" sub-route. I had catalogued those. Eleven of them.

Rhea Valtor had started adjusting her schedule around me like I was a gravitational anomaly, and yesterday she'd looked at me for four full seconds after training and I still didn't know what it meant, and women like Rhea not saying what they mean was a horror genre all by itself.

Iris Aurelia thought I was some kind of deep-cover intelligence operative — which, to be fair, was the only rational explanation available to her, because "terrified Korean guy with impeccable foreknowledge of a video game" wasn't in her threat taxonomy — and the sole upside of that was that she hadn't figured out the deeply embarrassing truth.

And Alice.

'...Alice.'

My bandaged hand twitched at the memory. The cold of her room. The weight of her against my chest. The purr — that small, ancient, impossible sound — where our fingers had laced together.

'Sleep well, okay. I'll keep the nightmares off the schedule.'

'God. Who am I. I sound like a protagonist. This is exactly the kind of line that gets flags planted.'

The Evil Goddess, naturally, chose this exact moment of vulnerability to make her entrance.

```
[System: Status Update — Day 29.]
```

```
[Mana Index: 0.007 (Critical. Further void-absorption may reduce your mana to zero. The consequences of a zero mana index are unknown. The Evil Goddess does not want to find out.)]
```

*Down from 0.009.* I did the math, because math was what I did when I was scared. Every time the thing inside Alice — the entity, the sealed apocalypse, the cosmic disaster with a cat's personality — sipped off my ambient mana to stabilize her, my index dropped. At this burn rate I'd hit zero in roughly a month.

'What happens at zero? Nobody knows! Not even the goddess! Love that. Love a mystery box where the possible contents range from "nothing" to "you unravel like a cheap sweater."'

I kept reading.

```
[Heroine Status:]
[Iris Aurelia — Affection 6, Route LOCKED, emotional state: JEALOUSY DETECTED.]
```

I sat up too fast and my vision went spotty.

"*Jealousy detected?!* JEALOUSY DETECTED?! Of *what?* Nothing happened! Something happened but it wasn't the *something* that warrants the jealousy flag! I was holding down a natural disaster! With my bare hands! Like putting a lid on a volcano! Do you put the jealousy flag on a man for extinguishing a FIRE?!"

The infirmary was empty. I was yelling at the air. Great. Add it to the file.

```
[Rhea Valtor — Affection 4, Route ACTIVE, emotional state: CONFUSION AND WORRY.]
```

'Confusion and worry. Fantastic. The human Valkyrie who has never experienced uncertainty in her life is currently experiencing it, because of me. That's fine. That's a stable fire I'm sure.'

```
[Alice/Celestia Rimehart — Affection 8, Route LOCKED, emotional state: BONDED.]
```

'Bonded.' The word sat there on the translucent panel, glowing faintly. 'I have been *bonded* to a sealed world-ending entity. Like a parking validation. Like a printer that only works for one computer. I am the designated human of Armageddon. There's no HR department for this.'

```
[Lyra Silverclaw — Affection 0, Route APPROACHING, current action: OBSERVING FROM DISTANCE.]
```

I recoiled physically.

"*Observing from distance?!* Wait, wait — she hasn't even *met* me! That's a route from the third act! You can't observe someone from a distance at affection *zero* unless you're a sniper! Is she a sniper?! Why is the untamed beast-folk heroine doing reconnaissance?! I'M NOT INTERESTING! STATISTICALLY I AM BELOW A TRAINING DUMMY!"

The panel, cruelly, continued.

```
[Lucas Ashford — Friendship Level: HIGH. Protagonist bond: FORMING.]
```

'No. No no no. Reject. Return to sender. I decline the protagonist bond. There's a form for this. There has to be a form for this.'

```
[Elara Voss — Threat Level: CRITICAL. Investigation: ONGOING.]
```

'And the one woman on this list who actually wants me dead is *actively investigating.* Outstanding. A perfect spread. One of everything. It's like a loot box where every drop is a different way to die.'

Then the final line rendered, and it was the one that broke me.

```
[Your invisibility rating: NEGATIVE. You are the opposite of invisible. You are a beacon.]
```

'...A beacon.'

I lay back down. Slowly. Like a man lowering himself into his own grave, which, at current trajectory, was no longer entirely a metaphor.

'A beacon. I specced into stealth. I *glutted* my stat points into stealth. I am a level 99 stealth build and the game just told me I'm a lighthouse.'

And then — because the universe had a sense of humor and the sense of humor was aimed at me — the last window opened.

The slow one. The elegant one. *Her* one.

```
[Note from Management:]

[Good morning, little void.]
```

```
[Did you sleep? Of course you didn't. Neither did I. I spent the entire night watching four different teenagers process their feelings about you, and I want you to know that from my vantage point it was better than theater.]
```

```
[The sun-boy has decided to adopt you. The soldier has discovered she has a heart and is furious with it for reporting for duty. The princess is compiling a dossier on your glacier girl with handwriting so sharp it's cutting the page.]
```

```
[And the beast hasn't even introduced herself yet. She's just... watching. Taking notes. She finds you *interesting*, Min-jun. Do you know what beast-kin do to things they find interesting?]
```

```
[No, you don't. That's the fun part.]
```

I made a sound. It wasn't a word in any language. Somewhere past Korean, past English, into the primal tongue of a gamer watching his damage-avoidance run convert, mid-execution, into a harem speedrun he never signed up for.

'This is fine.'

'This is fine.'

'Reset is not an option. There is no menu. There is no quit button. The save file is corrupted and the corrupted file is my *life.*'

The last text crawled across the panel, and I could hear her voice in it. Amused. Warm. Endlessly entertained, the way only an immortal could be entertained by the suffering of one very tired twenty-three-year-old.

```
[I have revised your epitaph again, by the way.]
```

```
[Current version:]
```

```
['Here lies Lucen Gray.']
['He didn't mean to.']
['He never meant to.']
['But everything he touched caught fire.']
```

The window held there for a moment, glowing gently in the antiseptic infirmary air, like it was letting me appreciate the craftsmanship.

'...It's not even wrong,' I muttered, staring at it. 'That's the worst part. Four years of sneaking practice, forty-seven hours on rooftops, a full stealth build — and the omniscient narrator of my life just published my biography in three sentences and it's *accurate.*'

The panel waited. Patient. Smug, somehow, in font form.

'Fine. You want a review? Three stars. Compelling narrative, the protagonist is unrelatable because he's *me,* and the romance options are objectively terrifying.'

```
[Cute.]
```

```
[Now rest, little void. Your glacier will be visiting soon, and I believe she is bringing soup she made herself.]
```

```
[She has never cooked before.]
```

```
[The entity helped.]
```

```
[Good luck.]
```

The window dissolved into sparks and was gone.

I stared at the ceiling.

The ceiling, wisely, said nothing.

'...Soup,' I whispered, to no one, to everyone, to the Goddess in her theater seat. 'Made by an seventeen-year-old sealed apocalypse and supervised by an eldritch entity older than the continent.'

I pulled the blanket over my head.

'Wake me when my mana hits zero. Actually — if it hits zero, don't wake me. Just update the epitaph. "He went out the way he lived: declining."'

Somewhere far off, down the corridor, the infirmary door opened, and a small polite voice — quiet, careful, and carrying a faint, resonant, cat-like hum of contentment underneath every syllable — asked the nurse which bed the boy with the bandaged hands was in.

Three hundred and forty-seven hours.

Every route. Every flag. Every bad end.

'Not a single one,' I thought, burrowing deeper under the blanket, 'prepared me for the soup.'
