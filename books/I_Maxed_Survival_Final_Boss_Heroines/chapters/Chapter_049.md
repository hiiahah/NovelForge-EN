# Chapter 49: Voss's Gift

The banquet was dying the way all good parties die — not with a bang, but with the slow, sad clink of someone's aunt collecting abandoned glasses.

I sat in a corner alcove of the Seminary's lower hall, spine parked carefully against the wall in a position that only hurt my bruises a moderate amount, watching the Shadow Banquet exhale its last breaths.

A fewstragglers remained. A pair of fourth-years laughing too quietly in the archway. A faculty member pretending to read a notice board. Servants gliding through like polite ghosts, erasing the evidence that hundreds of teenagers had done formal combat in this building tonight.

The twin moons' light came through the high windows in two different colors, which still messed me up. Two moons. Thirty-four days in this world, and I still filed that under "things that would trend on Twitter if anyone back home saw it."

'Alright. Debrief.'

'One. My void is gravity now, apparently. Everyone strong is falling sideways into my orbit whether I want it or not.'

'Two. I am never invisible again. That door is closed. It closed tonight sometime between Alice looking at me like I mattered and Lyra looking at me like I was prey she'd decided to keep.'

'Three. Eight bruises.'

'Four. Tomorrow is Monday. Classes resume. Lucas Ashford, Seat 4-C, will be radiating protagonist energy approximately thirty meters from Seat 7-F.'

'Five. I should go to bed.'

I did not go to bed.

Because at that exact moment, the hair on the back of my neck — the hair that had survived 347 hours of Eschaton Hearts lore and every Bad End flag I've ever catalogued — stood up like a field responding to lightning.

Footsteps.

Soft. Unhurried. The walk of someone who has never once in her life needed to hurry.

Vice-Headmistress Elara Voss stepped out of the shadows at the edge of the hall like she'd been cut from them.

'Oh no.'

'Oh no, oh no, why is she—'

"Lucen."

Her voice was warm. That was the terrifying part. It was always warm.

"There you are. I've been looking for you."

---

I stood up.

Mistake. Immediate regret. My ribs registered a formal complaint with the bruise committee.

Vice-Headmistress Elara Voss stopped three steps away from me, close enough for conversation, far enough to be polite. She was, as always, perfect. Midnight robes, hair pinned with silver, the kind of face that seemed designed by an artist who'd been asked to draw "a mother you trust at your funeral."

'Stellaris Royal Academy's Vice-Headmistress. Second most powerful person on campus. Smiles like tea and charity galas.'

'In the game, the player doesn't learn what she really is until Year 3.'

'I am currently a nobody who, by all appearances, is a sweet, unremarkable back-row student with the magical capacity of wallpaper.'

'So WHY is she walking toward me at midnight with—'

She was carrying a small velvet box.

'Ohhh no.'

'Nope. Nope. That's the worst possible thing an NPC can be holding. Velvet boxes are either engagements or plot. Neither applies to me, which means it's WORSE than both.'

"You fought beautifully tonight," she said.

"I lost."

"You fought with conviction." Her smile deepened, eyes crinkling at the corners with what my brain kept insisting was genuine warmth. "That's far more rare than victory. Anyone can win. Very few people stand their ground when the outcome is decided."

'She watched the matches. Of course she watched the matches. She watches everything. That's literally her function in the narrative structure.'

"I mostly survived," I said, which was technically my entire skill build in one sentence.

"Yes." She tilted her head. "You did."

The way she said it — soft, almost fond, like surviving was a virtue she'd personally been waiting for someone to demonstrate — sent a cold trickle down my spine that had nothing to do with my bruises.

'Kang Min-jun. Twenty-three years old. Unemployed. 347 hours logged. Count this as the moment the route locks.'

She held out the box.

---

"May I be presumptuous?" she asked.

'Sure. Why not. It's not like I can stop you. You could turn me into vapor with a bored glance, so honestly, asking permission is just courtesy seasoning on the execution.'

"...Sure," I said, with the deadpan confidence of a man signing his own death certificate because refusing the pen would be rude.

She opened the box.

Inside, on a bed of dark blue velvet, lay a pendant.

A thin silver chain. A smooth, dark stone — onyx, maybe, or something that wanted you to believe it was onyx. And inside the stone, a light. A faint pulse, slow as a sleeping heartbeat.

Thump.

Thump.

Thump.

I knew that pulse.

'I KNOW that pulse.'

'I've seen that exact item sprite. I've hovered my mouse over that exact tooltip.'

'Item: Resonance Amplifier.'

'Rarity: Unique.'

'Type: Amplification Artifact — Void-attuned.'

'Source: Church of Ending, Internal Supply.'

'Discovered by Lucas Ashford, Year 3, during the conspiracy flags chain when he raids the Church's private archives.'

'Effect: magnifies mana-void signatures. Period.'

My brain did the math at the speed of panic:

'Vice-Headmistress Voss, theory confirmed, is a member of the Church of Ending.'

'She's holding a tool designed to make void-types stronger.'

'I'm a void-type.'

'She's giving it to me.'

'At midnight.'

'Alone.'

'In a corner.'

'Smiling.'

'This is fine. Everything about this is fine. I'm going to have an aneurysm and it'll be FINE.'

---

"This is a focusing artifact," Voss said, her voice gentle, pedagogical, a teacher offering a struggling student a better pair of spectacles. "Very old. Very rare."

She turned the box slightly, letting the moons' light catch the glassy stone.

"It's designed to help people with unusual mana constitutions — like yours, Lucen — channel their abilities more effectively."

I looked at it.

She looked at me, waiting.

'Right. Okay. Let me list my options.'

'Option A: Take the pendant. Result: my worthless 0.009-out-of-100,000 void — which is, let's remember, officially LESS MAGICAL THAN A WOODEN TRAINING DUMMY — suddenly gets a megaphone. My dampening range expands. My void-absorption capacity grows. I become measurably better at suppressing Abyssal incursions. I become a more useful tool for her.'

'Also it makes me louder to everything that can hear a void. Everything. Up to and including whatever's sleeping under this academy.'

'Option B: refuse. Result: she knows I know what it is. There's exactly no reason an obscure back-row nobody should recognize a Church artifact. Refusal = exposure. Exposure = Game Over, but with worse lighting.'

'Option C: run.'

'...'

'Hahahaha.'

'I have a stamina of 14.'

'She's the Vice-Headmistress and probably has, like, a teleport linked to her ankle. This is not a speedrun I can win.'

"Why me?" I asked. Quietly. Just barely voice-cracked at the end.

Her expression softened further. If softness could be weaponized, she'd have atomized me with it.

"Because you're alone in a crowd," she said. "And because people like us — people with... hollow places — understand how loud that can get. This will quiet it."

'That's the trap.'

'It's not that she's lying.'

'She's telling the truth. That's the worst kind of trap — the kind with sincerity at the bottom. The pendant WILL help. It will make me stronger. It will sharpen my void, deepen my suppression, silence the static.'

'And over time — months, years, drip by drip — it will widen me. Expand the void until I'm not a boy with a hole in him anymore, but a weapon with a pulse. A key that can not only seal the Abyssal gates but RIP them open. Exactly the tool the Church of Ending has been waiting 200 years to find.'

'The gift helps me.'

'And hollows me.'

'Both. At once. Forever.'

'Rude.'

---

I took the pendant.

My fingers touched the silver chain. It was cool. Normal. Like jewelry from a mall.

The moment my skin made contact, the stone's glow beneath its surface brightened — a single slow pulse, like the artifact had noticed me.

'Yeah. Hi. I'm your new host. Be nice.'

I lifted it over my head and let it settle against my chest, beneath the collar of my academy shirt, against my sternum where my heartbeat lived.

It hummed.

Softly. Subtly. A low, resonant vibration that wasn't quite sound. It wasn't unpleasant. That was the horrifying part. It felt — right. It felt like my body had a missing bone and someone had just slid it back into place.

'Oh no.'

'Oh no, it feels GOOD.'

'This is exactly what an addiction feels like in the first thirty seconds. Cool, so I'm on magical artifact opiates now. Fantastic.'

"Thank you, Vice-Headmistress," I said.

She smiled.

Her eyes were kind.

Completely, genuinely kind.

That was the worst thing I'd seen all night, and I'd just spent an evening in a literal shadow banquet being emotionally dissected by five heroines and a goddess.

"Elara," she corrected gently.

"Thank you, Vice-Headmistress," I said again, because I am a coward and consistency is my brand.

A small laugh. Barely a breath. "Stubborn. I suppose that's a survival trait too."

'You have no idea, lady.'

'You actually have less idea than anyone else in this building because you think you're enrolling a useful void and I'm actually Kang Min-jun, professional game completionist, and I have already read your whole tragic backstory including the part where you burn. Twice.'

She stepped back, folding the now-empty velvet box into her sleeve with a grace that made the motion look like a ballet.

"Take care of yourself, Lucen," she said. "You're more important than you know."

'I know EXACTLY how important I am, thanks. I am approximately 0.009 important and tonight you gift-wrapped me into a staging area for a doomsday cult.'

"I'll try," I said.

She turned and walked toward the doors, unhurried, every step measured like a metronome set to "patient."

---

Her shadow stretched long behind her in the moonlight.

And for one instant — one flicker, one frame, one blink — I saw something else in her wake.

A second shadow.

Behind hers. Darker. Older.

A silhouette that didn't move like she moved. Something vast. Something patient. Something that had been standing behind her the entire time and had simply, graciously, let me see it.

At the threshold, the second shadow tilted its head.

'IT KNOWS I'M HERE.'

'IT'S WATCHING ME WATCH IT.'

'CHURCH OF ENDING. THAT'S THE CHURCH OF ENDING. THAT'S — that's the thing that wears her, or rides her, or nests inside the shape of her. That's the cold thing under the mother's smile.'

Then it was gone. Both shadows passed into the corridor beyond, and the hall was just a hall again, and I was a boy in a corner with a dead cooling patch and a pounding heart and a magical bomb resting against my chest.

I stood there for a long time.

Then I sat down.

Then I stood up again, because sitting had hurt, then remembered standing also hurt, and ultimately leaned against the wall in the exact posture of a man blue-screened by existence.

[System ping.]

I closed my eyes.

'No.'

[System ping.]

'No.'

[Persistent ping.]

'FINE.'

I stood in the empty hall, back to the wall, eight bruises catalogued, twin moons at the window, pendant warm on my sternum.

I thought about the matches tonight.

I thought about Alice's hands shaking. About the look on her face when the incursion's residue pressed up against reality and she was the only person that could hold it. About Lucas in Seat 4-C starting Monday, hero-swordsman chosen one, still not strong enough yet. About the Abyssal seals under the academy, humming their patient hum, waiting for something to loosen them.

I thought about the fact that tonight I had stood in front of an Abyssal flare and stayed standing, and that it had worked, and that it had worked because my void was louder than the pressure.

'She's giving me the upgrade I need to protect people.'

I knew exactly what it cost.

I knew exactly what she was.

'And if I take it off, tomorrow, when the next incursion twitches, Alice is alone at the front of the line again.'

I tightened my fingers around the stone.

'...No.'

'I'm keeping it on.'

'I'll keep it on even if it's poison, because tonight Alice nearly broke trying to hold the world shut, and the only thing worse than being a weapon is being useless. I'm not letting them stand at the front alone while I hide at Seat 7-F pretending to be scenery.'

'She thinks she's writing my collar.'

'Fine.'

'Let her.'

'I'm the void. Voids don't get aimed.'

'I'm the one choosing the direction.'

[DAY 34 — SUNDAY — 12:47 AM — SEMINARY, EMPTY]

The pendant pulsed once against my chest.

Soft. Patient. Exactly like a heartbeat that wasn't mine.

I pushed off the wall. Winced. Winced again.

'Eight bruises. Still eight.'

'New accessory: one (1) harbinger.'

I limped toward the stairs, one careful step at a time — a void wearing a louder void, a boy dressed in his own trap, a maxed-out survival build piloting a body held together with bruise ointment and stubbornness.

Above me, through the window, the twin moons hung in their patient sky like two eyes that had been watching all along.

'Fine,' I told the night quietly. 'Volume 2.'

'Come on, then.'

'I've got five more minutes of consciousness and eight more minutes of panic.'

'And somewhere in a hallway, a shepherd is walking home thinking she's finally caught her lamb.'

I smiled for the first time all evening. It wasn't a good smile. It was the smile of a speedrunner staring at a route he already knew by heart.

'She caught me.'

'Now she has to figure out what to do with something that doesn't stop.'
