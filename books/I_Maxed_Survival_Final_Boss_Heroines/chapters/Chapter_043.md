# Chapter 43: Alice Bakes for War

---

Alice Frostvein had killed nothing in her life.

This was, she reflected at five in the morning, absolutely going to change by breakfast.

"Operator."

The kneading paddle surrendered with a sad metallic squeak. She applied more pressure. The bottom of her mixing bowl hit the counter with a sound like a small church bell being murdered.

"Operate *properly*."

The Academy kitchen was enormous. Vaulted ceilings, copper pots hanging like an armory of small shields, four ovens the size of carriages, and — at this hour — one girl in an apron over her uniform with flour in her hair up to the eyebrows.

---

Operation: Make Lucen the Best Banquet Dessert Ever was underway.

Phase one had begun at 3:45 AM, when Alice had woken in the dark of her dormitory with her heart doing something complicated, and had decided, with the perfect logic of the recently enlightened, that this feeling required *baked goods*.

Phase two had involved locating the kitchen.

Phase three had involved the kitchen staff's night porter finding her at the door, and Alice looking at him with her big ice-blue eyes and saying, in the small soft voice she never used at anyone but meant absolutely nothing by:

"I need to bake. It's important."

He had handed her the keys, the apron, and — she suspected — a small piece of his soul, and gone back to bed to reconsider his life.

---

Now, two hours later, the kitchen looked like a bakery had fought a war and lost.

Bowls. Everywhere. Cooling racks. Three trays of something golden. A stand of piping bags lined up like soldiers awaiting orders.

Alice stood at the center of it, whisk in hand, hair tied back, a smear of frosting across her cheek like war paint, and surveyed her domain.

*It's not enough.*

"…I know it's not enough," she murmured.

The voice hadn't spoken. It didn't need to. The thing that lived behind her ribs — quiet these days, quiet in a way that should have been reassuring and wasn't — simply *pressed*, gently, like a cat leaning into a knee.

*More.*

"More what?"

A pause. The entity considered. Then, with the slow patience of something that had watched empires kneel and found them all slightly under-seasoned, it offered a thought.

Not in words. The entity didn't use words. It used *textures*.

It offered her the memory of the way Lucen Gray's void felt, from the inside of her chest, when she'd stood close to him in the corridor. Not cold. Not empty.

*Yearning.*

The man's void tasted like yearning. Like a hand reaching for a doorknob in the dark. Like the second before a held breath lets go.

Alice stopped whisking.

"You want me to… bake something that *answers* that?"

The entity settled back, smug, like a consultant who has delivered its invoice.

"That's…" Alice stared at the batter. "That's actually helpful. Thank you?"

The entity radiated the satisfaction of a creature that has never once been thanked in eleven thousand years and had not realized it wanted to be.

*We are a team,* it did not say.

"We are *not* a team," Alice told it firmly, and reached for the cardamom.

---

He liked simple flavors.

She knew this because she had paid attention, the way she paid attention to everything about him, because attention was free and she had so much of it and nowhere else to put it.

Nothing too sweet. At the festival, seventeen days ago, he'd stood at the pastry stall for a full forty seconds — she'd timed it, absently, the way one does — and chosen the cinnamon roll over the glazed cake, the honeyed fruit, the crystalline sugar sculptures.

Warm spices. Honest ones.

She piped the frosting and watched her own hands work.

The patterns came out of her without asking permission. Fine, curling lines through the buttercream — spirals that folded into spirals, fractals that made the eye feel like it was falling gently down a well lined with something soft.

Abyssal geometry.

She noticed, after a moment, that the cookies were too beautiful.

Not in the way food is beautiful. In the way a deep pool is beautiful. In the way the light under a closed door at midnight is beautiful.

"...Huh."

She looked at her hands. Looked at the tray.

The entity, inside, pretended very hard to be asleep.

"Are you doing this?"

*I am simply existing,* it did not say. *Existence leaks.*

Alice looked at the cookies for a long moment. Then she picked one up and bit into it,testing, suspicious, ready to be betrayed.

Warmth bloomed in her chest. Not magic. Not compulsion. Just — the feeling of a kitchen at dawn. The feeling of someone having been awake for hours, thinking of you. The feeling of coming through a door and the lights already being on.

She chewed slowly.

"...It's just love," she said to the empty kitchen, a little defensive. "That's allowed."

The entity said nothing, which was its way of laughing.

---

By six-thirty, the head cook arrived for her shift and stopped in the doorway with the expression of a woman who has walked into her own kitchen and found a cathedral.

"Student Frostvein."

"Good morning! I'm almost done, I promise, I'll clean everything—"

"Student Frostvein." The cook came forward slowly and lifted a cookie from a cooling rack with the reverence of a person handling a holy relic. "I have worked in this kitchen for nineteen years."

"Yes?"

"These are the finest things that have ever been made in it. Including my wedding cake. Including, and I say this with my whole heart, my *children.*"

Alice beamed.

"…I'm going to need to sit down," said the cook.

---

There were, at final count, one hundred and forty desserts.

Petit fours. Spiced shortbread. Honey-cardamom twists. Little glazed domes that caught the morning light like frozen dew. Cookies in the shape of stars, and moons, and — she told everyone firmly that this one was a *generic round cookie* — one that was unmistakably the silhouette of a boy in profile, if you looked at it right, which nobody was allowed to do.

"A small something," Alice explained to the wide-eyed kitchen staff, gesturing at thirty people's worth of pastry. "For the banquet."

"Dear," said the pastry sous-chef faintly, "the banquet is catered."

"Then they'll have options."

---

At 7:04 AM, the kitchen door opened and the subject of the operation walked in.

Alice's heart performed a small, full tactical retreat.

Lucen Gray looked like he'd slept the way other people fought wars — badly, with casualties. There was a water bottle in his hand and shadows under his eyes and he stopped three steps inside and stared at the landscape of sugar.

"Alice."

"Good morning!"

"It is seven in the morning."

"Yes!"

"How long have you been here?"

"Two hours!" she said brightly. "Or four. Time is a construct when you're baking."

Lucen looked at the trays. At the racks. At the small mountain of cookies glittering under frost-dusted parchment like the hoard of a very domesticated dragon.

"These are all for the banquet?"

"These are all for *you*."

She said it before her brain could convene an emergency session about it. The words just walked out of her mouth like they owned the place.

Lucen went still.

She watched his face do the thing it did — that careful, wary calculation, like a man standing at the edge of a frozen lake listening to the ice. He was always doing that. Always checking the ice.

'Stop checking,' she thought, suddenly fierce, in the privacy of her own head. 'It's solid. *I'm* solid. I baked for four hours. That's the most solid thing in this kingdom.'

She picked up a cookie — a good one, one of the simple ones, cinnamon and brown butter — and held it out.

"Try?"

He looked at it.

She watched him look at it.

*Somewhere in the deep architecture of the universe, a certain gray-eyed survivor's instincts were, presumably, screaming something about accepting food from the girl with an apocalypse entity in her chest. She did not know this. She only knew that he was hesitating, and that it hurt a little, and that she refused to let it show.*

His eyes came up to hers.

She was aware, distantly, that there was flour in her hair. Frosting on her cheek. Frost-sugar dusted across her nose like freckles, because she'd been working near the cold window all morning and small ice kept blooming where she breathed.

She looked at him and did not drop her gaze, and smiled, and waited.

Lucen Gray took the cookie.

---

He bit into it.

Then he stopped moving entirely.

For three full seconds, the most paranoid creature in Stellaris Royal Academy stood in a warm kitchen at dawn with his eyes slightly too wide, chewing nothing, holding perfectly still, like a man who has just been told something enormous in a language he almost speaks.

Alice watched him with her hands folded and her heart in her throat.

"...Alice."

"Good?"

"That is the best thing I have ever eaten," he said, in the tone of a man making a sworn deposition. "In either— in my *life*."

Something in Alice's chest unfolded like a letter being opened.

"Dangerously good," Lucen added, eyeing the cookie with genuine suspicion now. "This should be regulated. There should be a license. A waiting period."

She laughed. She couldn't help it. It came out of her bright and round and real.

On the counter beside her, unnoticed by either of them, the fine lace of frost on the inside of the window had quietly reorganized itself.

Tiny hearts. Dozens of them, blooming in the ice like the morning itself was pleased.

---

He took three more cookies "for the road," which she pretended not to be delighted by. He told her, solemnly, that the dormitory water situation remained a humanitarian crisis and that this kitchen was the only reason he was alive. He told her to sleep.

"I'll sleep after the banquet," she promised.

"That's a lie."

"It's a *plan*."

He almost smiled. She watched the almost-smile the way a miser counts coins, and stored it.

At the door, he paused. Looked back at her — flour-covered, frost-dusted, standing in the middle of enough pastry to feed a garrison, beaming like she'd personally invented mornings.

"...Thanks, Alice," he said. Just that. Quietly.

Then the door closed, and he was gone, and the kitchen was full of golden light and cooling racks and the smell of cinnamon, and Alice stood in the middle of it and pressed both hands over her mouth like she was holding something in.

Alice could not see any of this. She was busy.

---

She found the stool by the window and sat down, suddenly, all at once, the way four hours of dawn exhaustion finally cashes its check.

In her apron pocket, wrapped in waxed paper, was one last cookie. The one she hadn't offered. The one she'd made at 5:47 AM, when the kitchen was dark and quiet and hers, with the cardamom and the sea salt and the thing she couldn't name.

*Home,* she'd been thinking, when she made it. *Feels like coming home.*

She pulled it out. Held it against her chest with both hands, like a child with a treasure, like a knight with a talisman.

"He liked it," she whispered.

For once, the entity did not answer with hunger. Did not push, did not whisper, did not point at the void inside her and call it a door.

It leaned, gently, in the space behind her ribs. Warm. Present. Content.

*And if something ancient was learning, slowly, that there were things besides hunger — Alice would not have called it that. She would have called it being less alone.*

She smiled.

A real one. Not the mask. Not the soft, careful, everyone-please-don't-look-too-closely smile she'd worn since she arrived at Stellaris. Just a girl, in a dawn kitchen, in flour, smiling at nothing.

"Four more days," she told the empty room cheerfully, tilting her head back against the cold glass. "I've got dessert handled. Everything else will be fine."

The ice hearts on the window glittered.

Outside, beyond the walls of Stellaris Royal Academy, somewhere past the frozen greenhouse and the quiet training grounds, the storm kept its appointment with the calendar.

It had four days.

So did she.
