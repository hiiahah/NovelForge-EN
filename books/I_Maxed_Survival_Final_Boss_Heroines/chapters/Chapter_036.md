# Chapter 36: The Duel: Lucen Gray vs. Derrick Holt

Four hours and forty-seven minutes sounds like a lot of time.

It is not a lot of time.

It's barely enough time to walk a lovesick dragon-in-human-skin back to her dormitory, calm her down from a frost-blooming emotional spike, convince her that everything is fine, nothing is wrong, please go to your room and take a nap like a good eldritch horror — and then sprint across campus in formal festival clothes to an arena where a man with 163 units of mana wants to rearrange my skeleton.

"Are you sure you have to go?" Alice asked at her dormitory door, fingers twisting together.

'No, Alice. I'd love to stay. Unfortunately, a roided-up second-year challenged me to a legally binding duel and the academy is weirdly supportive of student-on-student violence.'

"I'll be back before you know it," I said instead. "Just rest. Read something. Do something boring. Boring is good. Boring is my favorite."

"That's a weird favorite."

"I'm a weird guy."

She smiled. Small. But real. And the faint film of frost that had started creeping up her doorframe — the frost I was absolutely, deliberately not looking at — began to recede.

[Entity stabilization: partial.]

[Containment: Tier 4. Holding.]

[You have bought time. Spend it well.]

Spend it well, it says. Like I'm not about to spend it getting enzymatically digested by a mana-boosted jock in front of two thousand witnesses.

---

Getting changed into training clothes takes a speedrunner exactly ninety seconds.

I know this because I timed it, and also because Finn timed it, and then announced the result to the entire dorm common room like he was commentating the Olympics.

"NINETY-TWO SECONDS! A NEW PERSONAL RECORD!" Finn bellowed, following me down the hallway as I jogged toward the arena. "Lucen, buddy, the energy in there is INSANE right now — the betting pool is at four hundred gold —"

"Who's favored?"

"Oh, Derrick. Obviously. Like, nine to one."

'Obviously.'

"But! But! Three crazy bastards put money on you." He grabbed my shoulder with both hands, eyes gleaming with the fervor of a man who has watched too many underdog sports anime. "The whole arena is expecting you to do the thing. The four-minute thing. The running-around thing. They're calling it the 'Gray Marathon Strategy.' Someone made a sign."

"A sign."

"A SIGN. It says 'RUN FOREST RUN.' I don't know what it means but the energy is incredible."

I stared at him.

'Is that a movie reference? Why is there a movie reference in this world? Is there a speedrunner god of cinema I don't know about?'

"Finn. Listen to me. Whatever happens tonight — win or lose — I need you to do something."

"Anything."

"After the match, I have somewhere urgent to be. If anyone asks where I went — stall. Say I'm injured. Say I'm doing a cool-down routine. Say I got kidnapped by a cult of sentient chairs. Anything."

Finn blinked. "...You're not going to celebrate if you win?"

"If I win, Finn, the celebration is survival."

He nodded slowly, absorbing this in the way only a true best friend NPC can — completely misunderstanding it and being loyal anyway.

"You're so cool and mysterious," he whispered.

'I'm so tired and terrified.'

---

The Grand Arena at night is a different beast than during the day.

Enchanted torches line the walls in rings, their flames a steady blue-white that kills every shadow and makes the sand of the dueling floor glow like snow. Two thousand seats, tiered in a half-moon, and every single one is filled.

Two thousand people.

To watch a first-year with a Mana Index of 0.009 fight a second-year with 163 units of magically enhanced murder fuel.

'This school has a toxic spectator culture and I am its unwilling content creator.'

The noise hit me as I walked out of the tunnel — a rolling wall of sound, cheers and jeers and chants that blurred together into pure crowd static. I couldn't pick out individual voices except for one.

"LUCEEEEEEEN!" Finn, from the front row, standing on his seat, waving the RUN FOREST RUN sign.

'Where did he even position himself— never mind.'

And then I saw the rest.

Front row, faculty section: Instructor Vance, already standing at the referee's mark in the center of the arena, scarred face set in its default expression of *I have seen things and none of them were good.*

Assessment panel behind the barrier — professors with recording crystals, senior instructors, a couple of grey-robed combat mages whose job was to barrier the arena and stop the loser from dying. Comforting.

Left gallery, about halfway up: a lone figure with the kind of straight-backed posture you can't fake. Rhea Valtor. Her arms were crossed. Even from here, I could feel her stare boring a hole through my skull.

'She came.'

'Of course she came. She drilled me until midnight for four nights. At this point I'm basically her thesis project.'

Further right, partially obscured by a pillar, sitting very deliberately in the middle of the common student section like he wasn't the most famous person in the academy — Lucas Ashford. The protagonist. The actual main character of this entire game. Watching my match.

'Great. The hero is in the audience. No pressure. Just perform well in front of the living embodiment of the canon storyline, Lucen.'

And then the VIP gallery.

Gilded railing. Velvet seats. And in the foremost seat, still and luminous as a portrait given breath, Iris Aurelia. Her hands rested in her lap with perfect composure.

Except — and my eyes caught it only because four days of brutal etiquette drills had carved the knowledge into my spine — her left thumb was pressed against her ring finger. Nervous tell. The exact tell she'd slapped my hand for displaying on day three.

'She's nervous.'

'The untouchable princess of the Grand Duchy is nervous about MY duel.'

'If I die tonight, at least that's a memory I take to the grave.'

And finally — the seat I checked last. The one I'd been scanning for since I walked in.

Vice-Headmistress Voss.

Middle tier. Faculty block. Notebook already open on her lap.

'She's note-taking at a student duel.'

'No. She's auditing. She's collecting data. Alice and her frosty aura are one thread, the corrupted crystal is another, my mana re-evaluation is the third, and Voss is sitting in that seat with a needle, trying to stitch them all together.'

Every faction. Every thread. Every ticking clock, gathered in one building.

'If this were a boss arena, I'd be looking for the exit size.'

I stepped onto the sand.

---

Derrick Holt was already waiting at the far mark.

He looked like a boss encounter with a rage mechanic. Shoulders wide enough to block a doorway, practice sword held loosely in one hand, and a faint golden haze clinging to his skin like heat shimmer — mana reinforcement, running hot, saturating muscle fiber with raw output.

One hundred sixty-three units.

For comparison, I had zero-point-zero-zero-nine.

'He could sneeze with that much mana and level a cottage. I've seen toddlers in this world with more juice than me. The training dummies mock me.'

His eyes found mine across the sand, and his lip peeled back.

"Gray." His voice carried. He wanted the audience to hear. "I hope you've been practicing your running. Wouldn't want to disappoint your fans."

"The sign was Finn's idea."

"Four minutes of cowardice. That's all you are. A rat who got lucky dodging." He slammed his practice sword into the sand point-first and spread his arms to the crowd. "TONIGHT! You all get to see what happens when a rat gets cornered!"

The second-years in Holt's faction whooped.

'Cornered rat. Original. Never heard that one before. Truly the Shakespeare of steroid users.'

I didn't answer. I just walked to my mark, picked up the practice sword waiting there, and gripped it.

Fingers along the grip. Not choked. Thumb resting, not clamping. Wrist loose.

*Rhea's correction. Session one. She hit my hand with her scabbard six times before I got it right.*

I let my shoulders settle. Spine stacked. Chin level. Not stiff — *ready.*

*Iris's posture. Session two. She circled me like a tailor, adjusting me with two fingers and disappointment.*

I breathed in. Four counts. Held. Out, four counts. Heart rate down. Panic compressed into a little box and shoved behind my sternum.

*Lucas's rhythm. From every sparring session where he knocked me on my ass and politely explained why.*

Four days.

I'd had four days to turn a 0.009-mana speedrun-gamer soul into someone who could stand in this ring.

[Combat Readiness Assessment]

[Physical conditioning: Improving (D-)]

[Sword form: Disabling Strike — drilled to muscle memory.]

[Evasion/composure: Untested at this output gap.]

[Win probability calculation: Refused. The system has seen your plans before.]

'Rude.'

Instructor Vance stepped between us and raised one scarred arm.

The arena settled into that particular silence crowds make when the violence is about to start — two thousand people inhaling at the same time.

"Standard rules," Vance rumbled. "Disarm, surrender, or incapacitation. Lethal injury is prohibited. The barrier mages will intervene." His eyes slid to me for a half-second — and I swear there was something in them. Not kindness. Assessment. *The man noticed things.* "Combatants ready."

Derrick rolled his shoulders. The golden haze around him brightened, mana crawling up his arms, and the sand beneath his boots began to tremble faintly.

'Yep. That's a full-body enhancement. That's a truck becoming a bigger truck.'

I lifted my sword into a mid-guard.

And inside my head, very quietly, the plan unfolded one last time.

'He's going to come straight at me. Ego characters always open with their biggest move. He wants one blow to end it — a decisive, humiliating statement. An overhead power strike, maximum commitment, full mana output.'

'That is the most predictable opener in the game.'

'And committed attacks have a window.'

'One window. Frame-perfect. If I mistime it by a fraction, I eat a practice sword moving at cart-crash velocity and wake up in the infirmary with a concussion and a Bad End.'

'No pressure or anything.'

[Reminder: The Void Counter is a zero-error-tolerance maneuver.]

[You have drilled the component skills 1,000+ times under fatigue.]

[Question: Are you ready?]

[Answer: It doesn't matter. There is no retry.]

"BEGIN!"

Derrick exploded forward.

---

The crowd screamed.

Ten meters gone in less than a second. Mana-enhanced legs, full sprint, sword drawn up over his shoulder in a movement so familiar I could have drawn it from memory — because I had. Full Power Vertical Smash, Derrick Holt's signature opener in the game, frame data burned into my brain from fight footage I'd studied two hundred hours ago as Kang Min-jun.

And me?

I didn't move.

'MOVE.' My hindbrain. Screaming. 'MOVE, YOU IDIOT, THAT MAN IS A BUS WITH ARMS.'

I didn't move.

The crowd's scream pitched higher — I heard it fracture into confusion, into *what is he doing*, into a woman's startled cry — and distantly, from the front row, Finn's voice cracking: "LUCEEEEN?!"

Seven meters.

The sand erupted under Derrick's final stride.

'Five-point-nine seconds of approach. His sword hand is loaded high. Right shoulder rotated forward. Weight transferring to his left leg — there — commitment point —'

Four meters.

He's rising into the leap now, because of course there's a leap, ego characters always add the leap, the sword coming up and up, blade blazing gold-white with every scrap of his 163 units poured into the edge, and the air around it screams.

My eyes stayed open.

I watched it all.

Every instinct I owned — twenty-three years of survival-biased gaming instinct, boss fights where standing still means death, muscle memory of a hundred dodge buttons — every single nerve ending in my body howled at me to *move.*

I stood still.

Sternum fixed. The hinge of the door. The point around which everything else rotates. *You're not a wall, you're a hinge*, Iris's voice in my head, cold and precise. *Stop resisting. Pivot.*

Two meters.

The blade began to fall.

Time did that thing it does in every clutch moment — that horrible, generous stretching, where one second becomes a mansion with many rooms and you live in all of them at once.

I saw the blade's arc. Downward. Committed.

I saw Derrick's eyes — triumph already blooming, the strike a foregone conclusion.

I saw the dead space.

Rhea's voice, flat as a blizzard: *When the attack is fully committed, the attacker is hollow. That hollow lasts for a fraction of a second. That fraction is yours.*

NOW.

I pivoted.

Not back. Not aside. *Around.*

My body rotated on the fixed point — foot planted, hips swinging, shoulders whipping through — and the world blurred sideways. The blade howled through the space my head had occupied a heartbeat ago, close enough that I felt the mana-hot wind of it shear across my cheek, close enough that a strand of hair drifted free.

Derrick's strike hit the sand.

He had nothing left. All of it — momentum, mana, mass, ego — was committed to a blow that had already failed, and his body was still traveling forward, still falling into the hole his own attack had dug.

The hollow.

The dead space.

Mine.

My practice sword was already coming around with the rotation — I didn't add power, I didn't need power, the rotation itself was the power, everything Iris had beaten into my posture and everything Rhea had drilled into my wrists converged into a single clean line —

—and struck the inside of Derrick's exposed wrist.

*Thock.*

Not a hard hit. A perfectly placed one.

The nerve cluster, the tendon line, the exact spot Rhea had made me strike against a wooden post a thousand times until my arm stopped being something I moved and became something that *answered.*

Derrick's fingers spasmed open.

His sword spun away into the sand.

And the world slammed back up to full speed.

---

Three point two seconds.

From *BEGIN* to the clatter of a fallen practice sword in a silent arena: three point two seconds.

Derrick Holt stood two meters from me, staring at his own empty hand.

His fingers flexed. Opened. Closed. He looked at them like they'd betrayed him personally, like maybe if he checked a fourth time the sword would be back in his grip.

It wasn't.

The arena made a sound I'd never heard a crowd make before — not a cheer, not a gasp, something in between, the noise of two thousand people whose brains hadn't finished loading. The second-years were on their feet with their mouths open. Finn was frozen mid-jump, sign upside down. Someone in the back dropped a whole tray of festival skewers and the clatter echoed like gunfire.

'I did it.'

'I actually did it. Frame-perfect. First try, no retries, on a live build with permadeath enabled.'

'Zero-to-one-hundred clutch. Easiest match of my life. Where's my speedrun medal.'

Derrick's gaze dragged from his hand to my face. The golden haze was guttering out of him, mana spent, and what was underneath was just... disbelief. Total, naked disbelief.

"You—" His voice cracked. "You didn't even— not one step — you didn't *fear* it—"

'Oh, buddy. I feared it so much. You have no idea how much I feared it. My fear has a fear.'

"Good correction on the overhead strike, though," I said. "You put your whole back into it."

Instructor Vance's arm came down.

"Disarm achieved." A pause — and for just an instant, the corner of that scarred mouth twitched. The expression of a man filing something away in a cabinet marked *interesting*. "Match to Lucen Gray."

The arena detonated.

The sound hit like a physical force — screaming, stamping, a chant of GRAY-GRAY-GRAY starting somewhere in the cheap seats and spreading like wildfire, two thousand people losing their collective minds over three seconds of violence. The assessment panel professors were already leaning over each other, shouting, hands waving at recording crystals. Somewhere up in the faculty block, I was distantly aware of a pen scratching furiously.

Voss.

Writing.

'Yeah. Write it down. Adjudicated timeline, my ass.'

But I didn't have time to bask. I'd forgotten how to bask.

Because the other timer — the real timer, the one nobody else in this arena could see — was screaming.

[Calamity Event Timer]

[Remaining time: 22 minutes.]

[ALICE STATUS: DISTRESS RISING.]

[The crowd noise. The mana flare. Thousands of hearts pounding around her in the dark stands.]

[The entity hears it all.]

[It is asking a question.]

[The question is: where is he?]

I dropped my practice sword in the sand.

Vance blinked at me. "Gray — the formal conclusion, you need to—"

"Sorry, emergency, apologies, great refereeing, ten out of ten!"

And I ran.

---

I poured everything into the sprint toward the exit tunnel, past the barrier mages' slack jaws, past Finn's outstretched high-five hand which I slapped out of pure reflex without breaking stride — "FINN STALL THEM TELL THEM I HAVE INTERNAL BLEEDING" — "THAT'S YOUR EXCUSE?!" — and into the torchlit dark of the tunnel.

Behind me, the arena kept roaring. Calling my name.

I didn't look back.

Not at Vance, standing over a practice sword abandoned at the victor's mark with the specific expression of a career instructor realizing he has a disciplinary nightmare on his hands.

Not at Lucas Ashford, on his feet now, forgotten protagonist of a story that had just hiccuped, staring at the tunnel with the look of a man who has watched something he cannot file under any category he owns.

Not at Rhea Valtor.

She had stood up before the declaration even finished. Her arms were still crossed, but her hands were gripping her own sleeves hard enough to strangle the fabric. *The timing read was hers. The disabling strike was hers. The dead space theory she'd pounded into me for four nights until my bruises had bruises.* He used it. All of it. In one move, one moment, with a precision she'd have graded acceptable from a senior.

And then he ran. Didn't celebrate. Didn't soak in the roar. Didn't even take the formal bows.

*Where,* she thought, nails biting into her palms, *the hell is he going?*

Not at Iris Aurelia.

In the VIP gallery, the Grand Duke's daughter was standing. She didn't remember standing. Her composure — the armor she'd worn for eleven years — had a crack in it wide enough to see through.

The Void Counter. Her Void Counter. The hinge principle, the sternum pivot, the thing she'd corrected him on with two fingers and glacial patience *four days ago.* Professional duelists spent years chasing that timing. Generals paid fortunes for instructors who could teach it. And a first-year with no mana had just executed it in front of two thousand people, perfectly, on the first attempt —

— and then thrown his sword in the sand and sprinted out of the arena like the building was on fire.

He hadn't looked at the crowd. He hadn't looked at the panel.

'He didn't care,' Iris realized, fingers slowly curling against the balcony rail. 'The duel. The fame. Any of it. That wasn't the point. The duel was an obstacle between him and — and something —'

Something that mattered more than two thousand people screaming his name.

"...What," she whispered, "are you chasing, Lucen Gray?"

And not at Alice.

She was in the middle tier, in the seat he'd chosen for her — close to an exit, far from the loudest sections. Invisible precautions he'd taken without explanation. The roar of the crowd was a sea crashing around her, ten thousand pounding hearts, and under it all, the thing coiled behind her ribs was *stretching.*

Her fingers tightened on the armrests.

Frost whispered outward.

It spread from her fingertips across the wood, out across the armrest, down the support strut, a lacework of ice crawling silently along her row in the dark where no one was looking. The girl beside her shivered and pulled her cloak closer, blaming the night wind.

[TIER 5]

[Entity containment: Tier 5 / FAILING.]

Alice's eyes were locked on the tunnel.

He was running. Not to celebrate. Not away from the crowd.

*Running toward the dormitory wing. Toward — her?*

*He knew. Somehow, impossibly — before she did — he knew something was wrong with her. That something was wrong with this night.*

'How does he know?'

The ice reached the seat-back beside her.

'Why does he always—'

---

I burst out of the arena into the cold night air, lungs already burning, festival lanterns streaking past.

[SYSTEM: DUEL — VICTORY.]

[Time: 3.2 seconds.]

[Method: Void Counter → Disabling Strike.]

[Combat footage will be reviewed by every faculty member and half the nobility in the empire within a week.]

[You just became the most talked-about first-year in Stellaris Royal Academy history. A no-mana commoner who ended a duel in three seconds and walked out on his own coronation.]

[Congratulations.]

[But none of that matters right now.]

'YEAH. NO KIDDING.'

The dormitory wing rose ahead, windows glowing — and then, one by one, starting from the upper floors, the lamps in Alice's corridor began to gutter and go dark.

Frost, crawling up the outside wall, silver-quiet in the moonlight.

[CALAMITY EVENT: IMMINENT.]

[Alice / Celestia Rimehart — Entity Containment: TIER 5. FAILING.]

[Bad End No. 22: The Frozen Library — Calamity Variant]

[Status: IMMINENT.]

[Timer: 19 minutes.]

[RUN.]

My legs were screaming. Lactic acid, stitch in my side, the bill for four days of midnight training coming due all at once.

I ran faster anyway.

'Hold on, Alice.'

'Forty-seven rain-slicked rooftop hours in *Eschaton Hearts* taught me one thing above everything else.'

'The timer is never the real boss.'

'The real boss is whether you get there before the music stops.'
