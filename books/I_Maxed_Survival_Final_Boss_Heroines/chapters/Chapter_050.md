# Chapter 50: End of Term: The Person Who Should Not Exist

\section*{End of Term: The Person Who Should Not Exist}

Midnight.

The campus was empty.

I walked the path from the seminary toward the dormitory, and for the first time in weeks, nobody was walking beside me.

No Iris, clicking her pen and analyzing my corpse from a polite distance. No Alice, radiating cold and cookies. No Rhea, hovering at exactly sword-range. No Lyra, perched somewhere above me like a candy-flavored gargoyle.

Just me. The twin moons. And a piece of forbidden jewelry that pulsed against my chest like a second heartbeat that hated me.

'Day 35, almost. Thirty-five days.'

'Thirty-five days since I crawled out of a ravine on my first morning in this body.'

I looked down at myself as I walked.

One cravat, midnight blue. From a girl with an analytical fortress around her heart.

One hairpin in my pocket, subtly enchanted. From a girl who baked her feelings because saying them was too dangerous.

One training token, worn smooth at the edges. From a girl chasing her father's ghost.

One butterscotch wrapper, still in my coat because I was apparently a hoarder of trash and trauma. From an ancient dragon who found me 'interesting.'

And now — one pendant. From a shepherd with kind hands and a slaughterhouse behind the door.

'Five accessories. Zero stats. Incredible loot distribution.'

'This is the worst MMO I've ever played.'

I stopped at the fork in the path — the same fork I'd stood at on Day 1, drenched in my own blood, staring at the academy like it was the final boss arena it was.

The moons were full. Both of them.

Two enormous silver eyes hanging over Stellaris Royal Academy, watching a boy who should not exist limp home.

'Fine. Let's do the math.'

'Straighten up, System. Show me the damage.'

I stared at it.

Then I laughed — one short, cracked sound into the empty night.

It was the same status window as Day 0. Identical. There had been no power fantasy. No hidden stat awakening, no secret class unlock, no 'You have absorbed the dragon's mana core!' notification.

I had not gotten stronger.

I had not gotten better.

By every metric that this world — that this *game* — measured a person by, I was exactly as worthless as the day I'd fallen out of a tree and into a ravine.

Mana Index 0.009. A magical presence weaker than an enchanted training dummy. The System's own flavor text didn't even call me an adventurer yet.

'A person who should not exist.'

'That one's accurate, at least.'

So why was I still walking?

I stood there under the moons and thought about it. Really thought about it. Without the panic, without the adrenaline, without the constant speedrunner calculation running in the corner of my brain.

And the answer kept coming back, wearing four different faces.

---

**Iris.**

Iris, who had been raised by a family that never once asked her if she was okay.

Iris, who hid a bleeding heart behind an analytical fortress so precise it could dissect a corpse and never once noticed she was describing herself.

Iris, who made me tea. Who fed me soup and pretended it was a control variable. Who sat through Decryption Sessions with me while I unraveled a language only one other person in this world had ever known — and who learned the shape of my name in Hangul because nobody had ever taught her that it was okay to want something small and useless and hers.

She put a cravat around my neck tonight that matched her eyes.

I was supposed to be afraid of her. I *was* afraid of her. She was canonically the girl who ended the world if you picked the wrong dialogue option in Year 3.

And I thought about her every evening when the tea went cold.

---

**Alice.**

Alice, who carried an ancient horror under her ribs and baked cookies so she'd have something to give instead of something to fear.

Alice, who cried in a frozen basement while I told her — with what felt like the last honest words I owned — that she was okay. That it was okay.

The moment I'd said it, something in the world had clicked into place. My void and her entity, tangled together like two threads pulled from the same cursed sweater.

If I died, she fell.

I hadn't asked for that responsibility. The bond had been a byproduct of survival, a glitch in the encounter design, a side effect of being in the wrong genre of game.

But it didn't come with a return policy.

'349 hours across all my runs, and nobody at Eschaton Hearts HQ thought to mention the emotional entanglement DLC.'

'I want a refund, and also I never want the refund.'

---

**Rhea.**

Rhea, who taught me to fight with hands that trembled every time they made contact with mine.

Rhea, searching for a father's ghost in an academy that had paved over him. Who asked me a question I couldn't answer, and accepted my silence with a grace that had broken something in my chest.

She deserved a real answer. She deserved the truth about the Ashenveil Retreat, about where I'd seen it, about what her father had been.

All I'd given her was silence and a shared training token.

And she stayed anyway.

---

**Lyra.**

Lyra, who was older than the roads. Lyra, with her child's hands and her ancient eyes, offering me butterscotch and calling me 'interesting' with the patient amusement of someone watching a moth land on a candle for the third time.

She was a catastrophe scheduled to happen. Every run of the game, every route, every timeline — she was the Bad End that arrived wearing a festival robe.

And she liked me.

And I had no idea what to do with that except keep showing up and unwrapping candy.

---

**Lucas.**

Lucas Ashford.

The hero. The actual, designated, protagonist-of-record hero. Seat 4-C, Homeroom 1-A, golden boy, destiny incarnate.

He was kind in a way that didn't require anything from you. He was brave in a way that didn't announce itself. He was everything I wasn't — not just his stats, not just his story role. He was *good* in the uncomplicated way I'd stopped believing in somewhere around my third dead-end job.

And my greatest fear — the one that kept me up at night when the pendant wasn't — was that my presence was dimming his light.

That the irregular in the code was taking up space the golden boy needed to grow.

'Three hundred forty-seven hours. I never played his route from the inside. I don't know what it looks like from the center.'

'I'm sorry, Lucas. I'm trying not to break your story.'

'I don't think I'm succeeding.'

---

**Voss.**

I put my hand over the pendant.

Inquisitor Serana Voss. The woman who had placed this thing around my neck with the gentle hands of a shepherd, knowing full well it was a leash.

The woman who had heard me talk about what she was — and decided it didn't matter, because the leash was already tied.

'Lock the door behind her. The Shepherd's Lamb is official.'

That's what she'd said, isn't it? In that head of hers. I didn't have to be in it to guess.

She thought she'd caught something.

Maybe she had.

I reached the dormitory steps and stood there for a long time, hand on my chest, feeling the pulse of the pendant sync up with — what? The Abyssal node? A tether point on her end? Some metaphysical void made of the two of us?

'It doesn't matter what it is.'

'What matters is what I do while it's on me.'

---

I pushed open the door.

My room was exactly as I'd left it: small, dim, and indisputably mine. A cot. A window. A cracked ceiling that I'd named Gerald in my head at some point around Day 12 because I needed something in this room that didn't plot against me.

I sat on the edge of the cot.

The cot creaked — one long, sympathetic objection, like it was reading the room.

The pendant sat cold against my sternum. Voss's gift. Voss's confidence. Voss's certainty that I would wear it because the one thing she'd learned about me was precisely correct: when it came to keeping people safe, I did not make rational decisions.

The Evil Goddess had put it into words earlier.

*'Three hundred forty-seven hours in the driver's seat, and the boy never learned the difference between protectiveness and surrender.'*

She thought I was surrendering.

For a long time — longer than I'd ever admit out loud to another person, if I ever found another person I talked to honestly — I thought she was right.

I took the chain in my fingers.

I felt the weight of it.

And then I made a decision.

Not to pull the pendant off.

Not to run.

Not to be invisible anymore.

Not to 'remain a background character' like some optimized route strategy that treated people as dialogue flags and affection numbers and Risk Factor — HIGH.

I'm going to protect them.

All of them.

Iris — because nobody has ever protected Iris in her life, and her fortress is so quiet and so complete and underneath it there's a girl who learned to make tea for someone and doesn't even know why it makes something in her chest feel strangely warm.

Alice — because I told her she was okay, and that's not something you take back. You don't say something like that and then let the person become the thing they were afraid of. The void and the entity are tangled now. If I let go, we both fall — so I won't let go.

Rhea — because her father deserves to be found, and she deserves to not find him alone. Even if I don't know the answer yet, I know where the question lives now. And I know how to look.

Lyra — because 'catastrophe waiting to happen' is not a personality, it's a fear. She's a person first. Or whatever she is first. She likes me — and I'd rather have that on the side of 'still here' than 'obliterated the academy.'

Lucas — because he deserves to shine, and if I'm the irregular mucking up his story, then I'll be the irregular that holds up the parts he can't see. He doesn't have to know. It doesn't have to be dramatic. I'll just be there.

Even Voss.

Especially Voss.

Because she tied herself to me with kind hands and an honest voice, and if she keeps being kind she will eventually look back and see the slaughterhouse, and someone needs to be there when that happens. Someone stubborn. Someone who does not fall. Someone who has already done the math on the 637 ways this goes wrong and is still standing.

'Yes. I know how that sounds. I know.'

'I'm aware this is the thought process of a man about to be devoured by a narrative.'

'I am choosing it anyway.'

The longest she had ever paused, actually. I could feel her attention like a held breath — like a Game Master staring at the table as the player walked off-script in a direction that didn't appear in any of her notes.

'Yeah,' I said to the empty room. 'I got it. I chose it.'

'I'm not a background character anymore, Kang Min-jun.'

'I might not be a protagonist.'

'But I'm done being scenery.'

**IRIS VON SEYRENTHAL — Study, Second Floor, Seyrenthal Wing**

The notebook lay open.

At the top of the page, in her foreign student handwriting — the elegant, sharp-angled script of a girl who had never once been taught how to write from her heart — was a name in a language that had no business existing on this continent:

**루센.**

She'd studied each stroke. Memorized the order. Practiced it until the ink stopped looking like an imitation and started looking like a signature that meant something.

*Ru-sen.*

*Lucen.*

Iris traced the characters with one finger, lingering on the second syllable.

She was smiling.

It was the softest expression she had worn since she was six years old, and nobody in the world had ever seen it, and she found that she did not mind that he — the subject of it — was not here to see it either.

Some things were for her alone.

She closed the notebook. Set it carefully on the shelf beside the first one. Lined up the spines.

'Profile 7-A: subject displays persistent, internally coherent altruism despite a survival profile that actively contradicts it,' she dictated softly, to no one. 'Conclusion: anomalous — but not a performance anomaly.'

'A genuine one.'

She was smiling again by the time the lamp went out.

**ALICE MERIDIAN — West Dorms, Third Floor**

The room was quiet.

The entity was dormant — as dormant as it ever got, down in the deep cold of her chest where she kept it like a terrible sleeping thing — and Alice sat cross-legged on the bed, holding the ice crystal in both hands.

It was the one from the greenhouse. The one he'd made on purpose. The one that was supposed to be impossible, and had been, until him.

It hadn't melted. Three weeks and it hadn't melted.

She held it up to the window, and the twin moonlight came through it in two pale lines, and she watched them cross on the wall.

'Bond status check,' she whispered to herself, into the room. 'Tomorrow. Session. Same time.'

The crystal pulsed. Once. Like something far away answering a knock.

Alice closed her fingers around it gently.

'See you tomorrow,' she whispered, softer.

The entity stirred, once, in its sleep — not with hunger. With something she had never once, in all her years of carrying it, felt from it before.

*Interest.*

---

**RHEA ASHVEIL — Training Grounds, Midnight**

She should have gone to bed hours ago.

Instead, Rhea was in the north yard alone under the moons, moving through the Ashenveil Retreat for the forty-first consecutive night — and it was only tonight, in the silence, that she noticed.

Her footwork was different.

One step in the third figure. The backward pivot before the seventh. Something in the angle of her leading shoulder that had not been there a month ago — something she had not practiced, and could not have taught herself, because it was not in her father's style.

It was in Lucen's.

She stopped. Stood in the middle of the yard. Looked down at her feet.

The ghost in her footwork was his. The boy who couldn't fight. The boy with the mana index of a paperweight. The boy who had looked her in the eye on the clinic floor and chosen, deliberately, to lose — and made it look exactly like what it wasn't.

Somewhere in all those drills, his dodging patterns had worked their way into her body.

She stood very still.

In the movements she could feel it — the shape of a style that didn't belong to either of them alone anymore. Her father's retreat, threaded through with the desperate, un-teachable improvisation of a boy who did not give up on anything, ever, even when he should.

His ghost. Her father's ghost. Both in her posture.

Rhea looked up at the moons.

'You're not supposed to do that to a person, Lucen Gray,' she said quietly.

She bowed at the yard — low, and formal, the way you bowed at an instructor.

Then she stayed a while longer.

---

**LYRA — The Rampart Wall, North Edge of Campus**

Lyra sat on the outer wall with her legs dangling over a three-story drop, watching the moons.

The song she was humming was older than the continent. She was fairly sure of that — she'd checked, once, and she'd been right before and after.

From her sleeve she produced a butterscotch. Unwrapped it. Placed it in her mouth.

Sugar. Nostalgia. An alloy of the two, whichever was older — she'd never successfully separated them, and she'd had ages to try.

She produced a second one.

Set it on the wall beside her. Looked at it.

'Tomorrow,' she said to it, companionably.

The dragon-scale pattern of her eyes reflected the moons in duplicate, two on two, and something very old and very patient and very fond considered the boy in the dormitory three towers east like a thing she intended to keep.

'Sleep well, interesting little void.'

'You have such a long way to go, and you're trying so very hard.'

'It is—' she paused. Considered the word in seventeen dead languages, found the one she wanted.

'—endearing.'

She ate her candy.

The second one stayed where it was, on the wall, beneath the patient sky.

For tomorrow.

For him.

---

**LUCAS ASHFORD — Dormitory 1-A, Desk by the Window**

The journal was open. The lamp was lit. It was much later than it should have been, but Lucas couldn't sleep — too much had happened, too fast, and he had never once been good at carrying things instead of examining them.

He wrote slowly, the way he did everything important.

*Day 35. The term is over.*

*There is something strange in this academy, and I haven't been able to look at it directly yet. I only ever see it out of the corner of my eye — in the classrooms and the courtyard and the quiet way certain people behave when a certain name comes up.*

*I found someone at this school who makes me want to be better.*

*I don't know why.*

*I just know I'm glad he survived.*

He looked at the page for a while. At the last sentence especially, because it felt louder than the rest, and he didn't know why it felt like that.

He closed the journal.

Tomorrow, he decided. Tomorrow he'd talk to Lucen properly, not in passing — not in the muddled, too-fast grammar of crisises and duels and kneeling on polished floors. Just... properly.

He didn't know yet that he had already promised that to himself on twelve other nights.

He'd surely know eventually.

---

**INQUISITOR SERANA VOSS — Office, Spire**

The Abyssal node sat on the desk, pulse muted to something almost — almost — comfortable. Aheartbeat.

Voss's hand covered the negative imprint of the Resonance Amplifier, the paired frequencies of her creation and his body running like a quiet current through the bones of her fingers.

He was sleeping now, she thought. Somewhere over there. In the dark. Eight bruises and a harbinger and a boy who made every wrong choice for the right reasons.

She closed her eyes.

'Three hundred forty-seven hours of watching you leave,' she whispered to the empty spire. 'Tonight, you stayed.'

'Sleep well, my little void.'

'We have so much work to do.'

The node thrummed once.

The moons watched through the spire window, patient, saying nothing.

The bruises were still there. Eight of them, yellowing at the edges. The pendant still rested against my sternum, cool and steady and very, very patient. Nothing had changed externally.

'Right,' I said to the empty room. I swung my legs off the cot. The floor was cold. The window was streaked with dawn. Stellaris Royal Academy was out there, sleeping off the first term, and out there in it were four girls and one golden hero and one kind shepherd, all of them about to wake up into the same morning I was waking into.

All of them thinking, somewhere in their day — if they were doing what Voss had done, if they were doing what Rhea had been doing on that darkened training ground, what Lyra was doing on her wall, what Iris was doing over her notebook —

— thinking, most unwise of all, about me.

The person who should not exist.

'Who exists anyway,' I told the empty room, to myself, to the dawn.

I grinned.

It wasn't a smart grin.

It was the grin of a speedrunner at the title screen, hovering over NG+.

'Six hundred and thirty-two left,' I said. 'No. Sixthundred and thirty-two *down*.'

'Let's start counting now.'

The twin moons were setting.

The sun was rising.

Day 35.

I was still alive.

I existed anyway.

I was going to keep doing that.
