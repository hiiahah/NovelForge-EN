# Chapter 29: Three Orbits, One Week

I didn't sleep that night.

Not because of the goddess. Not because of the [Sunrise Eternal] warning burning a hole in the corner of my vision. Not even because my hands still throbbed under Iris Aurelia's obsessively perfect bandage job.

I didn't sleep because I did something stupid.

I made a schedule.

In my head, obviously. My fingers were still two wrapped sausages of regret. But my brain — my beautiful, unemployed, 347-hour-completionist brain — insisted on doing what it always did when a game got complicated.

It built a route map.

It went like this:

**5:00 AM** — Dawn training with Rhea Valtor. Sword forms. Suffering. She touches my shoulders to correct my stance and my nervous system files a formal complaint.

**7:00 AM** — Breakfast. Delivered to my dormitory by Sera, on Iris's orders. Finn is now 70% convinced I have a secret admirer and 30% convinced I'm being slowly poisoned. He keeps offering to taste-test. I keep declining. For his sake.

**8:00 AM** — Classes. Seat 7-F. Back right corner, by the window, where God intended the forgettable extras to sit. Except Alice has started sitting next to me. She says it's because we're lab partners.

The lab meets twice a week.

She sits next to me every day.

**12:00 PM** — Lunch. Lucas Ashford has decided, with the unshakeable confidence of a protagonist who's never once been told no by the universe, that I am coming to the group table. Cedric and Mariel don't even ask anymore. They just scoot over.

I am bonding with the hero party.

Me. Background Character #47. Mana Index 0.009. Less magical than a haunted stick.

**4:00 PM** — Independent study. Currently devoted to one question: does a flower called "Lukaseu" actually exist anywhere in this world, or did I make up a fake family crest that Iris's intelligence network is going to discover is fake in approximately nine days?

(The answer, after four hours in the library annex, is: no. It doesn't exist. I'm a dead man.)

**7:00 PM** — Decryption Session, Tuesdays and Fridays. Iris Aurelia. The terrace. Tea that costs more than my tuition. Questions with trapdoors under them.

I stared at the ceiling of my dorm room and did the math.

Three heroines. Three daily orbits. One protagonist whose friend group I was accidentally joining. Plus classes, plus a fabricated noble lineage with a countdown timer, plus a wounded pair of hands.

'I am running seven save files at once,' I thought.

'And every single one of them is on fire.'

The training grounds at dawn were exactly as miserable as they always were, which is to say: cold, fog-drenched, and occupied by one (1) terrifying sword saint with red hair and the conversational warmth of a glacier.

"You're early." Rhea didn't turn around. She was going through a form — slow, deliberate, her practice blade carving lines through the mist like the mist had personally offended her.

"You're earlier."

"I am always earlier."

She finished the form, exhaled a plume of white, and turned.

"Today I teach you the first offensive kata of the Ashenveil form." She tossed me a practice blade. I caught it with my bandaged hands, which is to say the blade hit my chest and I scrambled to grab it like a raccoon catching a wedding bouquet.

Rhea closed her eyes.

Breathed deeply.

Opened them.

"Again," she said, without a single trace of emotion, which is how I knew she was screaming internally.

---

The kata was called *Ashwind's First Cut*. In the game, it was a DPS opener — a simple disabling strike to the weapon arm, flowing into a guard break. Simple. Elegant. The kind of move my character in Eschaton Hearts executed with a single button press.

In reality, it required my hips, shoulders, wrist angle, foot placement, weight distribution, and breathing to all agree with each other.

My body parts were not on speaking terms.

"No." Rhea crossed the training ground. "Your hips are lying to you."

"My hips are what now?"

"Here." And then her hands were on me.

One palm flat against the small of my back. The other gripping my shoulder, rotating it nine degrees. Her knee nudged my stance wider. Her foot hooked my ankle into position.

Clinical. Precise. Utterly without romance.

My skin, unfortunately, did not get the memo about the lack of romance. Everywhere she touched, my nerves lit up like a festival district at closing time.

'Calm down,' I told my cardiovascular system. 'She is adjusting your form. This is a tutorial. You have done tutorials. You are a grown man. You are Kang Min-jun, twenty-three, who once parried the Demon Lord's final phase blindfolded for a speedrun challenge—'

"Your heart rate spiked." Rhea's voice, right next to my ear. "Breathing is part of the form, Gray. Control it."

'I would like to formally die now.'

"Yes, instructor."

"Better." She stepped back. Assessed me with those amber eyes. "Awful. But less awful than yesterday. Again."

We drilled it for two hours.

By the end, I had performed Ashwind's First Cut approximately ninety times, and on attempt eighty-eight, something actually clicked — hips, shoulder, wrist, breath, all in one line, the dummy's arm joint cracking open under the practice blade.

For one second, I felt it. The thing the game could never render. The *flow*.

Rhea looked at the cracked dummy. Then at me.

"Acceptable," she said.

From Rhea Valtor, "acceptable" was a trophy ceremony with fireworks.

**7:00 AM. Breakfast.**

Sera appeared at my dormitory door with the precision of a military strike: a covered tray, a nod, and that look she had — the one that said *I know exactly what you are, commoner, and I am watching*.

Then, today, a twist.

"Also." She produced a small folded note. "From Her Highness."

She left. Finn materialized from behind his desk like a goblin smelling loot.

"Is that a LOVE LETTER?"

"It's an assassination threat, probably."

The note, in Iris's immaculate handwriting:

*Lukaseu — You missed exactly zero check-in meals this week. Adequate. The kitchen has included a pastry variety you have not yet tried. Report whether it is acceptable. — I.A.*

I stared at the note.

'She wants a pastry review.'

The Crown Princess of the Empire — the Dawn Tyrant, the final boss of the main route, the woman who in Bad End No. 12 had burned a duchy to the waterline — was soliciting my opinion on baked goods.

I ate the pastry. It was incredible. Of course it was.

"What do I even put in the report," I muttered.

"Tell her it was mid," Finn said. "Build mystery."

"Finn. If I tell the Crown Princess her pastry was 'mid,' I will cease to be a citizen of this plane of existence."

"...Write that it's good, then. Coward."

**8:22 AM. Corridor, east wing.**

"Lucen!"

I knew that voice. I knew the exact pitch, frequency, and the specific warmth of it, the way a man who has stood too close to the blast zone knows the sound of the whistle before the artillery lands.

Alice.

Celestia Rimehart. The girl who was not entirely a girl. The walking containment vessel for something old and hungry that lived behind her smile. My lab partner.

She rounded the corner with a small tray held out in front of her like an offering.

"I baked cookies! Try one."

Pale-blue cookies. Dusting of frost-sugar that glittered faintly in the corridor light, like the surface of a frozen lake. They were beautiful. They were perfect.

And every single one of my 347 hours of playtime was screaming at me.

Because I knew this mechanic. I'd seen it in her route. Alice — or rather, the thing that resonated through Alice — didn't do anything so crude as poison or love potions. Her baking carried trace Abyssal resonance. Micro-doses. Harmless, technically.

What it did was create a bond. A tiny, warm, magnetic pull between the baker and the eater. Not mind control. Not compulsion, exactly.

Magical comfort food.

You eat one, and for the rest of the day, being near her feels like sitting next to a fireplace in winter. You eat five, and you start rearranging your schedule to walk past her classroom. You eat them for a month, and—

'In the game, there was a whole hidden affection metric. The community called it the Cookie Counter. Players joked about it.'

Nobody joked about how the route ended if you maxed it out.

"Lucen?" Alice tilted her head. Her smile was bright and hopeful and open, and the awful thing — the truly awful thing — was that I couldn't tell where Alice ended and the Abyss began. I genuinely couldn't. In the game, even the writers had left it ambiguous.

When she held out cookies, was it the eldritch entity fishing for resonance?

Or was it a lonely girl who baked because it was the only way she knew how to say *please be my friend*?

The cruelest possibility was that it was both. That there was no seam. That the monster and the girl had been the same person all along.

I could refuse. It would be safe. It would be smart.

It would also be the cruelest thing anyone had done to her this week, and the week shift-changing with an eldritch horror was exactly the kind of emotional damage that turned [Route: ACTIVE] into [Bad End: The Devouring Cold].

I took a cookie.

Her whole face lit up like a constellation coming online.

I bit in.

'Oh no.'

It was the best thing I had ever eaten in either of my lives. Warm despite the frost-sugar, buttery, with some bright wintry note underneath that made my empty mana channels hum in a key I didn't know they had. My void — my 0.009 of nothing — *purred*.

I immediately, violently wanted another one.

"Good, right?" Alice beamed, rocking on her heels.

"It's... really good," I said, which was true, and which was the problem.

"I made a whole batch! I can bring more tomorrow. And Thursday. And— oh, do you like raspberry? I can do raspberry—"

She was already planning. I was already lost. I chewed my ensnaring cookie on the way to class and sat in seat 7-F in a state of profound spiritual defeat while Alice settled into the seat beside me, radiating contentment like a small sun.

**12:00 PM. Lunch.**

"GRAY!"

Lucas Ashford intercepted me in the dining hall with a grip on my shoulder that had the sheer gravitas of destiny itself. Seat 4-C energy. Golden boy energy. Protagonist energy.

"Group table. Today. No arguments. Cedric saved you a seat and Mariel said, and I quote, 'the quiet one with the busted hands is permitted.'"

"That might be the nicest thing anyone's said to me all week."

And so I went. Again. For the fourth time.

The terrifying part was how *normal* it was becoming. Cedric argued about dueling circuit rankings. Mariel narrated, in a flat monotone, an incident in which a second-year's summoned familiar had gotten into the pantry. Lucas laughed too loudly. Someone pushed a plate toward me without asking, because at this table food just moved toward whoever needed it, like the party AI was handling logistics.

Warm. Loud. Easy.

This was the part nobody warns you about. The companions aren't warning signs. The companions are *nice*. The flags aren't red, they're warm yellow and they look like lamps on a porch.

In the game, this was Week One of the prologue. The calm before every route began detonating.

I caught myself smiling into my soup and had a small internal crisis about it.

'Do not get attached. Attachment is the failure state. You have read the ending trees. You know what happens to the side characters the hero loves when the routes go bad—'

"Gray? You good? You did the thing where your face goes somewhere."

"Thinking about homework," I lied.

"Nerd," said Mariel, with her mouth full.

I liked her too. I was doomed.

**2:40 PM. Corridor, north stairwell.**

Someone decided I was having too nice a day.

Derrick Holt. Third son of a marquis, second-rate duelist, first-rate grudge — he'd been circling me ever since the Practical, ever since everyone saw a 0.009 walk out of the assessment without dying. Today he was leaning against the stair rail with two friends, doing the casual-wall-of-nobility formation.

"Gray." His smile had no business being that smug for someone whose house crest was a sheep. "Heard you've been busy. Breakfasts from *Her Highness*. Cookies from the ice girl. Training with the Valtor." He tutted. "Lots of effort for someone at the bottom of the register. Are you hoping proximity rubs off?"

In the game, Derrick Holt existed to be punched by the protagonist in Chapter 12 or so.

I looked at his face. Tallied my options. Physically: I could not beat him. Ashwind's First Cut worked on dummies, not smug marquis sons, and my hands were still mummified.

So I deployed the most devastating weapon in a speedrunner's arsenal.

I said nothing, nodded politely, and walked around him.

The sheer anticlimax hit harder than any punch. I heard one of his friends snort. Derrick's voice went up half an octave behind me — "—I'm *talking* to you—" — and then stopped, because shouting down a hallway at a commoner's back was beneath even his dignity.

'"What isn't worked on, isn't finished against,"' I thought. 'Sun Tzu. Or somebody. Doesn't matter. Ratio'd.'

**4:00 PM. Library annex.**

No such flower as "Lukaseu." Not in the botanical register, not in the florist's guild archive, not in the horticultural records of three provinces.

There was a *Lukasen vine*. Poisonous. There was a *Lukai blossom*. Extinct. There was nothing I could reasonably, defensibly claim was my family flower in time for Iris's inevitable verification.

'I've lied to the intelligence chief of the empire and the only thing between me and the Intelligence Bureau is horticultural fiction.'

I closed the book and put my bandaged forehead against the table.

Tomorrow. I'd solve it tomorrow. Friday's session existed on the other side of two more days of orbits, and right now, all I could do was keep them spinning.

---

**Tuesday. 7:00 PM. The terrace.**

Iris Aurelia sat with tea, a folder, and the silhouette of a god.

Evening session. The light came off the western sky in slabs of gold and lay itself across her hair and shoulders like it had been designed to. Infirmary. Bandaged hands. *Don't break them again.* Distant early warning. I shoved all of it into a box and sat down across from her.

"You're tense," she said, before I'd even reached for the cipher.

"Long day."

"I know. I have the notes." She opened the folder. Calmly. Casually. The way you open a menu. "You've been spending time with Celestia Rimehart."

The tea in my stomach turned to ice water.

"She's my lab partner."

"She's also been following you." Iris took a sip. "Specifically. Her movement patterns through the academy have shifted to overlap with yours. Corridors, meal halls, the annex. The overlap began the day after your lab assignment and has increased in frequency at a rate that isn't explained by course schedules."

'Alice has been following me? That hard?'

I hadn't even noticed. I'd been too busy not dying to notice that my eldritch lab partner had been quietly rerouting her entire existence through mine.

"I have Sera's reports," Iris continued, with the breeziness of a woman discussing rainfall. "And Rhea Valtor still trains you at dawn. Every day. And Lucas Ashford considers you a close friend — he's said so, at the group table, in those words, to three separate people."

She turned a page of the folder.

She had a *folder*.

She had a file on my social life that was more complete than my own awareness of it.

"You're very popular, Lucen Gray," she said, "for someone who claims he wants to be invisible."

And there it was. Under the composure. Under the porcelain absolute calm. Something tight in her voice. A hairline fissure. In another person — a normal person, a person with a normal affection axis — you would call it jealousy.

But Iris Aurelia didn't feel jealousy. Iris Aurelia conducted territorial analysis. Assessments of influence. Strategic concern over a volatile intelligence asset's network exposure.

It was completely different.

It was not different at all.

"I'm not trying to be popular," I said carefully. "These things just... happened. The lab assignment was random. Valtor drafted me. Ashford —" I gestured helplessly, "—Ashford happens *at* people. You've met him."

Something flickered in her eyes. Almost a smile. "I have met him."

"So why the dossier?"

She was quiet for a moment. The tea went dark in the last of the sun.

"Because things around you keep rearranging themselves, Lukaseu." Her voice was even. "People. Schedules. Rhea Valtor, who has never taught a first-year. Celestia Rimehart, who has never followed anyone. Me."

The last word landed soft and heavy.

"You say you want to be invisible," she said, "and yet everything in this academy is slowly orbiting you. I find that either meaningless—" the faintest edge sharpened her tone, "—or meaningful. And I dislike mysteries when they have a pulse."

'She's watching all three of them. She's watching all of us. And she does not, not even for a second, understand that the thing she's feeling has a name.'

Which meant I had to be the one who managed it. The only player at the table who knew it was a game.

"We should do the cipher," I said.

"Yes," said Iris Aurelia, closing the folder with finality. "We should."

We did the cipher. But her gaze kept drifting to my bandaged hands on the table, and twice — twice — she refilled my tea herself instead of letting Sera do it.

**That night. 11:40 PM.**

My dormitory. Finn snoring across the room with the heroic shamelessness of a man with no flags at all, the lucky bastard.

And me, staring at the ceiling, doing the math that no speedrunner ever wants to do.

Three heroines.

Iris — Decryption Sessions, meals, surveillance, and an affection level of 5 that was trending somewhere neither of us had a name for. Cutting contact meant the Intelligence Bureau. Locked.

Rhea — dawn training, patient hands, "acceptable." Cutting contact meant losing the only physical survival training I'd ever get, and telling the sword saint *no thanks* was its own death flag. Locked.

Alice — the cookie now humming in my void like a tuning fork. Her entity was partially resonating with me. Distance could destabilize her. A destabilized Alice wasn't a breakup; it was a weather event. Locked.

I couldn't push any of them away.

Every route depended on the others staying warm. Every escape hatch was wired to a different bomb. I wasn't juggling anymore — the knives were juggling *me*.

"I'm trapped," I said softly, to no one.

And the System, which had been quiet all evening in a way that I now understood was *loading*, answered.

'I am so, so screwed.'
