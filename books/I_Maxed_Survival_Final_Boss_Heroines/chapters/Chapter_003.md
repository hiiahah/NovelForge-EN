# Chapter 3: Enrollment by Clerical Error

The Registration Hall of Stellaris Royal Academy was carved from white marble, lit by floating crystal chandeliers, and filled with approximately two hundred of the wealthiest children on the continent.

And me.

One boy in a torn servant's tunic held together by hope and dried river mud.

'I have never felt more like a cockroach at a banquet.'

The girl standing in line behind me was wearing an actual tiara. An honest-to-god, diamond-studded, "my family owns three duchies" tiara. She was looking at me the way one looks at a stain on a white carpet.

I pretended not to notice.

Survival rule number one for extras in noble settings: **exist as background noise.**

Nobles don't kill background noise. Nobles kill things that *bother* them.

"Next!"

The line shuffled forward.

I shuffled with it, clutching nothing, holding nothing, owning nothing except a pair of boots with holes in them and a comprehensive, encyclopedic knowledge of every way this academy would be destroyed in the next three years.

'One hundred forty-two students. I recognize at least thirty faces from the game's CG gallery. That one with the green hair is the student council treasurer. That one picks his nose in the library CG. That one—'

My eyes swept the crowd again.

'—is irrelevant. Focus. Registration closes at sunset. I have maybe two hours.'

I had a plan.

A beautiful, airtight, water-tight plan.

There was just one small problem with it, which we would get to shortly.

---

The registration desk was manned by an elderly clerk with a magnificent bald head, spectacles perched on the very tip of his nose, and the general aura of a man who had personally stamped forty thousand documents and hated every single one of them.

The nameplate on his desk read: **REGISTRAR HELMOND — ADMINISTRATION, 41 YEARS OF SERVICE**.

'Forty-one years. This man has seen things. He's seen every scheme, every fake noble, every forged seal. I must be careful.'

"Name," he said, without looking up.

"Lucen Gray."

"House?"

"...No house."

His quill paused.

"Family name is Gray. That's it."

"Mm." He scribbled. "Proof of identity?"

Here it came.

"Lost it. Bandit attack on the eastern road, three days ago. Everyone in the caravan was—" I lowered my eyes. "I was the only one who made it out."

Technically true, if you replaced "bandit attack" with "waking up inside a video game as a disposable NPC servant boy who canonically dies in the opening cinematic."

Close enough. My conscience filed it under *acceptable translations.*

"I'm sorry for your loss," Helmond said, and for a moment he sounded like he actually meant it. "However. Policy requires documentation."

"Of course. I understand."

"Next is the mana index reading. Place your hand on the orb."

A crystal orb sat on the desk, glowing faintly.

In the game, this was a flavor animation. A little light show. The mana index determined your starting spell list.

I placed my hand on it.

The orb sat there.

Dim.

Dimmer.

It flickered once, apologetically, like a candle being told disappointing news, and displayed a number.

**0.009.**

Helmond stared at the orb.

The orb stared back, ashamed.

"point zero zero nine," Helmond repeated flatly.

"I've been told I'm very stable," I said.

"That's not how it— boy, the minimum is one point zero. The floor is one point zero. We have *furniture* in this academy with a higher mana index than you."

'Why is he saying it like that. Why is he saying it LIKE THAT.'

"I know."

"You *know?*"

"That's why I'm here. Clause 77-B of the Imperial Education Charter."

Helmond's spectacles slid down his nose.

"...I'm sorry. What did you just say?"

---

Hook, line, and sinker.

'Clause 77-B, enacted two hundred years ago after the Vernic Civil War. "In times of extraordinary peril or social disruption, the Academy Headmaster or any tenured faculty may grant provisional enrollment to individuals of demonstrated merit, pending formal documentation within one academic term."'

The clause was a fossil. A piece of legal archaeology buried in the charter, used exactly twice in two centuries.

Most people didn't even know it existed.

I knew it existed because in the game, the protagonist Lucas Ashford invokes it during the tutorial — sponsored by Professor Merith after he saves her from a carriage accident on the road today, right around now, canonically speaking.

My plan had been simple.

Let Lucas have his tutorial event. Find a *different* tenured professor. Any professor. A broom closet of a professor. The most boring, plot-irrelevant instructor on staff.

Get sponsored quietly.

Enroll quietly.

Vanish into the student body like a rat into the walls.

A perfect plan.

"Clause 77-B," Helmond said slowly, "requires a tenured faculty sponsor. In person. Willing to sign."

"Yes."

"Do you have one?"

"...Not yet."

There was a silence.

"Young man," Helmond said, rubbing the bridge of his nose, "it is two hours to sunset. Every professor in this academy is occupied with orientation preparations. You expect one of them to wander past and sponsor a boy with no papers, no mana, and" — he looked at my tunic — "apparently no change of clothing, out of sheer whimsy?"

'When he says it like that it sounds bad.'

"That is the plan, yes."

"That is a *terrible* plan."

"It's the only plan I've got."

And here is where things went wrong.

Because Helmond looked at me.

Really looked at me.

And I watched the gears in this old bureaucrat's head turn, watched him reconstruct my situation from the available data: the mud-stained clothes, the exhausted eyes, the story of a dead caravan, and the desperate, sweating boy who knew an obscure two-hundred-year-old clause by heart and refused, *refused*, to walk away.

I watched his expression soften.

'No.'

"A war orphan," Helmond murmured, mostly to himself. "Caravan dead. Family gone. Walked here alone. And still he stands straight. Still he *asks correctly*, like a gentleman..."

'No no no no.'

"Um. Sir. It's really not that—"

"Too proud to beg," Helmond whispered, dabbing at his eye with a handkerchief that I definitely did not see him prepare.

'WHERE did he pull that handkerchief from, it does NOT matter—'

"Sir, honestly, I'm just—"

"Even now, he minimizes." Helmond's voice cracked. "Forty-one years of service and the nobles throw tantrums over seating charts while *this boy* stands here with dignity—"

The noble girl with the tiara was openly staring now.

Several other people were staring.

A small crowd was beginning to form, drawn by the magnetic force of an old man crying at a desk.

'Abort. ABORT. This is the opposite of invisible. I am becoming a *human interest story*.'

"Sir, please, if I could just ask one question!" I said, loud and desperate, grabbing the thread of the conversation like a drowning man grabbing a rope. "Does the sponsor *have* to be a professor? The clause says tenured faculty. Does that mean *any* tenured faculty? Anyone at all?"

Helmond sniffed, composing himself with visible effort.

"Well. Yes, technically, any tenured faculty member. But boy, they're all—"

"A professor, a department head, an administrator... anyone?"

"Anyone with tenure, I suppose, but I hardly see what—"

And that is when a voice spoke from directly behind me.

"I'll sponsor him."

---

The hall went quiet.

Not regular quiet.

*Cathedral* quiet.

The kind of quiet that happens when the ambient mana in a room bends, politely, toward a single person, the way flowers bend toward the sun.

I knew that voice.

Oh, how I knew that voice.

I had heard that voice across three playthroughs, in exactly one scene each time — the hidden route scene, the one locked behind a 95% completion rate, where she smiles at the camera-of-the-protagonist in Year 3 and says *"I've been watching you grow for so long"* before the sky opens and everything the academy stands on comes screaming apart.

My entire body became a single, continuous held breath.

'Turn around. You have to turn around. Turning around is survivable. Not turning around is not survivable.'

I turned around.

She was tall, silver-haired, wearing robes of immaculate midnight blue. Her face was kind. Soft, even — the face of everyone's favorite teacher, the administrator who remembered students' names, who personally approved hardship scholarships, who was, by universal account, the warmest heart in the entire academy.

Vice-Headmistress Elara Voss.

Tenured faculty.

Beloved by all.

And the secret, smile-first-high-priestess-later architect of the Church of Ending — the hidden primary antagonist whose grand project, currently three years from completion, was to tear open the Abyssal Rift sitting directly under this building's foundations like an unpinned grenade.

"The Shepherd," I had called her, back when she was pixels.

'The Shepherd. The SHEPHERD. Of all the faculty, of every tenured nobody in this entire building, WHY—'

My brain produced a wall of static approximately the size of the continental shelf.

"She doesn't know. She doesn't know yet. Calm down. She's just being kind. She does this. She does NICE THINGS. It's part of her cover character. She's method acting. CALM. DOWN."

"You poor thing," Elara Voss said, looking me over with warm, maternal concern. "Helmond, surely we can make an exception?"

"Vice-Headmistress, I — the boy has been invoking Clause 77-B—"

"Then it's settled, isn't it?" She smiled. It was luminous. It was genuinely, horribly luminous. "I'm tenured faculty. I'll sponsor him."

"No need," I blurted.

Everyone stared at me.

'WHAT ARE YOU DOING, MOUTH.'

"I mean—" I swallowed. "—no need to trouble yourself, Vice-Headmistress. Surely someone less important could— ah. That came out wrong. I mean, someone of *lesser* importance could— that's also wrong. I mean—"

She laughed.

A soft, delighted laugh, and the crystalline charm of it rippled across the hall, and I could feel every noble girl in the vicinity mentally taking notes.

"How refreshing," she said. "Everyone wants *more* from me. You're the first person this year to want less."

'That's because everyone else in this hall is scheduled to survive the next three years. I'd like to join them.'

But.

This was the math of it, the cold speedrunner math: refusing a Vice-Headmistress's personal sponsorship, publicly, stiffly, for no coherent reason, was the single most memorable thing a servant boy with no papers could possibly do in that hall.

And being *memorable* to this woman was tantamount to being *signed into her ledger.*

Refusal was a flag.

Acceptance was... probably also a flag, but at least it was a flag on the *scripted* side of the fence, nominally.

'There's no clean option. There's never a clean option. Fine. FINE. Choose the survivable bad option. Like always.'

"...Then," I said, bowing my head. "Thank you, Vice-Headmistress. I'm grateful."

"Wonderful," she said, and I could have sworn, *sworn*, that for a fraction of a second her eyes lingered on mine with something more precise than kindness.

Something like interest.

Something like *inventory*.

---

The paperwork took four minutes.

Elara Voss signed the provisional enrollment form with elegant, flowing script, her penmanship so beautiful it looked printed. Helmond stamped it with trembling hands, still misty-eyed, murmuring something about "the spirit of the charter" and "what the founders intended."

I received a provisional student ID crystal.

**LUCE N GRAY — PROVISIONAL — TERM PENDING DOCUMENTATION.**

"Congratulations," Helmond said, gripping my hand in both of his. "You fought for this, young man. Don't let anyone tell you otherwise."

'I did not fight for anything. I exploited a two-hundred-year-old legal loophole and accidentally farmed a crying old man. But thank you.'

"I'll do my best," I said, because it was the safest possible sentence.

"You already have."

Behind me, soft footsteps.

"Lucen Gray."

I turned.

Elara Voss extended the enrollment form toward me, a copy for my records, held between two elegant fingers.

I reached for it.

Our fingers brushed.

Her skin was cold.

Not cool. *Cold.* The specific, wrong cold of something that has been in a deep place for a long time and has not fully reheated.

"You have very interesting eyes, Lucen Gray," she said.

Her smile widened by exactly one degree.

"I look forward to seeing what you become."

And then the world chimed.

**[This was not supposed to happen.]**

'Join the club. We have jackets. The jackets are also screaming.'

**[Bad Ends Remaining: 636.]**

**[Notes: Enrollment rank — Provisional Fourth-Class. Mana index — 0.009. Social standing — 'the mud boy'. Death flags acquired within first hour of enrollment — 1.]**

**[Note: The Evil Goddess has stopped screaming and is now laughing in a deeply concerning way.]**

"Is something wrong?" Elara asked, tilting her head.

Her eyes were pale grey.

Up close, they had a quality I had never been able to see through a monitor — a depth, a *distance*, like looking into a well and realizing the well was looking back and taking measurements.

"Nothing, Vice-Headmistress," I heard myself say. "Just... can't believe I'm really enrolled."

"Believe it." She turned, robes whispering against marble. "Oh, and Mr. Gray?"

"Yes?"

"Welcome to Stellaris." A final glance over her shoulder. "It welcomes everyone, in the end."

She glided away through the parting crowd of nobles, and the sunlight caught her silver hair, and she looked, to every single other person in that hall, like an angel doing charity.

'It welcomes everyone. In the end. IT WELCOMES EVERYONE IN THE END. Lady, I know where this school is BUILT, you get zero points for ominous wordplay—'

My hands, I noticed distantly, were shaking.

I gripped my enrollment form harder so no one would see.

Thirty seconds.

I had been an enrolled student for thirty seconds, and I had already collected a bad ending flag from the final boss of Act 2 — an antagonist who, canonically, should not have learned my name for another three years.

'So. One of the three flags the system detected earlier... wasn't something I stumbled into.'

'It was watching the registration hall.'

'Deduction, you absolute idiot: she was already watching.'

The question — the question standing in the middle of my mind with its arms folded — was *why*.

In the game, the Shepherd didn't notice background characters. She noticed *anomalies*. Mismatches. Things that didn't belong in her carefully arranged world.

A servant boy who wasn't in any of her ledgers, walking into a hall he had no business entering, quoting a two-hundred-year-old law by heart...

'I thought I was being invisible.'

'I was being *anomalous*.'

**[Hint: Aggressive invisibility requires actually being invisible.]**

**[Note: The Evil Goddess suggests you are, in fact, a glowstick wearing a camouflage jacket.]**

"Shut up," I muttered.

"Did you say something?"

The tiara girl. She was staring at me with the expression of someone witnessing an animal speak.

"No. Nothing."

"Hm." She looked me up and down — the mud tunic, the holey boots, the form clutched in my white-knuckled fist — and delivered her verdict. "A charity case."

"...Yes. That's exactly what I am. Nothing more."

"Obviously." She flicked her hair and swept past toward the desk, already forgetting me.

'Thank you. BLESS you, genuinely. Be exactly this dismissive of me forever. You and every noble in this building. I'm begging you all to keep underestimating me for four consecutive years.'

I looked down at my student ID crystal one more time.

Provisional.

Fourth-class.

One foot inside the walls.

Vicer-Headmistress Elara Voss's handwriting still drying on my papers.

Step one: complete.

Step two: in progress, and already on fire.

'...Okay. Inventory check. What do I actually have?'

I closed my eyes, alone in the marble crowd, and ran the list.

One: enrollment. Done, catastrophically.

Two: knowledge. Three full playthroughs of canon — every route, every event, every death flag, every hidden boss trigger.

Three: a mana index of 0.009, the measurably less magical than an enchanted wooden training dummy.

Four: a bad end flag attached to my neck within the first hour.

Five...

Five, the absolute certainty that canon had already drifted.

Elara Voss wasn't scripted to sponsor anyone today.

Which meant either my presence was already bending events...

...or the world's script was looser than the game had ever shown, and the things I "knew" were only mostly true, in the way a map is mostly true until you walk off the edge of it.

'Great. So my walkthrough has a margin of error. And the margin of error has silver hair and cold hands.'

The sun was setting outside the great windows. Orientation feast tonight. Dormitory assignments at dusk.

Lucas Ashford, the protagonist, was presumably somewhere out there right now, saving Professor Merith from a carriage, launching his beautiful scripted destiny, exactly on schedule.

Somebody's story was going according to plan, at least.

Wasn't mine, but somebody's.

'Fine. Recalibrate. New immediate objectives.'

I straightened my back.

One: survive the orientation feast without drawing attention.

Two: get assigned to the cheapest, moldiest, most forgettable dormitory room in this academy — the one in the cellar of the old east wing, which I knew for a fact stayed empty for two years because students thought it was haunted.

It was not haunted.

It was, however — according to the Year 2 collapse event — the single structurally safest room in the entire academy, seated directly over the Rift's only *reinforced* pillar.

A tragic coincidence of game lore, currently available to any boy smart and dead inside enough to volunteer for a basement.

'Let the nobles have the towers. Heights are for people who don't know what's underneath.'

Three...

I touched the edge of the enrollment form, where her signature glinted.

Three: find out why Elara Voss looked at me like she was reading a page she hadn't written.

'Without ever letting her look at me that way again.'

'Day one,' I thought. 'Shall we?'
