# Chapter 16: Sparring with the Sun

I didn't sleep well.

Shocking, I know. A man facing his own execution usually doesn't drift off counting sheep.

I lay in my dorm bed running Lucas Ashford's moveset through my head like a speedrunner reviewing a boss fight VOD. Frame by frame. Pixel by pixel.

*Three-hit combo. Pause. Overhead strike. Lateral sweep.*

*Three-hit combo. Pause. Overhead strike. Lateral sweep.*

347 hours of gameplay, and roughly sixty of those had been spent fighting sparring-version Lucas across a dozen different routes. The problem was that in the game, I'd been controlling a character I could buff, gear, and spec.

Here, the character build was: *Level 1. Mana Index 0.009. Special Skill: Vibrates with Anxiety.*

'It's fine.'

'He's friendly. He's the protagonist. Protagonists don't maim extras in friendly spars.'

'Probably.'

'Min-jun, buddy, the last time you used the word "probably" about this world, a freeze-tag penalty nearly liquefied your soul.'

I got up before dawn anyway.

Panic is a great alarm clock. Highly recommend it. Zero cost, terrible user experience.

---

The Central Training Arena was one of those buildings that existed purely to make you feel poor.

Stellaris Royal Academy didn't do anything halfway, and the arena was the proof. A massive domed hall with skylights cut into the ceiling at precise angles so the morning sun poured down in golden columns, like the gods themselves were turning on the stadium lights.

Which, knowing this universe, they probably were. Personally. Out of spite.

Polished stone floor. Tiered gallery seating rising up into shadow. Weapon racks along the walls stocked with practice swords — blunted wooden things that couldn't kill you but could absolutely make you wish they had.

I arrived shamefully early. Functional strategy: get in, warm up, do the thing, get out before the rumor mill spun up.

The rumor mill, I would later learn, had been spinning since 6 AM.

Finn had talked. Of course Finn had talked. Finn talked the way other people breathed.

But I didn't know that yet. I was standing alone in a pool of morning sunlight, doing warm-up stretches, feeling cautiously optimistic.

My first mistake of the day.

---

"Oh! You're already here."

I turned.

Lucas Ashford walked in through the main entrance, and I'm going to be honest — it was offensive.

The morning light hit him through the eastern skylight at the exact angle to make his blonde hair glow like a blessed relic. He moved with that effortless, liquid grace of someone whose body had never once betrayed him. Practiced sword resting on his shoulder. Smile warm enough to be classified as a heat source.

The protagonist.

The genuine, certified, flagship-model protagonist of Eschaton Hearts.

Four feet away from me. Smiling at me like we were friends.

We were not friends. We had shared one lunch and one tray of alleged meat buns. That was an acquaintance at best. A bun-ship.

'Oh no. He's more handsome in person. This is a hostile environment.'

"Morning, Lucen," he said brightly. "I'm really glad you agreed to this. I was worried I wouldn't find a sparring partner this early."

"You won't," said a familiar voice in my head, in a voice only I could hear. Wait. No. That was just my own anxiety.

"I'll do my best not to waste your time," I said instead. Diplomatic. Neutral. Extra-appropriate.

Lucas laughed — easy, genuine — and strolled over to the weapon racks.

He selected a practice sword and began stretching. Shoulders. Back. Legs. Each movement smooth as poured water, the kind of flexibility that came from a lifetime of actual structured training rather than, in my case, a lifetime of sitting in a gaming chair shaped like a racing car.

Fine. I could stretch too.

I bent forward to touch my toes.

*Crack.*

I rotated my shoulders.

*Pop.*

I twisted my spine.

*Crunch.*

Three sounds. Three separate joints filing formal complaints with management.

Lucas glanced over, mildly concerned. "Are you alright?"

"That's just my body's morning anthem," I said. "Every joint gets a verse."

He stared at me for a second.

Then he laughed again, shaking his head like I'd said something genuinely witty instead of a cry for help.

God. He even laughed like a protagonist. Warm, slightly surprised, like joy was his default setting and the world kept pleasantly exceeding it.

'He seems personally invested in my well-being. That's the oath-energy the Goddess warned me about. He's an irradiated kindness reactor and I'm standing downwind.'

'Do not get attached, Min-jun. Do NOT get attached. You are a cockroach. Cockroaches do not befriend the sun.'

---

We took our positions on the sparring circle.

Lucas rolled his shoulders once, settled into his stance, and—

'Oh no.'

'That's it. That's the stance.'

Right foot forward at forty-five degrees. Weight distributed sixty-forty. Blade held at center line, grip relaxed but ready. Elbow tucked. Guard compact.

I had seen this stance hundreds of times. On a screen. Usually right before my HP bar filed for bankruptcy.

It was textbook-perfect. No — it was *better* than textbook. The game developers had clearly mo-capped someone excellent, and the original Lucas Ashford was that someone, and he was currently aiming all of it at *me*.

"Ready?" he asked.

"Define ready," I muttered.

"What?"

"Nothing. Yes. Ready."

I raised my wooden sword into the guard position Penelope Vass had lectured into me over brew charts, and which Rhea Valtor had spent a painful morning hammering into my shoulders.

Feet staggered. Knees soft. Blade high, not too high.

Rhea's voice echoed in my skull: *'Stop holding the sword like it owes you money.'*

I adjusted my grip.

'Okay. Speedrunner mindset. This is just a boss fight. Phase one. Learn the patterns, don't greed for damage, reset if—'

I couldn't reset.

'—okay, just don't die.'

Lucas smiled.

"Then — begin."

---

He moved.

Fast.

Not full speed — I could tell immediately, because I'd seen full speed — but a clean, committed opening thrust aimed at my center mass. Textbook test attack. Checking my reactions.

'Thrust. Right-side parry, step off-line. You know this. You've done this four hundred times.'

*With a controller,* a treacherous part of my brain added.

I stepped off the line and let the blade slide past my hip.

My twist — the upper-body correction Rhea had drilled into me yesterday until my obliques screamed — carried me half a foot out of the strike path without wasting a single movement.

Lucas's eyebrows ticked up a millimeter.

He flowed directly into the follow-up — horizontal cut, waist height — and I retreated, and the retreat *wasn't* panicked scrambling, it was the Ashenveil Retreat's footwork, that strange, economical withdrawal pattern I'd reverse-engineered from his fragmented memories—

*His* memories, technically. Joshua's. Nobody's, anymore.

Step. Step. Pivot. The wooden blade hissed through the air where my ribs used to be.

"Oh?" Lucas said, pleasantly surprised.

Three-hit combo incoming. I knew it like my own name.

Cut. Cut. Rising cut—

I ducked the first, sidestepped the second, and the third I *couldn't* fully evade, so I caught it on my practice sword in a high parry and let the force shove me backward instead of trying to absorb it.

Rhea's voice again: *'You are a wet stick. Never try to be a wall. Be water. Annoying, cowardly water.'*

She hadn't said the cowardly part. That was my own interpretation. She'd probably endorse it.

The impact still rattled my teeth.

My arm vibrated like a struck tuning fork, and a wholly undignified noise escaped me — something between *"hngk"* and *"why."*

But the parry *held*. Directionally. Technically.

Pause.

Here it comes. Overhead strike.

---

Overhead strike. Coming down like the judgment of a just and reasonably athletic god.

I slid left. The wooden blade cratered the floorboards where I'd been standing with a *thunk* I felt in my fillings.

Lateral sweep. I hopped back over it. Landed ugly. Survived.

'I SURVIVED THE FIRST COMBO CYCLE.'

'I, Kang Min-jun, age 23, unemployed, have survived one full rotation of the protagonist's Phase One attack pattern.'

'Someone alert the Academy records office.'

Lucas reset his stance, and he was *smiling*. Not the polite smile from before. A real one. Eyes bright.

"You're not counterattacking at all," he observed.

"That's correct."

"Intentionally?"

"Extremely intentionally."

He laughed and came at me again.

---

The game animations, it turned out, were astonishingly faithful.

I don't know who did the combat design for Eschaton Hearts, but I wanted to find them and shake their hand, because every move Lucas made mapped onto the data in my head with terrifying precision. The rhythm was identical. The spacing was identical. The tiny tells — the way his left shoulder dipped half a centimeter before the lateral sweep, the way he exhaled before the overhead — all of it was *there*.

'Of course it is. The game was built on this world. It didn't invent Lucas. It *documented* him.'

Which meant I was functionally fighting a boss whose patterns I had memorized across sixty hours of attempts.

The catch — and there was a very large catch, roughly the size of a dragon — was that knowing a pattern and physically surviving it are two different games entirely.

My body was the problem. My body was *always* the problem.

A Level 1 body with the mana output of a decorative candle. Bodies, it turns out, have opinions, and my body's opinion after thirty seconds of continuous evasion was: *we're going to die, and I'd like to file that under your failures.*

Lungs burning. Legs complaining. Shoulder muscles sending increasingly formal letters of protest.

But Rhea's morning of training was holding. It was genuinely holding. The breathing pattern she'd taught me — in through the nose on the retreat, sharp out through the teeth on the dodge — was keeping my oxygen up. The parry angles she'd corrected were turning hits I couldn't have blocked last week into hits I could barely block now.

'Barely' was the operative word. Barely was doing a lot of heavy lifting in that sentence.

But barely-alive was still alive. That was the entire manufacturer specification of my build.

One minute down.

---

Around the two-minute mark, Lucas got *curious*.

I could see it happen. His rhythm shifted — still friendly, still controlled, but probing now. He started varying his entries. Feeding the same combo from different angles. Testing whether my dodges were genuine reads or rehearsed luck.

'He's adjusting. The AI is *adapting*.'

'This is not in the script. The game Lucas was a static boss. Static, Lucas! Stay in your lane!'

A diagonal cut, faster than the others.

I bent backward under it — spine creaking a formal objection — and the tip of the wooden blade passed so close over my nose that I felt the wind of it.

Then, because my body had apparently decided we were doing this now, the Ashenveil Retreat footwork carried me *through* the overextension, three gliding steps that put me at his flank—

And I did nothing with it.

I had a clean opening at his ribs. Two full heartbeats of it. Any halfway-trained first-year would have taken the shot.

I just... stood there. Guard up. Sword raised. Watching politely, like a man waiting for a bus.

Lucas disengaged, reset, and gave me a strange look.

"You had that," he said.

"Had what?"

"The flank. My ribs were open for a full second."

"Were they? I didn't notice. Tall guy, big movements, hard to see over the aura of imminent death."

He stared at me.

'WHY DID I SAY THAT. That was not a normal thing to say. Extras do not say that.'

"You have a very unusual sense of humor," Lucas said slowly.

"So I've been told. By teachers. In writing."

He shook his head, smiling, and came at me *faster*.

---

Three minutes.

I did not know there was a gallery audience growing above us. I did not know that word had trickled through the dormitories — *the provisional kid who nuked the crystal is sparring Lucas Ashford* — and that students were drifting in through the side entrances to watch the execution.

I did not know a man in instructor's gray was standing motionless in the shadow of the upper gallery, holding a small journal.

I did not know a girl with iron-straight posture was standing behind a pillar at the arena entrance, having arrived early to check whether the hopeless boy she'd trained yesterday would embarrass himself, and was now watching him not embarrass himself with an expression that was slowly, tectonically *changing*.

I knew none of this.

What I knew was: the world had shrunk to a circle of polished stone, a wooden sword, and the increasingly urgent question of whether my lungs considered this a survivable workload.

Lucas was at maybe forty percent. The thought arrived unbidden, and it was horrifying, because forty percent of the protagonist was reducing me to a wheezing, dodge-spamming panic goblin.

But the dodges were *landing*.

Three-hit combo. Weave. Weave. Sidestep. Overhead. Slide left. Sweep. Hop. Reset. Breathe.

It was almost meditative, in the way that fleeing a burning building is a form of cardio.

Lucas's smile had changed again. The friendly warmth was still there, but underneath it something sharper had woken up. Focus. Real focus.

The look of a man who had just realized the puzzle in front of him was harder than the box promised.

'Oh no. He's having *fun*.'

'Rule one of protagonist behavior: never be entertaining at them. Being entertaining at a protagonist is how extras become *supporting characters*.'

'ABORT. ABORT THE FUN.'

But there was no aborting. There was only the next attack, and the next, and the burning narrowing margin between my reaction speed and his blade—

---

Four minutes in, it happened.

Lucas feinted the overhead — the move I knew, the move I'd dodged forty times tonight alone — and I started the slide left on pure muscle memory, on sixty hours of trained response to that exact animation—

And the overhead never came.

It broke mid-arc. Folded into itself. Converted, impossibly, into a low thrust aimed at the space I was dodging *into*.

A feint-to-thrust combination.

That wasn't in the moveset.

That was NOT in the MOVESET. I had frame-data-level certainty that Lucas Ashford's sparring AI did not contain a feint-to-thrust cancel. That was a *living person* improvising against an actual opponent.

The game had documented him. The game had not documented everything.

I had no rehearsed dodge. My legs were already committed to the slide. My brain sent a desperate update to my body, something in the category of *absolutely do a different thing than this*, and my body, teamwork-oriented as ever, replied: *request denied.*

I twisted anyway.

Rhea's shoulder-roll recovery — the technique she'd spent twenty minutes forcing into me yesterday while I complained like a dying kettle — fired on pure reflex. Down, tuck, rotate, take the blow on the meat of the shoulder instead of the chest—

The wooden blade hit like a swung door.

*THUNK.*

The impact detonated through my left shoulder, and I went down, rolled through it — the roll actually *worked*, I came up on one knee, more from momentum than skill — and sat there on the polished stone, blinking, cataloguing damage.

Shoulder: present, furious, filing for secession.

Everything else: miraculously intact.

Silence in the arena.

Then Lucas lowered his sword.

---

"Four minutes," he said.

His voice carried across the empty floor. Quiet. Genuinely amazed in a way I'd never heard from him in the game, because in the game the voice lines didn't have a setting for *startled respect*.

"I wasn't going easy for that last minute," Lucas continued. "I want you to know that. I adjusted. I feinted. I committed."

'Yes. I noticed. My shoulder noticed. My shoulder has taken detailed notes.'

"You read my attacks like you've fought me a hundred times."

Because I had. On a screen. With a controller. In my underwear, at 3 AM, eating convenience-store kimbap.

"I've studied a lot of swordsmanship," I said. Technically true. Just... in the way an aviation enthusiast has technically studied being a pilot.

Lucas walked over and offered me his hand.

I took it, and he pulled me up like I weighed nothing, which, relative to a protagonist, I functionally did.

He was studying me. Really looking now. And something was happening behind his eyes — some internal calculation reaching a warm and terrible conclusion.

"You never attacked," he said. "Not once. Even when I left openings. Even at my flank."

"Mm."

"You fight like you're trying not to hurt your opponent."

'I fight like a man whose mana index is lower than a decorative training dummy. I fight like my opponent is a weather event. You could stand still with your eyes closed and my full-power strike might muss your hair.'

"That takes more discipline than fighting to win," Lucas said. His voice had gone soft with admiration. "Restraint that complete... I've never seen anything like it."

I almost laughed.

I almost cried.

I settled for nodding, with the grave solemnity of a man accepting an award for an achievement that was actually a medical condition.

"Thank you," I said. "Discipline is... very important to me."

Somewhere, I would have sworn on anything, I heard a goddess snort so hard she choked.

"We should do this again," Lucas said, grinning. "Regularly. You're the most interesting sparring partner I've ever had."

The most dangerous sentence in the English language, delivered in the warmest possible tone.

"I'd love that," said my mouth, which had apparently been bribed by panic.

His grin widened. He clapped me on the shoulder — the *injured* shoulder, because the universe has a comedy writer — gathered his things, and strode out of the arena, whistling.

I watched him go.

Then I sat down on the arena floor, hard, and waited for my heart rate to return to non-emergency levels.

---

I sat there for a long time. Alone. In the morning light.

Icing my shoulder wasn't really possible, so I just sort of... held it. Sympathetically. Like comforting a small angry animal that lived in my joint.

'Four minutes. You lasted four minutes against the protagonist.'

'As a level 1.'

'That's... genuinely absurd. That's a glitch-tier performance.'

'And he thinks you're a master of restraint. He's going to tell people that. People are going to BELIEVE that.'

'Your reputation is now officially unrecoverable. You are no longer a wall. You are a mural.'

That's when the window appeared.

```
[Observation: You survived 4 minutes of sparring with Lucas Ashford.]
[This is equivalent to a Level 1 character surviving 4 minutes against a Level 15 boss.]
[In the game, this achievement is called: 'THE MIRACLE OF COWARDICE.']
```

I laughed once. A short, broken sound.

'The devs really did hide achievements for everything.'

Then the window continued, and I stopped laughing.

```
[Unfortunately, it was witnessed by:]
[1. Instructor Vance (suspicion: ELEVATED)]
[2. Rhea Valtor (emotional investment: ESCALATING)]
[3. Approximately 12 students who 'happened' to be in the gallery]
```

I froze.

'Twelve.'

'There were TWELVE people in the gallery.'

'There were twelve people, and an instructor, and RHEA?'

'Rhea was HERE? When? The whole time? She saw... she saw the shoulder roll. She saw me use the shoulder roll. Twelve hours after she taught it to me. That's supposed to take WEEKS—'

I looked up at the gallery. Empty now. Of course it was empty now. Witnesses to your doom never stick around for the epilogue.

The System had one more thing to say.

```
[By dinner, the entire first-year class will know that the provisional student who breaks crystals and reads ancient languages also survived a 4-minute spar with the top-ranked enrollment candidate.]
[Your 'invisible background character' strategy is performing excellently.]
[Note: That was sarcasm. The Evil Goddess is learning sarcasm from you.]
[She considers this your worst contribution to her existence.]
```

Then, a final line, in a font that felt somehow *pleased*:

```
[Bad End Watch: No new flags detected.]
[But Instructor Vance has opened a file on you.]
[It's a physical file. In a drawer. With your name written on it. Isn't that romantic? ♡]
```

A file. In a drawer. With my name on it.

I stared at the window until it faded.

Then I lay back flat on the arena floor, in a puddle of golden skylight, holding my wrecked shoulder, and addressed the ceiling.

"Four minutes," I said to nobody. "I survive the single hardest fight available to my level bracket, and my reward is a *folder*."

Somewhere far above, the goddess was laughing again.

I could hear her now. Not literally — not the way I used to be able to. But I knew the rhythm of it by heart.

'Fine,' I thought, closing my eyes. 'Fine. File me away, Instructor. File me under D for Doomed.'

'It's where I keep all my plans anyway.'

Restraint-wise, at least, I was apparently a master.

Shame that restraint had never once in my life applied to flags.
