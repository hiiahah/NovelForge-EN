# Chapter 2: Stats of a Practice Dummy

Morning came whether I wanted it to or not.

Rude of it, honestly.

I mean, the sun just *rose*. Like it hadn't watched me almost get murdered twice yesterday. Like it hadn't seen me ride a wagon wheel down a cliff into a frozen river using only a corpse's bloating gases for ballast.

No accountability in this world, I swear.

I limped along the Imperial Highway with my ribs bound in strips torn from what was left of my servant's tunic, which currently looked less like clothing and more like a war crime held together by guilt.

Every step sent a jolt through my chest.

*Left foot. Pain. Right foot. Pain. Left foot. Pain.*

'Fantastic. They've invented a physical version of my life.'

The good news was I'd slept.

The bad news was that "slept" was doing a lot of heavy lifting in that sentence, considering I'd spent the night curled in the hollow of a rotting tree trunk with a rock in my hand just in case anything decided I looked edible.

Nothing did.

Possibly because I smelled like a drowned ant gallbladder smoothie.

Now, I had two priorities.

First, reach the capital.

Second, don't die before reaching the capital.

The third priority — food — had filed a complaint with priorities one and two, but it wasn't my fault. I didn't have any coins. I didn't have any bread. I didn't have a single damn 'Traveler's Ration,' despite how common they were in the game.

Do you know how many of those things I'd looted in my saves? Thousands. There were probably enough Traveler's Rations in my inventory at max level to feed a mid-sized nation.

And now I couldn't even remember what they tasted like. Something like biscotti made of sawdust and broken promises. God, what I wouldn't give for some broken codpieces.

*Focus, you idiot.*

I took a breath. Held it. Winced at the rib crackle.

'Status.'

The blue window unfolded before me, polite as a funeral invitation.

**INT:** 8 *(Suspiciously high for a servant. The System has determined that sarcasm does not count as intelligence, but has logged the discrepancy.)*
**WIS:** 2 *(See: entire previous chapter.)*

**Mana Index:** 0.009

**Skills:**
— [Survivor's Instinct (Passive)]: You perceive danger with uncanny clarity. Side effects include existential dread, insomnia, and the urge to narrate your own death.

**Titles:**
— [Man Who Clipped Through Death]: Unlocked by surviving Prologue Death Flag #1 in a way the System did not anticipate. The System will be adjusting its models.

---

I stared at it.

'This is worse than my college application essay.'

*Suspiciously high for a servant.*

*Sarcasm does not count as intelligence.*

I clenched my jaw and muttered, "Wow. Okay. Judged by my own stat window. Fantastic. I'm speedrunning unemployment in another world."

I swiped the window aside, called it back, and stared at the two numbers that mattered most.

**Mana Index: 0.009.**

**MP: 0/0.**

Ladies and gentlemen, allow me to present the single most important statistic in *Eschaton Hearts*:

Mana.

The lifeblood of Spellblades, Clerics, and Battle Mages. The juice that runs the cute animation of sparkles when you use sword skill 'Crimson Sever' and utterly bisect an armored knight.

The stuff that let the four heroines turn into continental-scale disasters.

The average academy student, by year one standards, had a mana index between 50 and 200.

A trained court mage? Five thousand to twenty thousand.

The Headmaster of Stellaris Royal Academy? Thirty-one thousand four hundred and twelve measured to two decimal points, because of course she was, the old geezer probably had a mana-rich diet and eight hours of uninterrupted sleep.

Me?

I had 0.009.

For the love of god — the academy's wooden training dummies, the actual physical lumps of straw that students beat up for target practice, were *enchanted to 0.01 mana*.

They were given mana *on purpose so they could register hits*.

That means the practice dummy is 0.001 units of mana more alive than me.

'I am measurably, quantifiably, mathematically... less useful than furniture.'

I pressed a hand to my face.

'No. Worse. I am furniture that's broken.'

I flashed back to eleven hours ago, standing in front of a glowing crystal orb in a noble household while the examiner squinted at it and went, "Huh."

I'd been hoping he was squinting because of an unreadable, limitless potential hidden deep within me.

No.

He was squinting because the orb hadn't bothered to light up. Not even a flicker. It just sat there like a paperweight that had given up on its dreams.

The examiner had looked at me like I was a tax form.

"Next."

Just like that. One word. *Next.*

'Yeah. Yeah, I remember. I remember.'

The last horse of the convoy ahead flicked its tail at a fly.

Right. Convoy.

Let me explain.

---

I'd limped along the Imperial Highway for about two hours before a merchant caravan finally rolled past me in a cloud of dust.

Four wagons. Canvas-covered. Packed with casks of grain, bolts of cheap wool, and a disproportionately large amount of what smelled like pickled eel.

The lead driver, a bearded grandpa who looked like an entire bag of walnuts, had looked me over once, sniffed twice, and said:

"You a runaway?"

"No."

"You look like you got hit by a wagon."

"*I* survived being hit by a wagon," I said, which was technically three different lies at the same time.

He stared at me.

I stared at him.

He sighed. "Get in the back. Don't touch the eels."

"Yes, sir."

That was the entire job interview.

And that is how I, Lucen Gray, twenty-seven-year-old secret god-gamer turned seventeen-year-old background NPC, became a hitchhiker in a pickled eel caravan.

I was lying on a lumpy sack of something grain-adjacent, pressed between the warm flank of a donkey and a barrel that kept making ominous gurgling noises.

'This is fine. This is fine. This is everything I've ever dreamed of.'

I stared up at the canvas ceiling and started thinking.

---

Okay.

Assessment time.

My body was a piece of garbage, but I had one thing going for me.

Knowledge.

I knew *Eschaton Hearts*. Not 'played it a few times' knew. Not 'watched a few YouTube walkthroughs' knew.

I *knew* it.

I'd done all forty-nine bad endings. I'd completed every heroine's route, including the hidden true route. I'd farmed the Kindling Knight's manor for the Blackened Knight's Ring. I'd discovered that if you booted the game on the second midnight of the third month and opened the closet in Miss Brunhilda's office wearing the Maid Headband, there was a hidden joke scene where she screamed about her past.

I knew where every grain table was, every treasure chest, every hidden lever, every secret dungeon, every NPC with a side quest, and every dialogue choice that unlocked the 'Legendary Hero' username flair.

Four years. That's how long the academy storyline ran in-game. Four in-game years of content, dialogue trees, seasonal events, midterms, festival arcs, club recruitment, the inter-academy tournament, winter siege, everything.

I had it all locked in here.

I tapped my temple.

And here was the tragic thing.

None of it — *none* of it — was going to work.

Because I'd already verified the bitterest truth of my second life: the game wasn't the game anymore.

Six hundred and thirty-seven bad endings.

I'd seen forty-nine.

I was now, possibly, inside something that *wore* the game's skin but had its own content my previous life never showed me.

Where did the extra five hundred and eighty-eight ways to die come from? Who the hell is writing the script if it's not the devs?

**[The Evil Goddess is watching you think.]**

"Great," I muttered. "Make it weird."

No response from the window. Just implication.

'Fine. Fine. Whatever. The gameplay is different. The file is corrupt. Same difference. Adjust strategy.'

And if there was anything I was good at — anything at all — it was strategy.

So here's the plan. Listen carefully, brain, because you're the only employee I've got left and your job performance has been *spotty* at best.

Step one: **Stay out of the story.**

*Eschaton Hearts* is about Lucas Ashford. Hero of prophecy. Golden boy. Walking heartthrob with hair that probably sparkles in the rain. He's the protagonist. He's got the aura. He's got the secret blessings and the holy sword and the best dialogue options.

Let him do his job.

The world — and by the world I mean the *literal continent* — depends on Lucas Ashford romancing the hell out of those four heroines.

And if he fails?

Oh boy.

If he fumbles the Iris flare-up route in Year 2, Iris collapses, blames her entire existence on humanity, and literally *burns the Imperial capital to ash*.

Bad End. Continental catastrophe. One hundred thousand dead in the opening cinematic.

If he fumbles the Alice event in the Winter Festival arc, she freezes an entire duchy into permafrost. Just... locks it. Forever. Winter Wonderland, population zero.

If he breaks Rhea's trust — and the original event requires him to comfort her in exactly the right way at the bell tower after the Blood Moon chapter — she unsheathes her sword, reverts to her ancestral berserker form, and *carves a two-kingdom gash through the continent like she's cutting a birthday cake.*

And Lyra?

The dragon?

If Lyra decides humanity is unworthy of love, she unseals herself, spreads her wings, and makes the Mooncrest mountain range *flat*.

Those four girls aren't heroines.

They are four morally gray nukes wearing school uniforms.

And I am a man whose job is to make sure no one accidentally bumps the launch button.

Step two: **watch for bad flags. Nudge from the shadows.**

Not actively. Not visibly. Not with dramatic entrances or gripping speeches or 'Actually, it's not what it looks like!' misunderstandings.

Just... subtle nudges.

If I see a flowerpot above a heroine’s head and I know a flag triggers there, I move the flowerpot. Done. That's it. Sneaky custodian work.

Step three: **graduate safely.**

Stellaris Royal Academy is the main setting of the first four years. If I get into the academy — even as a scholarship peasant janitor-adjacent background student — I can keep tabs on things, stay where the events are, monitor the heroines, and, critically, get access to the one thing in this world that guarantees survival:

*A room with locked doors and three square meals a day.*

Step four: after graduating, disappear. Quiet village. Chicken farm. Maybe a dog. A *small* dog, not a dramatic one. Live a boring, peaceful, statistically unlikely-to-die life.

Step five: profit.

*"And if anyone sees you,"* I muttered under my breath, *"stay boring. Always be boring. Be the beige wall of humanity. Be so invisible you have to remind yourself you exist."*

The donkey flicked an ear.

"Don't give me that look, buddy. This is sound strategy."

Yep.

It had to work.

Because if I got attached to anyone — if I got *noticed* — if any heroine even remembered my *name* —

'That's a shortcut to a bad ending, probably with dramatic violin music, rain, the heroine walking away while looking over her shoulder, and me standing there soaking wet with an umbrella I don't realize is upside down—'

I shook my head violently.

'Stop. Stop it. No route flags. No feelings. No anything. Servant boy Lucen does not exist, and more importantly — he never did.'

Which, thinking about it...

I blinked.

'Hold on.'

Wait a second.

No one was *looking* for me.

The noble household I served? They'd registered me dead. My corpse was... well, somewhere on the river, probably nibbled by fish. I'd seen the post-crash damage report in the corner of my vision when I respawned. They'd found the "body." Case closed. Tragic accident. The Ashford heir would probably make a solemn declaration to avenge the little servant boy who, in the original timeline, *died*.

'Which means I have no identity.'

No nobles. No papers. No family. No coin.

The perfect blank slate.

'...That's actually kind of incredible.'

A nobody had the best advantage in the world: no one remembered them.

If I entered Stellaris as a scholarship student — the 'Charities Fund' endowment covered five spot-holders every year, specifically designed for orphaned, promising commoners — then I could rewrite my entire existence.

No one would ask questions. No one would cross-reference a dead boy with no surname from a mid-level noble household.

I grinned.

'This is the best part of being a corpse. Complete creative control over your own afterlife.'

I lay back against the grain sack and stared at the ceiling.

'New life rule. If it breathes and it's a heroine, don't make eye contact. If it doesn't breathe and it's the System, pretend it's a hallucination. If it breathes and it's the protagonist, help silently from afar like a weird raccoon in a dumpster.'

Solid.

Honestly? This could be my best plan yet.

---

Ding.

"..."

I hate it here.

---

We rolled for two more days.

Two days of eel barrels, donkey warmth, bad roads, worse jokes from the merchant grandpa ("Why does the hero never pay tolls? Because he's always taking the *paladin* route!"), and the slow-growing awareness that my ribs were getting better about as fast as my dignity was getting worse.

On the third morning, the convoy crested a large, rising hill.

The sea of trees below suddenly opened.

I raised my head.

There it was.

The valley unfolded beneath us like someone had spilled a jewelry box across it.

Ivory towers.

Crystalline barrier arrays that caught the dawn light and shattered it into moving rainbow ripples. Floating observation platforms hanging mid-air like the gods had simply forgotten to drop them. Bridges of pale stone, gardens in permanent bloom, banners snapping in the wind — blue and silver, the crest of a rising star over a closed book.

Students in uniform crossing courtyards in organized lines.

Birdsong. Magic. Prestige.

Stellaris Royal Academy.

I stared.

I had seen this place in 4K. I'd run across those gardens in-game two hundred times. I'd climbed the East Clocktower to find that awful hidden collectible coin. I'd memorized the cafeteria layout for optimization purposes.

Seeing it in person, huge and real and glittering, was...

'...Beautiful.'

I almost choked on the word.

I glared at the vista like it had personally wronged me.

'Don't you dare make me feel awe right now. I know what you are. I know what sits under you.'

Because I knew.

I knew the academy's chairman had a private vault sealed behind twelve doors.

I knew the 'expansion project' of Year 2 was a lie covering up a foundation flaw.

And I knew — because the true ending route explicitly reveals it at the end of Year 3 — that Stellaris Royal Academy was built directly over a sealed **Abyssal Rift**.

A thin, layered membrane between this world and a place of raw, hungry, reality-eating nothing.

The tutorial zone. The safe zone.

Sitting directly on top of the mother of all ticking time bombs.

'Cute. Just cute. Lofty towers built on a pulsating cosmic ulcer.'

A blue window bloomed in front of my face.

**[System Note: Stellaris Royal Academy — Tutorial Zone.]**

**[Recommended Level: 15+]**

**[Your Level: 1]**

**[Note: The Evil Goddess suggests you turn around.]**

**[Note: The Evil Goddess also suggests you consider the chicken farm early. She says it might be the only way you live to see twenty.]**

"That's... surprisingly constructive criticism," I muttered.

The grandpa merchant looked over his shoulder. "You say something?"

"No," I said. "Just thinking about my future."

"Yeah? Got one?"

I looked down at the valley. At the towers. At the floating platforms. At the place where, within four years, four separate continental disasters would take root if a single boy with ridiculously beautiful hair failed in his studies of the heart.

I exhaled slowly through my nose.

"...Yeah," I said. "I've got one."

I gripped the side of the wagon.

Turned around?

Please.

The plan was already in motion.

Step one: get within those walls.

Step two: be *aggressively* invisible.

Step three: nudge fate from the shadows.

Because there was no way in hell I was turning back.

There was nowhere else to go.

And besides...

I smiled thinly.

'Somebody's got to make sure Lucas doesn't screw this up.'

Toward whatever 'flag' had just been staring at me from the crowd.

'I regret everything.'
