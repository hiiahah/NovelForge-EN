# Chapter 42: Decryption Session #7: Iris Smells a Rival

The door to Iris Aurelia's private study room opened at exactly 19:00.

Seven sessions in, and I had the route memorized like a speedrun segment. West wing, third floor, turn left at the statue of a princess who looked judgmentally at everyone, knock twice, enter when the door opens by itself.

The door opened by itself.

'Showtime.'

The first thing I noticed: no tea.

Every session so far — all six of them — had begun with tea. Some terrifyingly expensive blend brewed by Iris's own hands, served in cups that probably cost more than my dormitory building. The tea was part of the ritual. A calming prop in the psychological warfare that Princess Iris Aurelia called a "study session" and that Kang Min-jun, veteran of every interrogation-type event in Eschaton Hearts, called a boss fight with porcelain.

Tonight, the table was bare.

The second thing I noticed: the temperature.

The room was cold. Not window-open cold. *Controlled* cold. The kind of chill that happened when a fire-attribute mana prodigy was very deliberately not letting her warmth leak into the air.

In the game, there was an ambient-temperature mechanic in Iris's route. The room reflected her emotional state. Warm meant comfortable. Hot meant angry.

Cold meant she was *thinking*.

'Volume off. Lights dimmed. Final boss music loading...'

"You've accepted Rhea Valtor's duel," she said.

I wasn't even fully seated. My rear end was still four centimeters above the chair.

Let me be clear about what Decryption Sessions were.

Officially: study sessions. Iris tutored me in mana theory twice a week, in a private room, with ironclad secrecy, because a princess spending evenings alone with a mana index of 0.009 was the kind of gossip that toppled reputations.

Unofficially: interrogations. Measured, methodical, merciless. Iris Aurelia had decided, roughly twenty days ago, that Lucen Gray was a locked box, and Iris Aurelia did not leave locked boxes unopened. It offended something fundamental in her soul.

Tonight, apparently, she'd decided to skip the lockpicks and go straight for the sledgehammer.

Her voice stayed level. Clinical. "Because from where I'm sitting, Lucen, that isn't a strategy. That's a suicide note with good penmanship."

"She challenged me," I said. "About the Iron Marches. About whether her form's legacy is real or whether it just died with her father's soldiers. I was there. I trained under the techniques. I'm... evidence."

"Hmm." Iris's expression didn't move. "You do realize that makes you a witness, not a combatant. She could have requested testimony. A demonstration. An interview." A pause. "She requested your *blood* on a training floor instead."

"You've reviewed her combat profile, I assume," Iris said.

"You could say that."

I could say a lot more than that. I could say that I, Kang Min-jun, had fought Rhea Valtor as a boss thirty-one times across three playthroughs of Eschaton Hearts. I could say I knew her move frames better than I knew my own birthday. I could say that the reason I stood a microscopic chance in this duel was three hundred and forty-seven hours of game time that nobody in this world could ever know about.

"I said what I said." Iris flicked a finger; the display rotated. "The point is this. She has no weakness in standard technique. None. I've checked. I've had people check. In a standard fight, fought standardly, she doesn't lose to anyone in our year. She might not lose to half of the second-years."

"You're really building up my morale here."

"I'm not finished." The hologram shifted, highlighting something I hadn't seen — a waveform, jagged at the edges, pulsing in amber. "There is one variable. Her emotional state. "Except where the Iron Marches are concerned. Sera's analysis flagged four incidents this term where Valtor's composure degraded. All four involved the legacy of her father's soldiers. She's handed you her only vulnerability, wrapped in ribbon."

"I know."

I knew all of this. I'd watched Sera's invisible pen scratch across Rhea's psychological profile all semester in the game's UI, back when it was just lines of text on a monitor and not the actual psychological profile of an actual person who wanted to hit me with an actual sword.

"But here's the thing," Iris said, and the hologram dissolved with a soft chime, plunging the room back into cold half-light.

"You can't exploit that opening."

"Why not?"

"Because you're emotionally invested too."

Silence.

"I'm not—"

"You train with her at dawn." Iris's tone didn't change. She recited it the way she'd recited Sera's report time. Flat. Precise. Filing evidence. "Three to four mornings a week, for the past five weeks. You've integrated her techniques into your fighting style — Sera's footage shows Iron March forms in your sparring from two weeks ago. Synchronized breathing, mirrored patterns."

"That's called *having a training partner.*"

"When she's mentioned in conversation, your body language changes." Still that clinical recitation. Still that neutral face, porcelain-perfect and unreadable. "Your shoulders drop approximately two centimeters. Your breathing slows. Your blink rate decreases. Sera has charted it."

"Your bodyguard is charting my *breathing*?"

"Sera charts everyone's breathing. Yours simply does interesting things." Iris tilted her head — half a degree, the curiosity of a cat studying a mouse hole. "You're comfortable around her, Lucen. You are broadly, reliably, almost pathologically *uncomfortable* around everyone else at this academy. I've watched you navigate Stellaris for a month like a man defusing a bomb that occasionally asks him to share notes. But when Rhea Valtor enters your field of vision, you relax."

My mouth opened.

Nothing came out.

Because — damn it. Damn it, she wasn't *wrong*. Rhea was the only person in this whole academy whose every possible attack pattern I already knew. Training with her was the closest thing I had to a safe zone. Fighting-game logic: the most predictable boss is the most comfortable boss. She was the one final boss in this cursed world whose patch notes I'd already memorized.

But how did I say that out loud without sounding insane *or* sentimental?

"That's a tactical assessment, not an emotional one," I managed.

"Mm." Iris's eyes didn't leave mine. "Is it?"

The silence stretched.

Then she asked:

"Are you comfortable around me?"

My mouth, tired and stupid and four days from a duel I'd probably lose, said the truth instead.

The session wound down past ten.

We drilled theory until my eyes crossed. Iris identified three of my habitual openings — a shoulder dip before I broke left, a half-beat pause after feint number two, a rhythmic exhale she called "your tell that even I find offensive" — and sketched counters for each. Grudgingly. Punctuated by long silences where she stared at nothing and her pen stopped moving.

She was, I realized, doing the thing I knew from thirty-plus hours of her route.

She was *over-investing* in a problem. Iris Aurelia could not encounter an unsolved equation without solving it. She couldn't encounter a cracked wall without either repairing it or demolishing it. And somewhere in the past twenty days, her vast, terrible, princess-grade problem-solving machine had classified *Lucen Gray* as hers to work on.

The game had a term for that state.

The players had a term for it too, but it was less polite.

I packed up my notes — actual notes now, three pages of Iris's ruthless tactical marginalia — and headed for the door.

I was halfway through it when she spoke.

"Lucen."

"If you tell her the truth," Iris said. "Whatever it is you're hiding — because you *are* hiding something, we both know it, we've danced around it for seven sessions—" Her voice stayed level. Level. Perfectly, structurally level, the way a frozen lake is flat right up until the ice goes. "If you tell her the truth... will she still look at you the same way?"

The question sat in the doorway with me.

Because it wasn't really about Rhea. Even I, with my dodging stats maxed and my emotional intelligence running at roughly slime-tier, understood that much. The pronoun was doing a lot of work in that sentence. *Her.* The way *she* looks at you. And underneath it, almost inaudible under the princess diction, a different question entirely, one Iris Aurelia would bite through her own tongue before saying out loud.

I thought about it.

About Rhea, standing in the frost four mornings from now, looking at me for proof that her father's soldiers hadn't died for nothing. Behind the door, Iris Aurelia did not move for eleven minutes.

Her notes lay perfect on the table. Her schematic glowed violet in the dim air. Her tea — the cup she'd never poured tonight because she'd been too occupied arranging her composure — sat empty and dry at her elbow like a small porcelain accusation.

The crack reached the edge of the table and stopped, and the room kept its silence, and outside the doors of Stellaris Royal Academy, four days burned down to embers.
