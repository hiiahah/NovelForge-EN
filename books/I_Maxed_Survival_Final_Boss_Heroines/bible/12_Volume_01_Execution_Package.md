### Chapters 1–10 — Story Days 1–5

**Stage 1 Thesis:** Lucen wakes inside the game, survives the death he was scripted to suffer, enrolls in Stellaris Royal Academy under false pretenses, and immediately lands on the radar of the single most dangerous person in the empire: First Princess Iris Aurelia. ### CHAPTER 1 — "The Worst Possible Save File"

**Story Day:** Day 0 (Prologue Night — the night before the Academy Entrance Ceremony)

**POV:** Lucen Gray (Kang Min-jun) — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; unnamed bandits (×4); the voice of the Evil Goddess System

**Dramatic Objective:** Establish the transmigration, the horror of the situation, and the comedic survival tone in a single breathless chapter. The reader must understand three things by the final line: (1) this world will kill him, (2) he knows everything about it, (3) everything he knows says he's already dead.

**Central Misunderstanding:** None yet — pure survival setup.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook (first 3 paragraphs):**
Kang Min-jun's internal monologue: he's staring at a moonlit forest clearing. His body is small, malnourished, cold. His hands are shaking. He recognizes the silver birch trees, the broken merchant wagon, the spilled wine casks. This is the opening cutscene of *Eschaton Hearts* — specifically, the roadside bandit ambush that kills the unnamed NPC "Gray Servant Boy" to establish the cruelty of the world before the real protagonist appears.

He is the Gray Servant Boy.

He has approximately ninety seconds to live.

**Rising Tension:**
Min-jun's internal speedrunner brain activates. He rattles off what he knows: four bandits, iron-tier weapons, a Level 3 encounter designed to be unwinnable for the NPC. The merchant he was escorting is already dead behind the overturned wagon. In the game, the boy's corpse is found by Lucas Ashford the next morning, motivating Lucas's enrollment oath. Lucen is a narrative prop. A corpse with a purpose.

A translucent blue window appears:

```
[System: Welcome, Player.]
[You have been loaded into ESCHATON HEARTS — Story Mode.]
[Current Identity: Lucen Gray, Servant Boy. Age 16.]
[Status: A person who should not exist.]
[Mana Index: 0.009 / 100,000]
[All Stats: F]
[Luck: —]
[Note: An Evil Goddess warns you that you are already dead. Try not to make it worse.]
```

He doesn't have stats. He has a eulogy.

**Climax:**
The bandits close in. Min-jun screams internally, scanning for any survival option. He remembers: in speedruns, there's a bugged terrain clip behind the wagon where the collision mesh doesn't load properly because the devs never expected the NPC to move from his scripted death position. He throws himself backward, crashes through the gap between the wagon wheel and a boulder, and tumbles down a steep ravine the bandits can't follow — exactly the geometry skip speedrunners use to reach the Whispering Caves early.

He lands in a freezing stream, breaks two ribs on a rock, and nearly drowns in eighteen inches of water.

But he's alive.

**Cliffhanger:**
As he drags himself onto the bank, coughing blood, a new System window:

```
[Congratulations. You have survived Prologue Death Flag #1.]
[Bad End No. 1: 'A Nameless Grave' — AVERTED.]
[Warning: 637 potential Bad Endings remain.]
[Note: The Evil Goddess thinks you should have stayed dead. It would have been kinder.]
```

He stares at the message. Six hundred and thirty-seven.

He completed every route in 347 hours. He's seen maybe forty bad endings total. Where are the other five hundred and ninety-seven?

**Named Bad Ending:** [Bad End No. 1: A Nameless Grave] — averted.

### CHAPTER 2 — "Stats of a Practice Dummy"

**Story Day:** Day 1 (Morning — Road to Stellaris Royal Academy)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; nameless merchant convoy travelers; System voice

**Dramatic Objective:** Establish Lucen's physical weakness, his encyclopedic game knowledge, and his survival strategy: be invisible, avoid all heroine routes, protect Lucas Ashford's story progression, and survive until graduation without triggering any flags. Introduce the concept that he's replacing his own corpse in the timeline — no one will come looking for a dead servant boy, which means he has no identity, no money, no backing, and no enrollment papers.

**Central Misunderstanding:** None yet — internal monologue chapter.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Dawn. Lucen limps along the Imperial Highway toward the capital, ribs bound with strips torn from his servant tunic. He pulls up his full status screen and narrates it for the reader with the energy of a man reading his own autopsy report.

His mana index of 0.009 is elaborated: the average academy student has 50–200. A trained court mage has 5,000–20,000. The academy's wooden training dummies are enchanted to 0.01 to register hits. He is, in measurable magical terms, less reactive than furniture.

**Rising Tension:**
He outlines his plan. *Eschaton Hearts* has four main heroine routes and one hidden true route. Each heroine has a "Bad End" cascade that triggers if her emotional state destabilizes — meaning if the protagonist (Lucas Ashford) fails to romance her correctly, she becomes a continental-scale disaster. Iris burns the capital. Alice freezes an entire duchy. Rhea carves a kingdom in half. Lyra reverts to dragon form and levels a mountain range.

His job, as he sees it, is simple: stay out of the story. Let Lucas do his job. Nudge things from the shadows if a bad flag appears. Graduate. Find a quiet village. Raise chickens.

He hitches a ride with a merchant convoy heading to the capital, pretending to be a scholarship applicant.

**Climax:**
He reviews the one asset he has: perfect knowledge of the game's events, characters, hidden items, and quest triggers for roughly the first four in-game years (the span of the academy storyline). But the System has already shown him 637 bad endings he never encountered. The game has changed. Or he's inside something larger than the game.

He decides this changes nothing. The plan is still: be invisible.

**Cliffhanger:**
The convoy crests a hill. Below, Stellaris Royal Academy sprawls across a valley — ivory towers, crystalline barriers, floating observation platforms. It's beautiful. It's also, Lucen knows, sitting on top of a sealed Abyssal Rift that will crack open in Year 3 if no one stops it.

```
[System Note: Stellaris Royal Academy — Tutorial Zone.]
[Recommended Level: 15+]
[Your Level: 1]
[Note: The Evil Goddess suggests you turn around.]
```

He doesn't turn around. There's nowhere else to go.

**Named Bad Ending:** None directly referenced. Foreshadowing of multiple future catastrophes.

### CHAPTER 3 — "Enrollment by Clerical Error"

**Story Day:** Day 1 (Afternoon — Academy Registration Hall)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; Registrar Helmond (fussy elderly clerk); two unnamed guards; background noble students

**Dramatic Objective:** Lucen must enroll without identification papers, noble backing, or any detectable mana. This should be impossible. He succeeds through an exploit he remembers from the game — a bureaucratic loophole in the Imperial Education Charter that allows "exceptional circumstances" enrollment if a faculty member sponsors the applicant. But the faculty member who sponsors him does so by accident, and it's the worst possible person.

**Central Misunderstanding:** Registrar Helmond interprets Lucen's desperate, sweat-drenched insistence on enrolling as the quiet determination of a war orphan too proud to beg. Helmond is moved to tears.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Lucen stands in a marble hall surrounded by noble children in silk uniforms. He is wearing a torn servant's tunic held together with river mud. A girl in the line behind him is literally wearing a tiara. He has never felt more like a bug at a banquet.

**Rising Tension:**
The registration process requires: (1) proof of identity, (2) a mana index reading above 1.0, and (3) a letter of recommendation from a recognized noble house or institution. Lucen has none of these. He knew this. His plan was to invoke Clause 77-B of the Imperial Education Charter: "In times of extraordinary peril or social disruption, the Academy Headmaster or any tenured faculty may grant provisional enrollment to individuals of demonstrated merit, pending formal documentation within one academic term."

The clause was included 200 years ago after a civil war and has been used exactly twice. In the game, Lucas Ashford uses it — sponsored by Professor Merith after Lucas saves her from a carriage accident on the road. That event happens canonically today. Lucen planned to find a different faculty member.

He can't find one. Every professor is busy with orientation preparations. He's running out of time — registration closes at sunset.

He tries Registrar Helmond directly, explaining he lost his papers in a bandit attack (true). Helmond is sympathetic but firm: no papers, no enrollment.

**Climax:**
In desperation, Lucen asks if anyone — anyone at all — can invoke Clause 77-B. Helmond sighs and says only tenured faculty. At that exact moment, a voice behind Lucen says: "I'll sponsor him."

Lucen turns.

It's not a professor.

It's Vice-Headmistress Elara Voss — a character who, in the game, is a hidden antagonist working for the Church of Ending. She won't reveal her true allegiance until Year 3. Right now she's the academy's most beloved administrator: kind, maternal, silver-haired, impeccable reputation.

She's smiling at him warmly.

Lucen's internal monologue: a wall of pure static terror. This woman orchestrates the Abyssal Rift opening. She has no reason to notice a servant boy. Unless she's already noticed something wrong with the timeline.

But he can't refuse. Refusing would be more suspicious than accepting.

**Cliffhanger:**
He accepts. She signs his provisional enrollment form with elegant script. As she hands it back, her fingers brush his, and she says, "You have very interesting eyes, Lucen Gray. I look forward to seeing what you become."

```
[System Warning: Vice-Headmistress Elara Voss has taken an interest in you.]
[This was not supposed to happen.]
[Bad End No. 7: 'The Shepherd's Lamb' — Flag raised.]
[Note: The Evil Goddess is screaming.]
```

He hasn't been enrolled for thirty seconds and he already has a bad ending flag.

**Named Bad Ending:** [Bad End No. 7: The Shepherd's Lamb] — flag raised (not triggered).

### CHAPTER 4 — "Seat 7-F, Right Side, Window"

**Story Day:** Day 2 (Morning — First Day of Classes, Homeroom)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; Instructor Kaelin (homeroom teacher, retired knight); 38 students including background nobles; Lucas Ashford (first appearance); Iris Aurelia (first appearance — she arrives late)

**Dramatic Objective:** Introduce the classroom setting, Lucas Ashford as the golden protagonist, and — in the chapter's final moments — the arrival of Iris Aurelia. Lucen must secure the most inconspicuous seat in the room (7-F, back right, window — a position he knows from the game receives zero narrative focus). He succeeds. Then Iris changes everything.

**Central Misunderstanding:** Lucas Ashford, who canonically discovers the dead servant boy's body on the road and swears his enrollment oath over it, notices Lucen — a gaunt, bruised servant-class boy — and feels an inexplicable sense of recognition and guilt. Lucas doesn't know why. Lucen knows exactly why: he's looking at his own living corpse.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Lucen describes the classroom: tiered lecture hall, enchanted blackboard, a window that overlooks the Starfall Courtyard. He's wearing the academy's standard uniform — plain black with no house crest, marking him as a provisional student. Every other student has silk-trimmed crests. He looks like a funeral attendee who wandered into a fashion show.

He secures Seat 7-F. In the game, this seat belongs to a background NPC named "Student F" who has no lines, no events, and no plot relevance for the entire four-year storyline. Perfect.

**Rising Tension:**
Instructor Kaelin begins attendance. Lucen mentally catalogs every student, flagging the ones he recognizes from the game. Most are irrelevant. A few are minor antagonists (Baron Holt's son, the bully who harasses Lucas in Chapter 3 of the original game). One is—

"Lucas Ashford."

A young man stands. Sun-gold hair, honest blue eyes, the physique of someone who's been training with a sword since childhood. He radiates protagonist energy so intensely that Lucen half-expects background music to start playing. Lucas smiles at the class, open and warm.

Lucen sinks lower in his chair. That's the guy. That's the hero who's supposed to save the world, romance the heroines, and prevent five continental apocalypses. Lucen's entire survival plan depends on this man being competent.

Lucas catches Lucen's eye across the room. His expression flickers — a strange, pained confusion. He starts to say something.

Lucen looks away instantly, pretending to study his empty desk.

**Climax:**
The classroom door opens twenty minutes into Kaelin's lecture. Everyone falls silent.

Iris Aurelia enters.

Lucen describes her the way a prey animal describes a predator: copper-red hair cascading past her shoulders, molten-gold eyes that evaluate everything they touch, posture so perfect it's aggressive. She's wearing the standard uniform but it looks like armor on her. She is sixteen years old, first in line for the Imperial Throne, and she has personally executed two traitors to the Crown already.

In the game, Iris's Route is Route 4 — the hardest, the most dangerous, the one with the most Bad Endings. Her love is a wildfire. Her Bad End (No. 47: Sunrise Eternal) turns the capital into glass.

She scans the room. Her gaze passes over Lucas (a flicker of bored recognition — she knows every noble heir by file). It passes over the other students.

It stops on Lucen.

He's in Seat 7-F. The invisible seat. The nothing seat.

She stares at him for three full seconds.

Then she walks to her assigned seat (Row 1, center — of course) and sits without a word.

**Cliffhanger:**
Lucen's hands are shaking under his desk.

```
[System Alert: First Princess Iris Aurelia has noticed you.]
[Reason: Unknown.]
[Bad End No. 12: 'The Tyrant's Curiosity' — Proximity flag initialized.]
[Note: The Evil Goddess says — and this is a direct quote — 'You absolute moron, what did you DO?']
```

He didn't do anything. He sat in a chair.

That's apparently enough.

**Named Bad Ending:** [Bad End No. 12: The Tyrant's Curiosity] — proximity flag initialized.

### CHAPTER 5 — "The Protagonist Does Protagonist Things"

**Story Day:** Day 2 (Afternoon — Outdoor Training Grounds)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; Lucas Ashford; Instructor Kaelin; Baron Holt's son (Derrick Holt, bully); 38 classmates; unnamed training ground staff

**Dramatic Objective:** The first practical assessment: basic mana circulation and physical fitness. Lucen must score low enough to avoid all attention but high enough to not be expelled. Meanwhile, Lucas Ashford naturally dominates the assessment, and the canonical "bully confrontation" scene plays out — Derrick Holt mocks a commoner student, Lucas intervenes heroically. Lucen observes from the back, relieved that the story is progressing normally.

**Central Misunderstanding:** During the mana circulation test, Lucen's reading is so impossibly low (0.009) that the measuring crystal cracks — not from power, but from attempting to register a value below its minimum threshold. Instructor Kaelin and several students interpret the cracked crystal as evidence of Lucen suppressing an enormous mana pool. (In their experience, crystals only break from too *much* power, not too little.)

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
The training grounds: an enormous outdoor arena ringed by enchanted barriers. Students line up for basic assessments. Lucen stands at the very back of the line, behind a boy so large he functions as a human shield. The plan: score mediocre, be forgotten.

**Rising Tension:**
Students step forward one by one, placing their hands on a mana-measuring crystal. Average readings range from 50 to 180. Lucas Ashford scores 312 — jaws drop, Kaelin nods approvingly. This is the protagonist's opening stat showcase, and Lucen watches with genuine admiration. Good. Be strong, Lucas. Be strong so I don't have to be.

The Derrick Holt bully scene unfolds perfectly: Derrick shoves a commoner girl, Lucas steps in with a quiet speech about honor, Derrick backs down. Classic protagonist moment. Lucen is almost emotional. The plot is on track.

Then it's Lucen's turn at the crystal.

**Climax:**
He places his hand on the measuring crystal, hoping for a quiet, forgettable "F-rank: negligible" reading. The crystal hums. Flickers. The internal light stutters like a dying candle.

Then it cracks. A clean fracture down the center.

Silence.

Kaelin stares at the broken crystal. "That crystal is rated to measure up to 50,000 mana index," he says slowly. "The last time one broke was when Archmage Solren visited the academy. Fifteen years ago."

Lucen opens his mouth to explain that his mana is actually zero, that the crystal broke from underflow, that—

"Clearly you have some... unique circumstances, Mr. Gray," Kaelin says, pulling the crystal away with careful hands. "We'll arrange a private re-assessment."

Every student is staring at him.

Including, from across the training grounds where she's conducting her own assessment with the advanced class, Iris Aurelia.

Her molten-gold eyes are fixed on him like a hawk that just spotted movement in the grass.

**Cliffhanger:**
Lucen retreats to the back of the group, sweating so badly his uniform is damp. He pulls up the System.

```
[System Note: Your mana index of 0.009 caused an integer underflow error in the Crystal of Measurement.]
[The crystal interpreted your reading as: OVERFLOW — VALUE EXCEEDS MAXIMUM.]
[This error cannot be corrected without revealing the System's existence.]
[Congratulations: Everyone now thinks you're hiding the mana pool of an Archmage.]
[Bad End No. 12: 'The Tyrant's Curiosity' — Flag escalated from PROXIMITY to ACTIVE.]
[Note: The Evil Goddess has no words. Just a long, disappointed sigh.]
```

He doesn't have the mana pool of an archmage. He has the mana pool of a moderately enchanted dinner plate.

But now Iris Aurelia thinks otherwise.

**Named Bad Ending:** [Bad End No. 12: The Tyrant's Curiosity] — escalated to ACTIVE.

### CHAPTER 6 — "The Princess Asks a Question"

**Story Day:** Day 3 (Morning — Academy Hallway & Courtyard)

**POV:** Iris Aurelia — Third Person Limited (First Heroine Interlude)

**Characters Present:** Iris Aurelia; her personal attendant Sera; two Imperial Guard shadows (unnamed); Lucen Gray (brief encounter at end)

**Dramatic Objective:** The first POV shift to a heroine. Establish Iris's inner world: her crushing loneliness, her weaponized intellect, her exhaustion from being treated as either a political asset or a ticking bomb. She's been raised to read people like documents. Lucen Gray is a document she can't read, and it's driving her insane. This chapter reframes every scene the reader has witnessed through Lucen's panicked perspective — from Iris's side, his behavior looks completely different.

**Central Misunderstanding:** From Iris's perspective: a boy with no background, no family, no records, a mana reading that shattered a military-grade crystal, who sits in the most strategically invisible seat in the classroom (she knows this because she mapped every sightline on Day 1), and who looked directly at her once — then deliberately looked away. In her entire life, no one has ever looked away from her by choice. He's either the most dangerous operative she's ever encountered, or the most fascinating enigma. Possibly both.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Iris in her private quarters at dawn, reviewing Lucen's enrollment file. It's almost empty. Provisional enrollment under Clause 77-B, sponsored by Vice-Headmistress Voss. Bandit attack survivor. No family records. No mana certification. The file is so thin it's suspicious.

Her attendant Sera suggests he's probably just a lucky commoner. Iris says nothing. She's already cross-referenced his enrollment date with Imperial road patrol reports. There were no bandit attacks reported on that road in the last month.

**Rising Tension:**
Iris's morning routine reveals her world: she walks through the academy and every student either bows or flinches. She hears the whispers — *the Dawn Tyrant, don't meet her eyes, she executed Lord Castellan's son last spring.* (He was a traitor smuggling weapons to insurgents. She was twelve. She didn't enjoy it. No one asks if she enjoyed it.)

She attends a private meeting with Vice-Headmistress Voss and probes, casually, about Lucen's sponsorship. Voss is charming, deflecting, generous with details that reveal nothing. Iris notes this. Voss never deflects without reason.

She reviews the cracked crystal report. Speaks with Instructor Kaelin privately. His professional assessment: "Either the boy has mana reserves beyond our measurement capacity, or something about his constitution is fundamentally anomalous. Either way, I've never seen anything like it."

**Climax:**
After morning classes, Iris takes a deliberate route through the eastern courtyard — the path to the provisional student housing. She's calculated that Lucen walks this way to avoid the main thoroughfares.

She's correct. He's there, walking quickly with his head down, clutching a worn leather satchel.

She steps into his path.

He stops. Looks up. His eyes widen in what she catalogs as genuine fear — but not the kind most people show her. Most people fear her power, her title. His fear is... specific. As if he knows something about her that she hasn't shown anyone.

"Lucen Gray," she says. "You're the provisional student."

"...Yes, Your Highness."

"Your crystal broke."

"It was... a malfunction, I think."

"Crystals don't malfunction."

A pause. He doesn't stammer, doesn't grovel, doesn't try to impress her. He just stands there, terrified, and says: "Then I don't have an explanation, Your Highness. I'm sorry."

He's sorry. For breaking a crystal. As if that's the offense.

She stares at him for a long moment. His eyes are gray, unremarkable — except that they don't slide away from hers. He's afraid, clearly. But he's holding eye contact. The last person who held eye contact with her while visibly afraid was her father, the Emperor, and he was telling her that her mother had died.

**Cliffhanger:**
She says: "I'll be watching you, Lucen Gray."

She turns and walks away before he can respond. Her heart is beating slightly faster than normal. She attributes this to the morning chill.

Behind her, she doesn't see Lucen slump against a wall, slide to the ground, and begin quietly hyperventilating.

From Iris's perspective, the chapter ends on: *Interesting.*

**Named Bad Ending:** Reference to [Bad End No. 47: Sunrise Eternal] through Iris's internal thoughts — she reflects on the pressure she's under from the Imperial Court, and the reader glimpses the edges of the rage that, without Lucas's intervention in her route, will one day level the capital.

### CHAPTER 7 — "The Evil Goddess Has Opinions"

**Story Day:** Day 3 (Afternoon — Lucen's Dormitory Room)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; System/Evil Goddess voice; brief appearance by dormitory neighbor Finn (comic relief commoner student)

**Dramatic Objective:** Worldbuilding and strategic planning chapter. Lucen reviews his situation, catalogs the flags he's already triggered, and formulates his survival plan for the first semester. The System/Evil Goddess provides snarky commentary throughout, establishing her as a persistent narrative voice. Introduce Lucen's Hangul notebook — his private strategy guide written in Korean (a language that doesn't exist in this world), containing his accumulated game knowledge: heroine schedules, event triggers, item locations, bad ending conditions.

**Central Misunderstanding:** Lucen's dormitory neighbor Finn sees him furiously writing in an incomprehensible script and assumes he's some kind of genius scholar working in an ancient language. Finn tells three other students. By dinner, the rumor mill says Lucen Gray can read Pre-Collapse Runic Script (a lost language only Archmage-tier scholars can decipher).

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Lucen lies on his dormitory bed — a narrow cot in the provisional student wing, which is essentially a converted storage closet. He's staring at the ceiling. He has survived three days. He has already broken a measuring crystal, attracted the attention of a future tyrant, been sponsored by a hidden villain, and somehow convinced an entire class that he's an undercover archmage.

He was supposed to be invisible.

```
[Note: The Evil Goddess would like to remind you that 'invisible' typically means people do NOT stare at you in mute awe.]
```

**Rising Tension:**
He pulls out a leather journal he bought with his last copper coins and begins writing. In Korean. Hangul characters, neat rows, organized by category. Heroine schedules for the first semester. Lucas Ashford's canonical event timeline. Locations of hidden items and stat-boosting resources he might be able to use (most require mana he doesn't have, but a few are physical-attribute items). Known bad ending triggers for each heroine.

He writes for hours. This notebook is his lifeline — the only advantage he has over this world.

He explains the Evil Goddess system to the reader: she's not a standard game system. She has personality. She has *opinions*. She appears to be some kind of monitoring intelligence bound to him specifically, and she's deeply pessimistic about his survival chances. Her warnings are genuine but unhelpful, like a backseat driver who only speaks up to tell you the bridge is out after you're already on it.

```
[Evil Goddess Note: I can hear you narrating about me.]
[My warnings are EXTREMELY helpful.]
[You simply lack the survival instincts to act on them.]
[For example: Right now, you are writing every strategic secret you possess in a PHYSICAL NOTEBOOK that can be STOLEN.]
[What happens when someone takes it, Min-jun?]
```

Lucen pauses. She's... not wrong. But no one in this world can read Korean. It's an alien language. The notebook is perfectly encrypted.

```
[Note: Famous last words.]
```

**Climax:**
Finn, his neighbor, knocks on the door to invite him to dinner. Lucen shoves the notebook under his pillow but not before Finn catches a glimpse of the Hangul characters. Finn's eyes go wide. "Is that... Pre-Collapse Runic? My grandfather was a linguist and he had a fragment of a Pre-Collapse tablet. The characters look similar—"

"It's just personal shorthand," Lucen says quickly.

Finn nods, clearly not believing him. "Right. Personal shorthand. That you invented. That looks like a lost magical language. Sure."

Lucen can hear the rumor forming in real-time.

**Cliffhanger:**
At dinner in the communal hall, Lucen sits in the farthest corner. He overhears a conversation two tables away: "—heard the provisional student reads Pre-Collapse Runic—" "—the one who broke the measuring crystal—" "—Vice-Headmistress Voss personally sponsored him—"

He's building a legend he never wanted.

And across the hall, in the section reserved for the Imperial students, Iris Aurelia is eating alone. She appears to be reading a book.

But Lucen can see her eyes. They're not moving across the page.

She's watching him.

```
[Bad End No. 12: 'The Tyrant's Curiosity' — Interest level: CLIMBING.]
[Note: Eat faster.]
```

**Named Bad Ending:** [Bad End No. 12: The Tyrant's Curiosity] — sustained active flag.

### CHAPTER 8 — "Lucas Ashford Is Annoyingly Perfect"

**Story Day:** Day 4 (Morning — Joint Combat Basics Class)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; Lucas Ashford; Instructor Vance (combat instructor, scarred veteran); Rhea Valtor (first appearance, background only); 38 classmates; Derrick Holt

**Dramatic Objective:** First combat class. Students are paired for basic sparring. Lucen must demonstrate enough competence to avoid expulsion-level failure while remaining unremarkable. Lucas Ashford dominates the session, cementing his protagonist status. Rhea Valtor (the Ashen Sword) appears in the background as a transfer student who demolishes every sparring partner without expression. And Lucen — through pure game knowledge of combat patterns — accidentally demonstrates a defensive stance that shouldn't exist yet.

**Central Misunderstanding:** Lucen uses a defensive footwork pattern he remembers from the game's advanced combat tutorial (unlocked at Level 40+). He doesn't realize that in-world, this is the Ashenveil Retreat — a military technique known only to the disbanded Seventh Legion, which was destroyed twenty years ago under mysterious circumstances. Instructor Vance, a Seventh Legion survivor, recognizes it instantly.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Outdoor sparring ring. Wooden practice swords. Lucen is praying to be paired with someone gentle. The system pairs him with a stocky noble boy named Erik who looks like he's been sword-training since infancy.

Lucen internally reviews his assets: zero mana, a body that weighs maybe 55 kilograms soaking wet, and 347 hours of muscle-memory knowledge about how video game characters move. He has never held a real sword in his life. Min-jun's pre-transmigration body was a 23-year-old office worker whose primary exercise was reaching for energy drinks.

**Rising Tension:**
Lucas Ashford spars first as demonstration. He's beautiful to watch — economical, powerful, generous with his defeated opponents. Pure protagonist material. Lucen relaxes slightly. Good, the story's working.

Rhea Valtor spars next. The room goes quiet. She's a tall girl with ash-gray hair, eyes like cold steel, moving with a precision that makes Lucas look sloppy by comparison. She defeats her opponent in one strike. No one applauds. Everyone's afraid.

Lucen recognizes her from the game immediately: Route 3 heroine. The Ashen Sword. Daughter of a disgraced general. Her bad end (No. 34: The Severed Crown) involves her assassinating the Emperor and triggering civil war. She's the most technically lethal heroine in the cast.

He needs to stay far away from her.

**Climax:**
Lucen's turn. Erik advances aggressively. Lucen panics and his body moves on pure reflex — not real combat experience, but the dodge patterns he executed thousands of times with a controller. Step back at forty-five degrees, pivot the back foot, let the blow pass the shoulder, reset center.

She extends her hand.

He takes it. She pulls him up.

"You don't know, do you," she says quietly, pitched for his ears only. "You don't actually know who taught you."

He looks at her. This is the closest to the truth he can offer.

"No," he says. "I don't."

It's not a lie. He doesn't know who taught him. Because no one did. A machine showed him, through a screen, in another life.

Rhea nods. Something in her resolves — not satisfaction, not closure, but... acceptance. A decision to live with the mystery instead of being consumed by it.

"I accept your silence," she says. "As promised."

She turns to the audience. Bows. Walks off the floor.

The audience erupts in applause, but Lucen barely hears it. He's watching Rhea's back as she walks away, and his chest hurts with a pain that has nothing to do with the hit he took.

He wanted to give her the answer. He would have given anything to tell her: *your father's soldiers lived. Someone carried the flame.*

But the truth is worse than no answer. The truth is: a boy played a video game and pressed buttons and your father's legacy was a gameplay mechanic he memorized for speedrun efficiency.

Some truths are kinder left unsaid.

### CHAPTER 48 — "After the Duel: Four Conversations"

**Story Day:** Day 34 (Sunday Night — Shadow Banquet, Post-Duel)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; Iris (Conversation 1); Alice (Conversation 2); Lucas (Conversation 3); Lyra (Conversation 4)

**Dramatic Objective:** Post-duel emotional processing through four rapid conversations. Each character finds Lucen in a different part of the banquet hall and shares a moment with him. The conversations overlap in timing — he's moving between groups, and each heroine's conversation reveals something essential about her feelings and about the shape of the story to come.

**Central Misunderstanding:** Each person interprets the duel differently:

Iris: "You stood still and let her hit you. That's either surrender or the most sophisticated form of combat communication I've ever witnessed." (It was both.)

Alice: "You lost on purpose. Because winning would have hurt her." (Half right.)

Lucas: "You fought with honor. That matters more than the outcome." (Classic protagonist read.)

Lyra: "You showed her nothing. That was the bravest thing." (She understands the Empty Stance instinctively — dragons don't need techniques. They just *are*.)

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Post-duel. The banquet resumes. Lucen is bruised and limping slightly but still on his feet. He navigates the room, heading for a quiet corner to breathe.

He doesn't get a quiet corner.

**Conversation 1 — Iris (Balcony):**
Iris intercepts him at the eastern balcony. Night air. Twin moons.

"You wore my cravat," she says.

"I wore everyone's gifts."

"Yes." A pause. "But you wore mine closest to your throat."

This is technically true and also the most Iris way possible to express romantic territory. The cravat is around his NECK. She literally has him by the throat, symbolically.

She stands beside him at the railing. Their shoulders almost touch. "You fight like you're already dead," she says. "You said that once. I believe it now."

"Is that a compliment?"

"It's the saddest thing I've ever seen. And the most beautiful."

She walks away. She doesn't look back.

**Conversation 2 — Alice (Dessert Table):**
Alice finds him restocking on cider. She's been crying — happy tears, she insists. She hugs him. Her body is cold. His body is warm. The temperature differential is noticeable.

"You were amazing," she says into his chest.

"I lost."

"You were AMAZING."

She pulls back and looks at him. "After tonight, can I ask you something?"

"You can always ask."

"The thing inside me. The cold thing. You said you could feel it."

"Yes."

"Does it scare you?"

He thinks about this. Min-jun, the video game player, was terrified of Alice's entity. Lucen, who held her hands in a frozen library, is not.

"No," he says.

Her eyes fill with tears again.

**Conversation 3 — Lucas (Main Hall):**
Lucas claps him on the shoulder. "You fought like a man with nothing to lose."

"I am a man with nothing to lose."

"That's not true anymore." Lucas gestures around the room — at Iris on the balcony, at Alice wiping her eyes, at Rhea standing alone but at peace. "You have people now, Lucen. Whether you wanted them or not."

Lucas's sincerity is physically painful. This man. This stupid, wonderful, golden-hearted man who was supposed to be the hero.

"You're right," Lucen says. "I do."

"Then fight like it. From now on."

**Conversation 4 — Lyra (Observers' Gallery):**
Lucen finds a quiet stairwell. At the top: Lyra, sitting on a windowsill, eating another piece of butterscotch.

"You showed her nothing," Lyra says.

"I showed her everything. There was just nothing to show."

"Exactly." She grins — a dragon's grin, too wide, too many teeth for a child's face. "That's why it was brave."

She holds out another candy. He takes it.

"You're going to have a very interesting life, Lucen Gray," she says. "I intend to watch."

"From a safe distance, I hope."

"Dragons don't do safe distances." She winks. "We do proximity."

He sighs. Eats the candy.

It's really, really good.

**Cliffhanger:**
Four conversations. Four women. Four threads that will weave through the rest of this story until the world burns or blooms.

He stands alone in the stairwell and looks out the window at the twin moons.

He came to this world to die. He survived to hide. He hid so badly that he became the center of everything.

He's not invisible. He never was. He's a void — and voids attract everything around them.

**Named Bad Ending:** No new flags. Resolution and setup chapter.

### CHAPTER 49 — "Voss's Gift"

**Story Day:** Day 34 (Sunday Night — Shadow Banquet, Late Hours)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray; Vice-Headmistress Elara Voss; the Headmaster (background); a few remaining banquet attendees

**Dramatic Objective:** The banquet is winding down. Most students have left. Voss finds Lucen alone and presents him with a "mentorship gift" — a pendant that, she says, will help "focus his unique abilities." Lucen recognizes it from the game: it's a Resonance Amplifier, a device the Church of Ending uses to magnify mana-void signatures. Wearing it would make his void stronger — and make him a better tool for the Church's purposes.

He can't refuse without revealing that he knows what it is.

He accepts it.

**Central Misunderstanding:** Voss genuinely believes she's helping him. She's not lying when she says the pendant will help him. It will. It will make his void stronger, his dampening more effective, his ability to suppress Abyssal energy more potent. It will also, over time, expand his void to the point where he becomes a weapon capable of ripping open — not just suppressing — the Abyssal seals.

She's giving him a gift that is simultaneously helpful and a slow-acting bomb.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Late night. The banquet is nearly empty. Lucen is sitting in a quiet corner, processing the evening's emotional hurricane. Most students and faculty have retired.

Voss approaches. Warm. Maternal. Carrying a small velvet box.

"Lucen. You fought beautifully tonight."

"I lost."

"You fought with conviction. That's more rare than victory."

**Rising Tension:**
She opens the box. Inside: a pendant on a thin silver chain. A smooth, dark stone — onyx or something similar — with an inner glow that pulses faintly.

"This is a focusing artifact," she says. "Very old, very rare. It's designed to help people with unusual mana constitutions — like yours — channel their abilities more effectively."

He looks at it. He knows exactly what it is. In the game, Lucas discovers Resonance Amplifiers in Year 3 when he uncovers the Church of Ending's plot. They're tools for amplifying mana signatures — designed for void-types specifically, though the Church doesn't advertise this.

If he puts it on, his 0.007 mana void becomes a 0.007 mana void with a megaphone. His dampening range increases. His void-absorption capacity grows. He becomes more effective at suppressing Abyssal incursions — and more useful to Voss.

If he refuses, Voss will know he knows what it is.

**Climax:**
He takes the pendant. Puts it on. It hums against his chest — a subtle vibration that resonates with his void.

"Thank you, Vice-Headmistress."

"Elara."

"Thank you, Vice-Headmistress."

She smiles. Her eyes are kind. Completely, genuinely kind.

"Take care of yourself, Lucen. You're more important than you know."

She leaves. Her shadow lingers in the doorway — and for just a moment, Lucen sees another shadow behind hers. Darker. Older. The silhouette of something vast and patient.

The Church of Ending.

Lucen holds the pendant against his chest. It's warm. It's useful. It's a collar.

### CHAPTER 50 — "End of Term: The Person Who Should Not Exist"

**Story Day:** Day 34 (Sunday Night / Day 35 Dawn — Academy Grounds to Lucen's Dormitory)

**POV:** Lucen Gray — First Person (with final multi-perspective montage)

**Characters Present:** Lucen Gray (alone for most of the chapter); brief montage POVs of all major characters

**Dramatic Objective:** Volume 1 finale. Lucen walks home from the banquet under the twin moons, alone for the first time in weeks. He reviews everything that's happened. He acknowledges — for the first time — that he's changed. That the survival plan has changed. That he's no longer just trying to avoid dying. He's trying to protect people he's come to care about.

This is the most dangerous thing that could have happened to him.

The chapter closes with a multi-perspective montage — every major character, in their own space, thinking about Lucen as the first term ends — and a final System message that reframes the entire volume.

**Central Misunderstanding:** None. This chapter is about clarity. The first moment where Lucen sees his situation for what it actually is, without the filter of panic or game knowledge.

**Beat-by-Beat Progression:**

**Opening Hook:**
Midnight. The campus is empty. Lucen walks the path from the Crystal Ballroom to his dormitory — the same path he walked on Day 1, bruised and bleeding from a ravine, carrying nothing.

Now he walks it wearing four women's gifts, carrying the pendant of a religious conspiracy, bearing the scars of a frozen library and a broken crystal and a three-second duel and a fight he lost on purpose.

The twin moons are full.

He pulls up the System:

```
[Status: Lucen Gray / Kang Min-jun]
[Day: 35]
[Mana Index: 0.007 (Critical)]
[Level: 1]
[All Stats: F]
[Luck: —]
[Status: A person who should not exist.]
```

The same status as Day 0. He hasn't leveled up. He hasn't gained stats. He hasn't become powerful.

But everything else has changed.

**Rising Tension:**
He thinks about each person:

Iris: a girl who has never been cared for, who hides her bleeding heart behind an analytical fortress, who made him tea and fed him soup and put a cravat around his neck that matches her eyes. He's supposed to be afraid of her. He is afraid of her. He also thinks about her every evening when the tea grows cold in the Decryption Sessions.

Alice: a girl carrying an ancient horror, who bakes cookies as a love language and cried in a frozen basement when he told her she was okay. She's bonded to him now — his void and her entity, entangled. If he dies, she falls. He didn't ask for this responsibility. He can't give it back.

Rhea: a girl searching for her father's ghost, who taught him to fight with hands that shook when they touched him. He couldn't give her what she wanted. She accepted his silence with a grace that broke his heart.

Lyra: an ancient being in a child's body, watching him with amused patience, offering candy and calling him interesting. She's a catastrophe waiting to happen, and she likes him, and he doesn't know what to do with that.

Lucas: the hero. The real hero. The one who should be at the center of this story. Lucas is kind and brave and everything Lucen is not, and Lucen's greatest fear is that his presence is dimming Lucas's light.

Voss: the enemy who loves him. The shepherd with kind hands and a slaughterhouse behind the door.

**Climax:**
He reaches his dormitory. Sits on the cot. Holds the Resonance Amplifier pendant.

He makes a decision.

Not to remove the pendant. Not to run. Not to hide. Not to be invisible.

To protect them. All of them. Even if it kills him.

Because he's a person who should not exist, and he exists anyway, and as long as he exists, he might as well matter.

**Iris** in her study, the notebook open, her finger tracing the Hangul characters for his name: 루센 (Rusen). She's smiling. It's the softest expression she's worn since she was six.

**Alice** in her room, the entity dormant, the ice crystal from the greenhouse in her hands. She whispers: "See you tomorrow."

**Rhea** in the training grounds, alone, performing the Ashenveil Retreat. Her footwork is different tonight — unconsciously, she's integrated one of Lucen's dodging patterns. His technique is in her body now. His ghost dances with her father's.

**Lyra** on the academy wall, dragon-eyes reflecting the twin moons. She's humming a song that's older than the continent. She unwraps a butterscotch and eats it. She saves one for tomorrow. For him.

**Lucas** at his desk, writing in a journal. He writes: *"I found someone who makes me want to be better. I don't know why. I just know I'm glad he survived."*

He doesn't know how literal that last sentence is.

**Voss** in her office, hand on the Abyssal node's negative imprint. She whispers: "Sleep well, my little void. We have so much work to do."

He lies on his cot. The pendant glows faintly against his chest. The dormitory is dark. The campus is silent.

He closes his eyes.
