## VOLUME 1 — THE SAVE FILE GLITCH & SURVIVING ORIENTATION

### Chapters 1–50 | Primary Heroine Focus: Iris Aurelia

---

### Thematic Core

Lucen Gray awakens inside the game he speedran 347 hours to 100% — not as the protagonist Lucas Ashford, but as the unnamed mob NPC who dies in the tutorial prologue's carriage explosion. Volume 1 is pure survival comedy: every desperate evasion of death flags gets misread as elegance, every panicked retreat registers as noble restraint, and every mumbled Korean curse word becomes "the mysterious ancient tongue." The volume establishes the foundational irony that will drive all 450 chapters — **the harder Lucen tries to be invisible, the more luminously visible he becomes.**

#### ARC 1A — THE WORST POSSIBLE SPAWN POINT (Ch 1–8)

**Ch 1: "The Game That Ate Me"**

Kang Min-jun, 23, collapses after finishing his 14th completionist run of *Eschaton Hearts* — a dating-sim-meets-apocalypse-fantasy RPG notorious for its 49 named Bad Endings. He wakes inside a rattling carriage. The date on his status window confirms he has exactly 11 minutes before the tutorial carriage detonates.

He recognizes the NPC beside him: Mob Passenger C, whose only canonical line is a scream.

He *is* Mob Passenger C.

**Ch 2: "Eleven Minutes to Live"**

Lucen (his in-world name, pulled from the Status Window reading 'Lucen Gray') scrambles through his game knowledge. The carriage explosion is a scripted event caused by a rogue demon beast beneath the road — it kills all passengers to give protagonist Lucas Ashford his tragic backstory motivation. There is no canon method to survive. Lucen uses his speedrunner memory of terrain geometry to locate the carriage's weakest structural panel (the left rear axle housing) and kicks it loose, tumbling onto the road three seconds before the blast.

He lies in a ditch, bleeding, staring at a system window:

> **[System Alert]**
> You have survived an event with a 0.000% recorded survival rate.
> Generating compensatory status…
>
> **[Status: Lucen Gray]**
> — A person who should not exist.

**Ch 3: "Stats of a Practice Dummy"**

Lucen inspects his full status sheet. Mana Index: 0.009 / 100.000. Strength, Agility, Intelligence, Endurance: all F-rank. Luck: unrated dash '—'. He stares at the Luck field for a long time. The system adds a cheerful annotation:

> [Note: An Evil Goddess warns you to stay away from bitches at all costs!]

He doesn't know what this means yet. He will learn.

**Ch 4: "The Road to Stellaris Royal Academy"**

Lucen limps toward the capital, stitching together a cover story. Mob Passenger C was a scholarship charity case admitted to Stellaris Royal Academy — the elite institution where all main heroines and the protagonist converge. Lucen's plan: attend classes, keep his head down, avoid every single romance flag, graduate quietly, and live as a countryside herbalist.

He arrives at the capital gates and sees the academy's seven spires piercing the sunset. Beautiful. Terrifying. One of those spires collapses in Bad End 47.

**Ch 5: "Registration Day — The Cruelest Lottery"**

Lucen enters the registration hall. He needs to secure a seat in the back of Lecture Hall 7-B — the most irrelevant, overlooked classroom in the entire academy. He rehearsed this. He has a strategy.

The registrar stamps his form. Seat 1-A. Front row. Center.

Of the First Princess's personal lecture section.

> [Bad End No. 12: The Tyrant's Curiosity — triggered when an unknown variable enters Iris Aurelia's direct line of sight during the first week.]

**Ch 6: "Don't Look at the Sun"**

Lucen's first lecture. He sits rigid in Seat 1-A, staring at his blank desk, refusing to make eye contact with anyone. Behind him, the lecture hall buzzes with aristocratic heirs. Ahead of him, at the instructor's observation balcony, First Princess Iris Aurelia watches the new class with molten-gold eyes.

Her gaze passes over him.

Pauses.

Returns.

He feels it like a sunburn on the back of his neck.

**Ch 7: "The Notebook Incident — Part 1"**

Lucen takes notes in Korean (Hangul) — the one script that cannot exist in this world. It's his private shorthand for game mechanics, heroine danger ratings, and Bad End triggers. During a break, he steps away to use the restroom. When he returns, his notebook is gone.

A single golden hair rests on his desk.

**Ch 8: "The Notebook Incident — Part 2"**

Lucen tracks the notebook to Iris's private reading salon using his knowledge of the academy map. He kneels outside the door, composing a polite retrieval request. The door opens before he can knock. Iris stands there, holding his notebook open, her molten-gold eyes burning with something far worse than anger.

Curiosity.

"This script. It's not Ancient Elvarin. Not Draconic cipher. Not any classified Imperial notation." She tilts her head. "What language is this, Lucen Gray?"

His survival plan just caught fire.

#### ARC 1B — THE PRINCESS'S COLLATERAL (Ch 9–20)

**Ch 9: "Decryption Sessions"**

Iris does not return the notebook. Instead, she proposes a trade: she will keep it safe and unread by Imperial intelligence (who would classify it as potential espionage cipher) if Lucen agrees to weekly private sessions where he "decrypts" selected passages for her. Lucen realizes refusal triggers Bad End 12. Acceptance triggers proximity to the single most dangerous heroine in the game.

He accepts.

He hates this.

**Ch 10: "The First Decryption — A Mistranslation That Changes Everything"**

Their first session. Iris points to a passage. Lucen's actual Korean text reads: "Iris — Route 4 Final Boss. DO NOT ENGAGE. Will burn the entire capital if affection drops below 30 during Act 3. Keep distance at all costs. Terrifying woman."

Lucen panics. He "translates" it as: "The morning star rises not to conquer, but to illuminate those beneath her who cannot see their own worth. Her fire is not destruction — it is the only warmth some will ever know."

Iris is silent for nine full seconds.

Her ears turn pink.

"…Continue."

> [System Alert: Iris Aurelia — Affection +5. Current: 15/100]

**Ch 11: "The Weight of Seat 1-A"**

Lucen discovers that Seat 1-A was historically reserved for the Princess's chosen academic rival — a position of honor that also makes him a target for every noble heir seeking to prove themselves. Three aristocratic students challenge him to "courtesy duels" within the first week.

Lucen's combat capability is literally less than a wooden practice dummy.

**Ch 12: "The Art of Cowardly Genius — Duel 1"**

First challenger: Cedric von Hamel, a mid-rank sword noble. Lucen cannot fight. Instead, he uses his knowledge of the dueling arena's structural layout (memorized from a speedrun skip) to position himself so that Cedric's opening charge strikes an unstable training post, which collapses onto a rack of practice shields, which cascade into Cedric's legs.

Cedric trips. Falls. Knocks himself out on the arena floor.

The spectating students erupt. "He didn't even move! He read the entire trajectory!"

Lucen, who simply stood in the one spot he knew was safe from debris, smiles weakly.

> [Note: An Evil Goddess is laughing at your misery. She rates this performance 8/10.]

**Ch 13: "The Art of Cowardly Genius — Duel 2"**

Second challenger: Beatrice Lowell, a wind mage. Lucen forfeits instantly, bowing deeply and declaring he "could not raise a hand against a lady of such evident grace."

Beatrice goes scarlet. The audience murmurs about his chivalry. Iris, watching from the balcony, narrows her eyes.

Lucen's actual internal monologue: *She would've killed me in two seconds flat. Tactical retreat. Tactical retreat.*

**Ch 14: "The Art of Cowardly Genius — Duel 3"**

Third challenger: Marcus Vane, a brute-type brawler who won't accept forfeits. Lucen uses the 3.7-second animation lock he remembers from Marcus's NPC attack pattern — a wide right hook followed by a 0.8-second recovery window — to duck under, stumble past, and accidentally headbutt Marcus in the solar plexus while tripping over his own feet.

Marcus collapses, winded.

The crowd loses their minds.

Professor Veldran, the combat instructor, writes in his evaluation journal: "Lucen Gray. Seat 1-A. Exhibits a martial philosophy I have never encountered. Minimal movement. Maximum efficiency. Requires further observation."

Lucen wants to cry.

**Ch 15: "Why Is She Smiling?"**

Iris summons Lucen for an unscheduled decryption session. She's read ahead in the notebook. She points to a page. His Korean text reads: "If Iris smiles, something terrible is about to happen. Her smile is a declaration of war. DO NOT TRUST THE SMILE."

He translates it as: "Her smile is the blade that parts the night — beautiful, necessary, and unsheathed only when she has already decided to protect someone worth protecting."

Iris closes the notebook gently.

"You see me quite clearly, Lucen Gray."

She smiles.

Something terrible is about to happen.

> [Affection: Iris +3. Current: 23/100]

**Ch 16: "Lucas Ashford Enters the Stage"**

The actual protagonist of *Eschaton Hearts* arrives at the academy two weeks late — standard canon timing. Lucas Ashford: golden hair, emerald eyes, tragic backstory (the carriage explosion killed his mentor), and a Mana Index of 87.4 / 100.000. He's everything a hero should be.

Lucen watches from a window as the entire student body gathers to welcome him.

*Good. Let them look at him. Not at me.*

**Ch 17: "The Protagonist's Handshake"**

Lucas, during his welcoming tour, spots Lucen sitting alone in the courtyard reading a tactics manual. Lucas extends a hand with a radiant smile. "You're Lucen Gray, right? Seat 1-A? I've heard the upperclassmen talking about your dueling record. Let's be friends!"

Lucen shakes his hand while mentally reviewing every crisis Lucas triggers in the main storyline: three continental wars, two apocalyptic summonings, and the collapse of the Saintess's sanity.

"Sure," Lucen says. "Friends."

> [Note: The Evil Goddess is doubled over. She can't breathe.]

**Ch 18: "The True Danger of Friendship"**

Lucen realizes that befriending Lucas means proximity to heroine encounter events. In-game, Lucas's "friendship network" pulls supporting characters into crisis flags like a gravitational field. Lucen resolves to maintain a polite but distant acquaintanceship.

Lucas invites him to lunch every single day for the next week.

**Ch 19: "The First Heroine Collision"**

Lucas drags Lucen to the academy's rooftop garden to "study together." This is the exact location and timing of Lucas's canonical first meeting with the Second Heroine, who will arrive in Volume 2. Lucen tries to leave. Lucas insists he stay. The wind picks up. Petals scatter.

Nobody arrives. The flag hasn't triggered yet — the Second Heroine transfers in during the Festival Arc.

Lucen exhales.

But Iris is standing at the garden entrance, watching them. She does not look pleased that someone else is occupying Lucen's time.

**Ch 20: "Decryption Session 3 — The Temperature Drops"**

Iris's next session is colder. More clinical. She asks about a passage that reads (in Korean): "Lucas is the key to all heroine routes. Support him from the shadows. Never let him die."

Lucen translates: "The golden one is the pillar of this era. I exist only to ensure the pillar does not fall."

Iris stares at him. "You would diminish yourself so completely for another?"

Lucen nods. It's the truth, just not the way she interprets it.

Her expression does something complicated.

"I won't allow it," she says quietly.

#### ARC 1C — THE FIRST EVALUATION EXAM (Ch 21–35)

**Ch 21: "The Exam That Kills Mobs"**

Stellaris Royal Academy's First Evaluation Exam is announced: a multi-stage practical assessment in the Whispering Hollow, a controlled dungeon beneath the academy. Canon knowledge tells Lucen this exam kills three unnamed NPCs in the original game. Since Lucen *is* an unnamed NPC who shouldn't be alive, he suspects the dungeon will try to correct that error.

**Ch 22: "Team Assignments — The Universe Hates Me"**

Teams of four are assigned by lottery. Lucen's team: himself, a nervous healer named Sorin (comic relief ally), a prideful minor noble named Garrett Voss (future mid-boss in Lucas's route), and — by a lottery that Lucen is 90% certain was rigged — Iris Aurelia.

Iris examines her team assignment card. Smiles.

> [The smile. THE SMILE.]

**Ch 23: "Preparation Day — Garrett's Contempt"**

Garrett Voss refuses to strategize with "a commoner with no mana." He declares he'll carry the team alone. Sorin tries to mediate. Iris says nothing, watching Lucen with those gold eyes.

Lucen internally reviews the dungeon layout, enemy spawn timers, treasure chest locations, secret passages, and boss mechanics. He has cleared Whispering Hollow in under 4 minutes on his speedrun. He just can't let anyone know that.

**Ch 24: "Into the Hollow"**

The exam begins. Thirty teams enter the dungeon simultaneously through separate gates. Lucen's team descends into a crystalline cavern network infested with lesser shadow constructs — Rank E monsters that are lethal to anyone with F-rank stats.

Lucen positions himself behind Garrett and maps escape routes.

**Ch 25: "The Mob's Cartography"**

Lucen quietly guides the team by making "suggestions" that happen to navigate them along the dungeon's optimal clear path, avoiding three ambush rooms and a trapped corridor. Garrett takes credit for the "good instincts." Sorin notices Lucen's directions but says nothing.

Iris notices everything.

**Ch 26: "The Second Floor — Garrett Learns Humility"**

Floor 2 contains a mid-boss: a Crystalline Shade, Rank C. Garrett charges in. Gets swatted into a wall. Sorin heals him. Garrett charges again. Gets swatted harder.

Lucen knows this boss has a 12-second vulnerability window after its third slam attack. He waits. Counts. At the exact moment the window opens, he shouts "NOW" and Iris, who has been silently observing, unleashes a single devastating spell that shatters the Shade.

Post-battle, Iris looks at Lucen. "You knew the timing."

Lucen shrugs. "Lucky guess."

> [Note: Your Luck stat is literally unrated. The Evil Goddess wants you to know she appreciates the irony.]

**Ch 27: "The Secret Room"**

Lucen "accidentally" leans against a wall panel that opens a hidden chamber. Inside: a rare artifact — the Cinderveil Pendant, a defensive item that creates a one-time absolute barrier. In-game, this pendant is a critical item for surviving Bad End 38.

Lucen pockets it. No one questions the "lucky find."

> [Item Acquired: Cinderveil Pendant — Single-use absolute barrier. Nullifies one lethal attack.]

**Ch 28: "Floor 3 — The Collapse"**

The dungeon begins to destabilize. This is scripted — the Whispering Hollow always partially collapses on Floor 3 to separate teams and test adaptability. Lucen grabs Sorin and pulls him into a safe alcove he memorized.

Garrett is trapped under rubble on the other side.

Iris is nowhere to be seen.

**Ch 29: "Alone with the Healer"**

Lucen and Sorin navigate the collapsed tunnels. Sorin, terrified, asks how Lucen stays so calm. Lucen's actual reason: he has died to this dungeon 47 times across various speedrun attempts and the geometry is burned into his brain.

What he says: "Panicking won't move the rocks."

Sorin decides Lucen is the bravest person he's ever met.

**Ch 30: "Iris in the Dark"**

Lucen finds Iris alone in a crystalline chamber, surrounded by the shattered remains of six Rank B shadow constructs she killed solo while separated. She's unhurt but her mana reserves are depleted. For the first time, Lucen sees her look — not vulnerable, never vulnerable — but *tired*.

He hands her a mana recovery potion (standard dungeon loot he'd quietly collected).

"You should save this for yourself," she says.

"My mana index is 0.009. That potion would be wasted on me."

She looks at him with an expression he cannot read.

> [Affection: Iris +4. Current: 34/100]

**Ch 31: "The Floor 3 Boss — Abyssal Warden"**

The exam's real test: a Rank A Abyssal Warden guards the exit. In canon, Lucas's team defeats it after a dramatic struggle. Lucen's team was never supposed to reach this boss. The game didn't account for a mob character surviving this far.

The Warden turns its eyeless head toward Lucen. Its status display reads:

> [Abyssal Warden — Rank A]
> [Anomaly detected. Corrective protocol: ENGAGED.]

The dungeon is trying to kill him specifically.

**Ch 32: "0.009"**

Iris is mana-depleted. Sorin can only heal. Garrett is still trapped. Lucen stands before a Rank A boss with F-rank stats and 0.009 mana.

He does the only thing he can: he runs.

Not away. *Through* the Warden's attack pattern, exploiting the 1.3-second gap between its sweeping claw phases that only exists because of a hitbox geometry quirk he abused during his speedruns. He weaves through lethal attacks like water through fingers, not because he's fast, but because he knows exactly where every attack will land.

The spectating professors monitoring via scrying crystals go silent.

**Ch 33: "The Pillar Trick"**

Lucen lures the Warden into charging a load-bearing crystalline pillar — a strategy that only works because he knows the pillar's structural weakness from a specific speedrun route. The pillar shatters. Ten tons of crystal collapse onto the Warden, pinning it.

It's not dead. But it's immobilized.

Iris, using the last dregs of her mana, fires a single concentrated beam into the Warden's exposed core.

The Warden dissolves.

> [Evaluation Exam — Floor 3 Boss: DEFEATED]
> [Team Ranking: 1st (unprecedented for a non-protagonist team)]

**Ch 34: "The Aftermath — The Name They Whisper"**

The exam results are posted. Team Iris/Lucen/Sorin/Garrett placed first overall. Garrett claims credit publicly. Sorin stays quiet. Iris says nothing.

But the combat professors know. The scrying crystal footage is reviewed in a closed faculty meeting. Professor Veldran plays the clip of Lucen weaving through the Warden's attacks eleven times.

"That is not luck," Veldran says. "That is not skill. That is *omniscience*. He knew where every attack would land before the creature moved."

The academy headmaster, Erevan Duskwalde, a man who has not been surprised in forty years, leans forward.

"Watch him."

**Ch 35: "The Hangul Notebook — Iris Reads Further"**

After the exam, Iris holds another decryption session. She opens the notebook to a page Lucen didn't expect her to reach yet. His Korean reads: "The Whispering Hollow Exam — key survival event. Must keep Iris's mana above 40% for Floor 3 boss or team wipes. Optimal route: avoid ambush rooms 2, 5, 7. Use pillar collapse strat on Warden."

She points to the page. "Translate this one."

Lucen's blood runs cold.

She already knows. She navigated the same rooms he avoided. She felt him guide the team. And this page — written *before* the exam — proves he had foreknowledge.

He translates it as: "The labyrinth tests the worthy. A wise man maps the dark before entering. The star does not need a guide, but the guide may still light her path."

Iris sets down the notebook.

"You mapped the dungeon before we entered. You knew the boss's patterns. You guided us without anyone noticing." Her voice is soft as a drawn blade. "Who are you, Lucen Gray?"

"Nobody important."

"Liars don't survive my attention, Lucen."

> [Affection: Iris +6. Current: 40/100]

#### ARC 1D — THE FIRST STORM (Ch 36–50)

**Ch 36: "Rumors Spread Like Fire"**

The student body begins to talk. The mob from Seat 1-A who won three duels without attacking. Who led the first-place evaluation team. Who the First Princess summons for private sessions. Lucen's plan to be invisible has catastrophically failed by Week 3.

**Ch 37: "Lucas's Admiration — A Dangerous Thing"**

Lucas seeks Lucen out with genuine excitement. "You beat the Warden! That pillar trick was genius! Teach me!" Lucen tries to decline. Lucas insists. They begin an "unofficial training partnership" that Lucen desperately hopes won't trigger any heroine proximity events.

Three separate heroines are canonically drawn to Lucas's training locations.

**Ch 38: "The Student Council's Interest"**

The Stellaris Student Council — a political body with genuine authority — sends Lucen an official observation notice. The Council President, a fourth-year named Seraphine Vel Astera, wants to "assess his potential contributions." In canon, Seraphine is a minor antagonist who exploits useful students.

Lucen recognizes her as the trigger for Side Quest 14: "The Council's Cage."

*Another flag. Wonderful.*

**Ch 39: "Garrett's Grudge"**

Garrett Voss, humiliated that the exam credit he claimed is quietly being attributed to Lucen by those who watched the scrying footage, begins plotting revenge. He contacts Marcus Vane (the brawler Lucen headbutted) and Beatrice Lowell (the wind mage Lucen forfeited to) to form an anti-Lucen faction.

Lucen is aware of none of this. He's too busy trying to avoid Iris's decryption sessions.

**Ch 40: "Decryption Session 5 — The Crack in Her Armor"**

Iris asks Lucen to translate a passage he wrote about her Bad End. His Korean: "Bad End 47: Sunrise Eternal. If Iris's affection exceeds 90 while trust remains below 50, she becomes the Dawn Tyrant. "You believe I could become a wildfire?"

"I believe anyone could. That's why standing beside them matters."

The room is very quiet.

A girl with silver-blue hair and amethyst eyes rounds the shelf. She's wearing a junior cadet uniform two sizes too large, eating a stolen pastry, and carrying a book titled *"Compendium of Continental Extinction Events."*

He freezes.

This is Lyra Silverclaw. The Golden Dragon. She isn't supposed to appear until Volume 4.

The concealment spell that hides her presence from everyone in the academy *doesn't work on him* because his mana is too low to interface with the spell's detection threshold. He's so weak he's invisible to invisibility.

"I… yes?"

Lyra's eyes widen. Then narrow. Then sparkle.

"Interesting."

Lyra decides Lucen is "her person" with the absolute certainty of an ancient dragon who has lived long enough to know what she wants. She swears him to secrecy about her presence and identity. In exchange, she'll "protect him from threats he can't perceive."

And Lucas — wonderful, oblivious, golden-hearted Lucas — stands up in the gallery and says, "Lucen's the most honest person I've met here. If he knew the dungeon, it's because he studied harder than anyone. Isn't that what this academy is for?"

## VOLUME 2 — THE GRAND ACADEMY FESTIVAL

### Chapters 51–100 | Primary Introduction: Alice / Celestia Rimehart

---

### Thematic Core

The annual Grand Festival transforms Stellaris into a carnival of class exhibitions, mock tournaments, and social intrigue. Lucas stumbles into crisis after crisis, and Lucen is dragged into covert rescue operations that the heroines — and the student body — increasingly witness. Volume 2 introduces the second heroine, Alice (true name: Celestia Rimehart), whose playful surface conceals an Abyssal Witch origin that could devour the academy. The comedy escalates as Lucen's "mob" disguise erodes and his reputation mutates from "mysterious genius" to "the man even the Princess can't ignore."

#### ARC 2A — FESTIVAL PREPARATIONS & THE TRANSFER STUDENT (Ch 51–65)

**Ch 51: "The Festival Announcement"**

Headmaster Erevan announces the 127th Grand Academy Festival — one month of exhibitions, inter-class competitions, a formal ball, and the highlight: the King's Mock Tournament, where students duel for the Starlight Crest. In canon, Lucas wins the tournament and catches the eye of two heroines simultaneously, triggering a jealousy cascade.

Lucen's plan: work backstage on the most boring committee possible.

He is assigned to the Entertainment Committee, chaired by Iris Aurelia personally.
