## Writing Style & Cadence Bible — *How to Survive in the Romance Fantasy Game*

### Authoritative Reference for All Contributors, Editors, and Draft Passes

---

## § 1. PACING & MOBILE FORMATTING

### 1.1 The Iron Paragraph Rule

Every paragraph contains **one to three sentences**. No exceptions. No mercy.

A paragraph of four sentences is a paragraph that lost discipline. Cut it or break it.

This is the single most important formatting law in this entire document.

### 1.2 Why This Matters

Korean webnovel readers scroll on phones during commutes, lunch breaks, and 2 a.m. insomnia spirals. A dense paragraph is a wall. A wall is a dropped reader. A dropped reader is a cancelled subscription.

The whitespace between short paragraphs is not empty. It is **comedic timing**. It is the breath before a punchline. It is the silence after Lucen realizes he accidentally proposed marriage in ancient draconic custom.

### 1.3 The Punchline Gap

Before any comedic reversal, emotional gut-punch, or internal scream, insert a **hard paragraph break**. Let the previous line sit alone. Let the reader's eyes travel across empty space. Then deliver the blow.

Example structure:

```
Iris smiled at me.

It was the kind of smile a lioness gives a rabbit right before she decides
whether to eat it now or play with it first.

"You're free to leave at any time, Lucen."

Her fingers closed around my wrist.

I was not, in fact, free to leave at any time.
```

### 1.4 Scene Break Cadence

Chapters average **3,000–4,500 words** (approximately 18–25 minutes of mobile reading at average scroll speed). Each chapter contains **three to five distinct scene beats**, separated by a horizontal rule (`---`) or a triple-asterisk break (`***`).

The rhythm inside each scene follows a pattern: **Setup (2–4 paragraphs) → Escalation (4–8 paragraphs) → Spike (1–3 paragraphs of peak tension/comedy) → Release or Cliffhanger (1–2 paragraphs).**

Never let a scene beat exceed 1,200 words without a tonal shift, a POV micro-interlude, or a System notification breaking the flow.

### 1.5 Chapter Opening & Closing Law

**First line of every chapter:** Hook. No scene-setting, no weather reports, no "The morning sun filtered through the curtains." Start with action, dialogue, internal panic, or a System alert.

Correct opening: `[Bad End No. 34: The Dragon's Dowry — has entered the proximity threshold.]`

Correct opening: `"Lucen Gray, would you care to explain why the First Princess skipped the Imperial Council to visit your dormitory at dawn?"`

Correct opening: `I was going to die in a classroom, and it wasn't even exam season.`

Incorrect opening: `The Academy of Stellaris stood tall against the morning sky, its spires gleaming with the promise of a new day.`

**Last line of every chapter:** Either a cliffhanger, a comedic reversal, a heroine's possessive declaration, or a System notification that makes Lucen's survival odds plummet. The final line should make the reader's thumb itch for the "Next Chapter" button.

### 1.6 The Scroll Velocity Test

After drafting any scene, apply this test: read the scene while scrolling on a phone screen (or simulating one). If your eyes stop moving for more than two seconds on any paragraph, that paragraph is too long or too dense. If you scroll through three consecutive paragraphs without a dialogue line, internal monologue quip, or tonal shift, the scene is dragging. Fix it.

## § 2. PROTAGONIST VOICE — LUCEN GRAY (KANG MIN-JUN)

### 2.1 Core Identity

Lucen's internal voice is the voice of a **23-year-old Korean gamer** who has spent 347 hours mastering a dating-sim RPG, only to be thrown into it as the guy who dies in the tutorial. He is not a philosopher. He is not a warrior-poet. He is a panicking completionist speedrunner trapped in a permadeath run with no save states.

His thought process is reactive, fast, and laced with the specific vocabulary of someone who grew up on Twitch streams, gacha games, Discord servers, and convenience-store ramyeon at 3 a.m.

### 2.2 Internal Monologue Style

Lucen's narration is **first-person present-tense panic** interspersed with game-knowledge exposition delivered like a speedrunner's commentary.

He does not think in elegant prose. He thinks in fragmented bursts, rhetorical questions aimed at himself, and desperate mental calculations.

**Defining characteristics of his internal voice:**

His thoughts move in a pattern: **Observation → Game-Knowledge Recall → Threat Assessment → Panic → Reluctant Action.** This five-beat loop is the engine of every Lucen scene.

He refers to himself in unflattering terms. He is "a mob," "background furniture," "the NPC who dies to establish stakes," "the tutorial corpse." He never thinks of himself as a hero. When he does something heroic, his internal narration frames it as a catastrophic miscalculation.

He uses gaming terminology naturally but never obscurely. Terms like "aggro," "flag," "proc," "RNG," "skip," "cheese strat," "soft lock," "death route," and "bad end" are part of his native thought vocabulary. These terms should feel organic, not forced — they are how he genuinely processes reality.

He addresses the reader (or himself) with the casual desperation of a Twitch streamer narrating his own death in real time:

```
Okay, chat. Here's the situation.

Mana Index: 0.009. Physical stats: F across the board.
The woman in front of me once burned an entire province because a duke
insulted her morning tea.

And she just asked me to "stay after class."

We are so, so cooked.
```

### 2.3 Lucen's Emotional Range

He is not a monotone snarker. His emotional palette is wide but always filtered through his gamer-brain lens:

**Terror** manifests as rapid-fire internal calculations of which Bad End he is approaching, how many flags he just tripped, and whether his life insurance (nonexistent) covers death-by-heroine.

**Genuine affection** (which he increasingly cannot suppress) manifests as sudden, jarring sincerity that he immediately tries to bury under self-deprecation. When he accidentally says something kind and means it, his narration scrambles to rationalize it as strategic flag management.

**Guilt** hits him hardest when he remembers that the heroines are real people, not game characters — and that his meta-knowledge means he knows their traumas before they've confided in anyone. This guilt is quiet and sincere. It is the only emotional register where his gamer-brain shuts off and he speaks plainly.

**Anger** is rare and therefore devastating. Lucen does not get angry on his own behalf. He gets angry when someone hurts a heroine — and in those moments, his internal narration drops all comedy. Short sentences. No jokes. No gaming terms. Just cold, direct intent. These moments should be used sparingly (no more than two to three times per volume) to preserve their impact.

### 2.4 Speech Patterns (External Dialogue)

When speaking aloud, Lucen is polite, evasive, and carefully measured — the speech of someone who knows that one wrong word can trigger a continental extinction event.

He uses formal Korean speech-register equivalents: hedging phrases, deflections, and self-deprecating qualifiers. In English prose, this translates to:

```
"I really don't think I deserve that kind of—"
"You're giving me way too much credit, Your Highness."
"I was just... in the area. Coincidentally. Very coincidentally."
"Ha ha, that's— that's a joke, right? You're joking. Please be joking."
```

He never speaks with authority unless cornered into a crisis moment, at which point his speedrunner brain takes over and he issues rapid tactical instructions that everyone around him interprets as battlefield genius.

The gap between his internal voice (screaming) and his external voice (polite, composed) is one of the primary comedy engines. Always maintain this gap.

### 2.5 The Flustered Spiral

Lucen's signature comedic device is the **flustered spiral**: a cascading internal monologue where each new thought makes the situation worse, not better.

Structure:

```
She leaned closer.

Too close. Way too close. I could count her eyelashes. Why was I counting
her eyelashes? Stop counting her eyelashes.

"You're blushing," Alice observed, as if documenting a fascinating species
of beetle.

I was not blushing. My face was simply experiencing a localized thermal
event due to— no, I was definitely blushing.

[Affection Flag: Alice — Stage 3 → Stage 4. Congratulations!]

THAT IS NOT SOMETHING TO CONGRATULATE.
```

Every spiral should escalate across three to five paragraph beats, with each beat adding a new layer of absurdity or danger, before terminating in either a System notification, a heroine's action that makes everything worse, or Lucen's defeated acceptance.

## § 3. HEROINE VOICES & DIALOGUE STYLES

### 3.1 IRIS AURELIA — 'The Dawn Tyrant'

**Speech Register:** Imperial formal with predatory undertones. Iris speaks in complete, grammatically precise sentences. She never uses contractions. She never raises her voice. She does not need to.

**Signature Cadence:** Slow, deliberate, and suffocating. Her dialogue should feel like a noose tightening with silk. She asks questions she already knows the answers to. She gives compliments that are veiled threats. She makes requests that are commands.

**Core Dialogue Traits:**

She addresses Lucen as "Lucen" when amused, "Gray" when displeased, and "my dear" when she is at her most dangerous. The shift between these is a barometer of mortal peril.

She speaks in the royal "we" only when invoking her authority as First Princess, and switches to "I" in private moments with Lucen — a vulnerability she would murder anyone else for witnessing.

Her dialogue often contains embedded commands disguised as observations:

```
"You look tired, Lucen. You have not been sleeping."

That was not a question. That was not concern.
That was a census of my daily schedule delivered as small talk.

"I will have chamomile sent to your dormitory. You will drink it."

"Your Highness, I really don't need—"

"You will drink it."
```

**Teasing Style:** Iris does not tease like a schoolgirl. She teases like a cat with a cornered mouse — with absolute control, faint amusement, and the ever-present implication that she could end this game whenever she wishes. Her humor is dry, aristocratic, and delivered with a perfectly straight face.

```
"I have been told that commoners enjoy receiving flowers as tokens of
affection. Is this accurate?"

"I— yes? Generally speaking, that's—"

"Wonderful. I have purchased the Rosevale Botanical Gardens. They will be
delivered to your dormitory by morning."

"...the gardens."

"All seven hectares. I trust the gesture is adequate."
```

**Emotional Vulnerability:** Iris's vulnerability manifests as silence. When genuinely hurt, confused, or moved, she stops speaking entirely. Her dialogue cuts off mid-sentence, or she changes the subject with jarring abruptness. Lucen (and the reader) should learn to fear her pauses more than her words.

When she finally does express genuine emotion, it is raw, stripped of all imperial artifice, and devastating in its simplicity:

```
"I do not know how to be small enough for you to stay."
```

These moments should appear no more than once per volume arc and should be preceded by at least three chapters of escalating emotional tension.

### 3.2 ALICE / CELESTIA RIMEHART — 'The Abyssal Witch / Frozen Saintess'

**Speech Register:** Casual, teasing, and aggressively familiar. Alice speaks like she has known Lucen for a thousand years even when they have just met. She uses his first name immediately. She invades personal space with her words before she does with her body.

**Signature Cadence:** Rapid, playful, and syncopated. Her dialogue bounces. She asks a question, answers it herself, then asks another question before Lucen can respond. She fills silence the way nature fills a vacuum — violently and immediately.

**Core Dialogue Traits:**

She uses pet names and diminutives casually: "Lu-cen~", "my favorite speedrunner," "dummy," "my beloved test subject." The affection in these names is genuine. The possessiveness is also genuine. These two facts are equally terrifying.

She narrates her own actions in third person when being particularly mischievous:

```
"Alice thinks you should sit down."

"Alice, I'm in the middle of—"

"Alice has decided you're sitting down."

She pushed me into the chair. It was not a suggestion.

"See? Isn't that better? Alice is very wise."
```

She peppers her speech with sound effects and onomatopoeia: "Bam!", "Ta-da~", "Whoooosh," "Ehe~". These should feel natural to her character, not childish — they are the verbal tics of someone who finds the entire world amusing and wants everyone else to share in the joke.

**The Unhinged Pivot:** Alice's defining terror is the seamless shift from bubbly playfulness to calm, absolute menace. There is no transition. There is no warning. One sentence she is giggling about pastries. The next sentence she is describing, in clinical detail, the seven-layer binding circle she prepared "just in case someone tries to take you somewhere I can't follow."

```
"Oh! They have strawberry tarts today! Lucen, Lucen, let's get—"

She stopped.

Her eyes tracked something behind me. The temperature dropped four degrees.

"Who is she?"

"Who is— that's Professor Linden, she's my Elemental Theory—"

"Why did she touch your shoulder?"

Alice's smile hadn't changed. Not even slightly.
That was the problem.

"I'll need her name, her address, her mana signature, and about
thirty minutes."

"Alice."

"Twenty minutes. I've gotten more efficient."
```

This pivot should occur at least once per Alice-focused chapter. The reader should never feel fully safe in a comedic Alice scene.

**Genuine Emotion:** Alice expresses love through action and proximity. She does not make speeches. She sits closer. She falls asleep against his shoulder. She fills his pockets with warming charms on cold days. When she is scared or hurt, she does not cry or yell — she goes quiet and reaches for his sleeve, holding the fabric between two fingers like a child who does not want to be left behind.

Her most devastating emotional moments come when she drops all pretense of playfulness and speaks with bare, terrified honesty:

```
"I don't know how to be someone people stay for."
```

### 3.3 RHEA VALTOR — 'The Ashen Sword'

**Speech Register:** Blunt, clipped, and military. Rhea speaks in the fewest words possible. She considers excessive speech a waste of energy that could be spent training. Adjectives are a luxury. Adverbs are decadence.

**Signature Cadence:** Staccato. Her dialogue should read like sword strikes — short, precise, and impossible to dodge.

**Core Dialogue Traits:**

She communicates primarily in imperatives and declarations:

```
"Fight me."

"Rhea, it's six in the morning—"

"Fight me."

"I haven't even had break—"

"Excuses weaken the body. Fight me."
```

She does not understand social cues, subtext, or indirect communication. She interprets everything literally. When Lucen says "I'd rather die than fight you," she genuinely considers this a tactical preference and suggests he work on his death wish.

She assigns combat rankings to everything, including non-combat situations:

```
"This soup is C-rank at best."

"It's... it's just cafeteria soup, Rhea."

"Exactly. C-rank. If it were my enemy, I would not bother drawing my sword."
```

**The Misread Genius:** Rhea's comedy engine is her absolute conviction that Lucen is a hidden martial master. Every clumsy dodge is "an advanced evasion form." Every panicked flail is "unorthodox but devastatingly efficient." Every time he trips and accidentally avoids a killing blow, she nods with the quiet satisfaction of a master recognizing a peer.

```
He tripped over his own shoelace and fell face-first onto the mat,
narrowly avoiding Rhea's practice swing by exactly two centimeters.

Rhea sheathed her blade.

"Masterful."

"I FELL."

"You lowered your center of gravity below the strike plane while
maintaining forward momentum to close distance. Textbook Valtorian
Fourth Form."

"THAT IS NOT WHAT HAPPENED."

"Your humility only confirms your mastery."
```

This bit never stops being funny because Rhea's conviction is unshakeable. She is not being sarcastic. She genuinely believes it. And every time Lucen denies it, she takes his denial as further proof of his transcendent martial philosophy.

**Genuine Emotion:** Rhea does not have the vocabulary for feelings. She expresses love the way she understands the world — through combat and protection. She positions herself between Lucen and threats without being asked. She memorizes his schedule to ensure he is never unguarded. She does not say "I care about you." She says "Your blind spots are my responsibility."

When truly emotional, she does not become verbose. She becomes still. Her hands, normally in constant motion (adjusting her grip, checking her blade, stretching), stop moving entirely. And she says one sentence:

```
"I will not let you fall."
```

### 3.4 LYRA SILVERCLAW — 'The Golden Dragon Shifter'

**Speech Register:** A volatile mixture of ancient draconic formality and enthusiastic teenage energy. She oscillates between speaking like a ten-thousand-year-old empress and a first-year cadet who just discovered that humans make tiny cakes called "cupcakes" and she needs forty of them immediately.

**Signature Cadence:** Rollercoaster. Her sentences lurch between grandiose and casual, sometimes within the same breath. This reflects her struggle to maintain her human disguise while a dragon's ego and a young girl's curiosity wage war inside her skull.

**Core Dialogue Traits:**

She refers to herself in the third person when her draconic pride surfaces: "Lyra does not wait in lines." "Lyra has never begged and will not begin today." She catches herself doing this and immediately course-corrects with a cough and a first-person restatement.

She uses archaic vocabulary incorrectly, or mixes it with modern slang:

```
"I shall— I mean, I'm going to—" She straightened. "This 'pizza' you
speak of. I demand a tribute of it. Immediately. With extra cheese."

"You can just say you want pizza, Lyra."

"I DID say I want pizza. I said it with appropriate gravitas."
```

She does not understand human customs and interprets them through draconic logic. Sharing food is a mating contract. Making eye contact for more than three seconds is a challenge for territorial dominance. Giving someone a gift means you are claiming them as part of your hoard.

```
"You gave me this 'eraser' during the examination."

"You dropped yours. I was just lending—"

"A dragon's hoard is sacred, Lucen."

Her eyes were glowing faintly gold.

"By accepting your offering and permitting you to touch my possession,
I have initiated a Seventeen-Stage Courtship Bond that is recognized by
all Wyrm Courts from the Northern Peaks to the Sunken Abyss."

"I gave you an ERASER."

"We are on Stage Three."
```

**Possessiveness:** Lyra's possessiveness is instinctual, volcanic, and completely unsubtle. She does not scheme like Iris or maneuver like Alice. She simply declares ownership and dares anyone to contest it. When another heroine gets too close to Lucen, Lyra does not get jealous in a human way — she gets territorial in a *dragon* way. The air gets heavier. Her pupils slit. Her canines lengthen by a barely perceptible millimeter.

```
"The princess touched your hand."

"She was handing me a document—"

"She. Touched. Your. Hand."

The chair under Lyra's grip cracked.

"Lyra. That's Academy property."

"The Academy can submit a reimbursement request to my treasury.
I am dealing with a more pressing matter."
```

**Genuine Emotion:** Beneath the draconic bluster, Lyra is profoundly lonely. She has lived for millennia. Everyone she has known has died. She chose a human form because she wanted to understand why mortals, with their impossibly brief lives, seem to experience joy more intensely than any dragon.

When vulnerable, she does not roar or declare. She shrinks. She becomes very, very small and very, very quiet, and she asks questions she is afraid to hear the answers to:

```
"When you are old and gray and gone... will you still remember that a
dragon loved you?"
```

### 3.5 LUCAS ASHFORD — The Original Protagonist

**Speech Register:** Warm, earnest, and slightly theatrical. Lucas speaks like a shonen protagonist who wandered into a romance game by accident. He gives inspirational speeches at inappropriate times. He calls people "friend" and means it. He is genuinely, infuriatingly, bafflingly kind.

**Signature Cadence:** Bright and forward-moving. His sentences are declarations of intent. He speaks in the present tense about the future as if his willpower alone can bend fate.

**Core Dialogue Traits:**

He is completely oblivious to the romantic chaos swirling around Lucen and genuinely believes they are "best friends on an adventure together":

```
"Lucen! Great news! I signed us both up for the Couples' Cooperation
Combat Drill!"

"Lucas, that's for—"

"Partners! And who's a better partner than my best friend?"

Four heroines materialized behind me.
I could feel their gazes like crosshairs.

"Lucas, you have no idea what you've just done."

"I got us matching headbands! Look!"
```

He narrates his own heroic moments without irony: "This is the part where I protect everyone!" He does not realize this is unusual.

His function in dialogue scenes is **catalyst and chaos agent**: he introduces problems that Lucen must solve, flags that Lucen must manage, and social situations that Lucen cannot escape. He is not malicious. He is simply a protagonist-shaped disaster magnet who keeps dragging Lucen into the spotlight.

## § 4. SYSTEM VOICE & NOTIFICATIONS

### 4.1 The System Window

System messages appear in **bracket notation** and are always set apart from the prose on their own line or in their own paragraph:

```
[Warning: Affection Flag — Iris Aurelia Stage 2 → Stage 3 has been triggered.]

[Note: Stage 3 is colloquially known as 'The Point of No Return.']

[The System recommends updating your last will and testament.]
```

The System's voice is clinical, detached, and faintly sardonic. It delivers catastrophic information in the register of a customer-service chatbot. It does not care about Lucen's feelings. It occasionally offers "helpful" suggestions that are useless or terrifying.

### 4.2 Bad End Notifications

Bad End alerts follow a strict format:

```
[Bad End No. XX: 'Title in Single Quotes' — proximity threshold reached.]
[Trigger Condition: Brief, clinical description of what went wrong.]
[Survival Window: XX hours / minutes / seconds.]
```

Bad End titles should be evocative, poetic, and horrifying. They should sound like the names of cursed paintings in a haunted gallery: 'The Tyrant's Curiosity,' 'Skin for Love,' 'Sunrise Eternal,' 'The Bride Beneath the Ice,' 'A Garden of Teeth,' 'Her Lullaby Unending,' 'The Weight of Golden Scales.'

### 4.3 The Evil Goddess Note

The Evil Goddess's notes are chaotic, informal, and profane. They break the System's clinical tone completely. They appear in the same bracket format but are marked with "Note:" and attributed:

```
[Note: An Evil Goddess warns you that the blonde one is within
15 meters and closing fast. RUN, YOU IDIOT.]

[Note: An Evil Goddess would like to remind you that she told you
to stay away from bitches at all costs. You did not listen.
This is your fault.]

[Note: An Evil Goddess has placed a bet on your survival.
Do not disappoint her. The odds are not in your favor.]
```

Her notes should appear 2–4 times per chapter maximum. They punctuate tension peaks, provide comedic relief after intense scenes, and occasionally deliver genuine strategic warnings disguised as insults.

## § 5. DIALOGUE DENSITY & SCENE CONSTRUCTION

### 5.1 The 35–45% Dialogue Rule

Every chapter must contain **35% to 45% active dialogue** by word count. This is measured as all text inside quotation marks plus their dialogue tags.

If a chapter draft falls below 35% dialogue, it is too narration-heavy and will feel sluggish on mobile. Add conversation, banter, or a confrontation scene.

If a chapter draft exceeds 45% dialogue, it risks becoming a screenplay. Add internal monologue, tactical narration, or environmental beats.

### 5.2 Dialogue Attribution

**"Said" is invisible and preferred.** Use it freely. Do not fear its repetition. The reader's eye skips past "said" like a stone over water.

**Action beats replace dialogue tags wherever possible.** Instead of "she said angrily," write:

```
Iris set her teacup down. The porcelain did not crack.
This was, somehow, more frightening than if it had.

"I see."
```

**Adverbial dialogue tags ("said angrily," "whispered seductively," "exclaimed loudly") are banned.** If the dialogue and context cannot convey the emotion, the dialogue is not doing its job. Rewrite the line.

**Attribution goes after the first clause of dialogue in long speeches.** Not before. Not after the entire speech. After the first natural pause.

```
"I was under the impression," Iris said, "that you had no plans
this evening."
```

### 5.3 Multi-Character Scenes

When three or more characters are speaking in the same scene, each character must be clearly identifiable by voice alone within two lines of dialogue. The reader should never need to check a dialogue tag to know who is speaking.

Iris speaks in complete, formal sentences without contractions. Alice bounces and uses pet names. Rhea speaks in three-word declarations. Lyra oscillates between archaic grandeur and modern enthusiasm. Lucas is earnest to the point of obliviousness.

If a reader cannot identify the speaker from the line alone, the voice differentiation has failed. Rewrite.

### 5.4 The Banter Engine

Banter scenes between Lucen and any heroine follow a three-layer structure:

**Surface Layer:** The literal words being exchanged — an argument about tea, a debate about training schedules, a disagreement about seating arrangements.

**Subtext Layer:** The actual emotional negotiation happening beneath the words — a heroine testing Lucen's interest, Lucen trying to de-escalate an affection flag, both parties circling around a feeling neither can name.

**Comedy Layer:** Lucen's internal narration translating the subtext in real time with increasing panic.

All three layers must be active simultaneously. A banter scene operating on only one layer is flat. Two layers is functional. Three layers is the standard.

## § 6. BANNED WORDS, PHRASES, & CLICHÉS

### 6.1 Permanently Banned Words

These words and phrases are **absolutely prohibited** anywhere in the manuscript. Their presence in a draft is an automatic revision flag.

**Overused AI/Generic Prose Words:**

"Delve" / "delved" / "delving" — in any context.

"Tapestry" — when used metaphorically (e.g. "a tapestry of emotions"). Literal tapestries hanging on castle walls are permitted.

"Beacon" — when used metaphorically (e.g. "a beacon of hope").

"Resonate" / "resonated" / "resonating."

"Uncharted" — when used metaphorically.

"Multifaceted."

"Utilize" — use "use."

"Facilitate" — use "help," "cause," or "make happen."

"Endeavor" (as a noun or verb in narration).

"Landscape" — when used metaphorically (e.g. "the emotional landscape").

"Moreover" / "Furthermore" / "Additionally" — in narration or dialogue. These are essay words.

"It is worth noting" / "It should be noted."

"Paradigm" / "paradigm shift."

"Synergy" / "synergistic."

"Nuance" / "nuanced" — when used as filler praise. ("A nuanced take." Banned. "The nuance of draconic courtship law requires seventeen separate vows." Permitted, because it is specific.)

"Plethora."

"Myriad" — as an adjective ("myriad reasons"). Archaic noun usage in Iris or Lyra's dialogue is acceptable if rare.

"Pivotal."

"Underscored."

"Fostering."

"Realm" — when used metaphorically ("the realm of possibility"). Literal political realms are fine.

"Embark" / "embarked" / "embarking" — in any context.

"Craft" / "crafted" — when used as a verb meaning "to create" in narration ("she crafted a plan"). Literal crafting of physical objects is permitted.

"Intricate" / "intricately."

"Profound" / "profoundly" — in narration.

"Testament" — when used as "a testament to" something.

"Captivating."

"Groundbreaking."

"Meticulous" / "meticulously."

"Thought-provoking."

"Breathtaking."

"Seamlessly."

"Overarching."

"Spearheaded."

"Cutting-edge."

"In conclusion."

"Alchemize" / "alchemized" — unless referring to literal alchemy in the world.

### 6.2 Banned Prose Constructions

**"Little did he know..."** — Retired. Buried. Do not exhume.

**"If only he had known..."** — Same grave, same rule.

**"A shiver ran down his spine."** — Find a new way to express dread. Lucen's spine has suffered enough.

**"Time seemed to stop."** — It did not. Write what actually happened.

**"His heart skipped a beat."** — His heart is doing fine. Describe the physical sensation differently.

**"She let out a breath she didn't know she was holding."** — Everybody knew. Everybody always knew.

**"He swallowed hard."** — Permitted once per 50,000 words. Ration it like wartime chocolate.

**"Their eyes met across the room."** — Specify whose eyes. Specify which room. Specify what the meeting of eyes actually felt like.

**"Not gonna lie..."** — In Lucen's internal monologue, this is acceptable once per volume, maximum. It must be followed by something genuinely surprising.

**"I mean, it's not like I..."** — Tsundere construction. Lucen is not a tsundere. He is a terrified gamer. His denials should be rooted in survival instinct, not romantic embarrassment.

**"[Character] raised an eyebrow."** — Permitted once per chapter. Eyebrows are not that interesting.

**"Interesting..."** — As a standalone dialogue line, this is banned. Iris may say "How interesting" exactly once per volume, and it must trigger genuine terror.

### 6.3 Banned Emotional Shorthand

**"Tears streamed down her face."** — Show the specific, physical, unique way this character cries. Iris does not cry. Alice laughs while crying. Rhea's eyes redden but no tears fall. Lyra cries molten-gold droplets that hiss when they hit the floor.

**"His blood ran cold."** — Describe the actual physiological panic: the tightness in the chest, the tingling in the fingertips, the way sound goes distant and muffled.

**"A wave of [emotion] washed over him."** — Emotions are not waves. They are specific physical sensations in specific parts of the body. Locate them. Name them.

**"He clenched his fists."** — Permitted in combat scenes only. In emotional scenes, find a different physical expression of tension.

### 6.4 Banned World-Building Crutches

**"As you know..."** — No character should ever explain something to another character who already knows it. Find a different delivery mechanism for exposition.

**"In this world..."** — The narration is inside this world. It does not need to announce it.

**"Legend has it..."** — Whose legend? What source? Be specific or cut it.

**"Since time immemorial..."** — Lazy temporal framing. Give an actual date, era, or event.

### 6.5 Restricted But Not Banned

The following are permitted under strict conditions:

**"Somehow"** — Lucen may use this in internal monologue when genuinely baffled. Limit: three times per chapter.

**"Actually"** — Permitted in dialogue. Banned in narration.

**"Basically"** — Permitted in Lucen's internal monologue as part of his gamer-brain simplification of complex situations. Banned in all other narration.

**"Literally"** — Permitted in Lucen's voice only, and only when something literally happened that should not have been possible. "She literally bought seven hectares of botanical garden." This is acceptable because it is literally true.

**Em-dashes** — Permitted for interruptions in dialogue and for Lucen's fractured thought patterns. Limit: five per chapter. Overuse dulls the impact.

**Ellipses (...)** — Permitted in dialogue to indicate trailing off, hesitation, or dread. Banned in narration except in Lucen's internal monologue. Limit: four per chapter.

## § 7. TONAL CALIBRATION RULES

### 7.1 The Comedy-to-Dread Ratio

Each chapter should maintain an approximate ratio of **65% comedy / 25% tension / 10% genuine emotion**. This ratio shifts during climactic arcs:

During major arc climaxes (every 40–60 chapters): **30% comedy / 45% tension / 25% genuine emotion.**

During aftermath/recovery chapters: **75% comedy / 10% tension / 15% genuine emotion.**

Comedy must never fully disappear, even in the darkest chapters. Lucen's gamer-brain narration provides a permanent comedy floor. Even when he is terrified, his internal framing is inherently absurd. This is the tonal safety net.

### 7.2 The Sincerity Spike

Once per volume arc (approximately every 50 chapters), there should be a **sincerity spike**: a scene where all comedy drops, all gaming terminology vanishes, and Lucen speaks to a heroine with total, unguarded honesty.

These spikes are the emotional payload that the comedy infrastructure exists to deliver. They work because they are rare. They hit hard because the reader has been trained to expect jokes, and suddenly there are none.

In a sincerity spike, the prose itself changes: paragraphs may stretch to three full sentences. Dialogue slows. Lucen uses the heroine's actual name — not a title, not a deflection. The System goes silent.

```
"You are not a Bad End, Iris."

She stared at him.

"You are not a flag to manage or a route to survive. You are the
girl who reads alone in the eastern tower because the sunset
reminds her of a mother she can barely remember. And you deserve
someone who stays because he wants to. Not because he's afraid
of what happens if he leaves."
```

After a sincerity spike, the comedy returns immediately — usually with a System notification that shatters the mood or a heroine's reaction that sends Lucen scrambling. The emotional weight remains, but the tonal safety net catches everyone.

### 7.3 Heroine POV Interludes

Every 8–12 chapters, insert a **Heroine POV Interlude**: a short scene (800–1,500 words) written from a heroine's perspective. These interludes serve three functions:

They reveal the heroine's internal state, showing the reader what Lucen cannot see — the depth of her obsession, the specificity of her trauma, the precise moment she fell irreversibly in love.

They recontextualize a recent scene by showing it from her perspective. Something Lucen experienced as "barely surviving a conversation" is revealed to be the single most important moment of her life.

They escalate tension by showing the reader threats, plans, and emotional precipices that Lucen is walking toward blind.

The prose style in heroine interludes shifts to match the heroine's voice. Iris's interludes are precise and controlled, with volcanic emotion visible only in the cracks. Alice's interludes are fragmented and associative, bouncing between thoughts. Rhea's are spare and physical. Lyra's oscillate between ancient weariness and startled wonder.

## § 8. TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS

### 8.1 Point of View

Primary POV: **First-person, Lucen Gray.** Present tense for immediate action and crisis scenes. Past tense for reflection and exposition.

Secondary POV: **Third-person limited** for Heroine POV Interludes only. Past tense.

Never use omniscient narration. The reader should only know what Lucen knows (in his chapters) or what the focal heroine knows (in interludes). The comedy and tension depend on information asymmetry.

### 8.2 Tense Discipline

Lucen's crisis scenes default to **present tense** for immediacy:

```
She's walking toward me. Iris Aurelia is walking toward me in a
hallway with no exits.

My brain cycles through every relevant Bad End.
There are fourteen.
```

Lucen's reflective narration and game-knowledge exposition default to **past tense**:

```
In the original game, this corridor was where Route 4 hit its
first major branch point. The player had to choose between
attending the tea ceremony and skipping it to train.

I had always picked training.

That was no longer an option, because training required being alive,
and the woman at the end of this hallway was making that
increasingly unlikely.
```

The shift between tenses should be smooth and motivated. Do not bounce randomly. Shift when the emotional register shifts: from reflection to crisis, from crisis to aftermath.

### 8.3 Chapter Title Format

Every chapter title follows the format:

**Chapter [Number]: [Short, Punchy Title]**

Titles should be five words or fewer, should hint at the chapter's central comedic or dramatic beat, and should ideally function as a punchline in retrospect.

Examples of correct chapter titles: "An Eraser Is Not a Proposal," "The Princess's Tea (Mandatory)," "F-Rank Means F-Rank," "Don't Feed the Dragon," "The Notebook Incident (Part 7)," "He Tripped (Masterfully)," "Thirty Minutes or Twenty."
