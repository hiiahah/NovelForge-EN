## 1. Core Logline / One-Sentence Summary

A completionist speedrunner wakes up as the statless, lore-irrelevant mob character who dies in the tutorial of his favorite romance-fantasy game — and every desperate act of cowardly self-preservation he takes to avoid 49 named Bad Endings is catastrophically misread by the game's heroine-tier yandere raid bosses as tragic nobility, quiet genius, and self-sacrificing love, dragging him deeper into the lethal harem routes he was trying to flee while the world's seven extinction-level Calamities creep toward ignition on a clock only he can see.

---

## 2. Expanded 3-Sentence Hook

**Premise.** Kang Min-jun, a 23-year-old who sank 347 hours into the romance-fantasy epic *Eschaton Hearts*, opens his eyes as Lucen Gray — a character so irrelevant the system status screen itself reads *"A person who should not exist"* — with a mana index of 0.009, all F-rank stats, and an unrated Luck score displayed as a blank dash, dropped into the ruthless Stellaris Royal Academy where the five main heroines are continent-destroying raid bosses hidden behind beautiful faces and the original protagonist Lucas Ashford is a golden-boy hero whose unresolved crisis flags will detonate every catastrophe in the game's lore.

**Escalation.** Every time Lucen ducks a death flag — faking illness to skip a duel, memorizing patrol routes to avoid a possessive First Princess, scribbling survival notes in Korean he assumes no one can read — the heroines interpret his evasion as enigmatic self-sacrifice, his silence as noble restraint, and his frantic notebook as a cipher of forbidden genius, until the Dawn Tyrant Iris Aurelia confiscates his Hangul guide and leverages it into mandatory private "decryption sessions," the Abyssal Witch Alice starts drawing territorial magical circles around his dorm room, the Ashen Sword Rhea declares his panicked dodging a lost school of swordsmanship, the ancient Golden Dragon Lyra begins nesting in his closet, and the System itself starts firing off increasingly unhinged warnings — *[Note: An Evil Goddess warns you to stay away from bitches at all costs!]* — while his Bad End counter ticks upward past numbers he didn't even know the game had.

**Climax.** When the seven canonical Calamities begin cascading ahead of schedule — each one tied to a heroine's unresolved trauma route he has been accidentally deepening — Lucen is forced to stop running, burn every scrap of his 347-hour completionist knowledge, and attempt what the game's code declared mathematically impossible: resolve all five heroine routes simultaneously, neutralize every extinction event, and survive the True Ending with 0.009 mana and zero combat stats, armed with nothing but walkthrough memory, catastrophic luck, and the terrifyingly sincere devotion of five women who would each individually burn the continent to ashes before letting him die.

## 3. The Three Core Selling Points (Webnovel Dopamine Hooks)

### Selling Point 1: "The Coward's Paradox" — Misinterpretation Comedy Perfected

Every chapter runs on a dual-reading engine. The reader sees Lucen's internal monologue — panicked, calculating, self-deprecating, desperately trying to minimize his existence — and then immediately witnesses the heroine POV interlude where the exact same action is filtered through infatuation, reverence, or obsessive analysis into something devastatingly romantic. He eats lunch alone to avoid a flag; Iris concludes he is performing aristocratic solitude as a political statement and rearranges the entire academy seating chart to place herself beside him. He memorizes escape routes through the dormitory; Rhea tracks his movement patterns and determines he is practicing an extinct evasion martial art at a grandmaster level. He flinches when Alice touches him; Alice decides his "trembling restraint" proves he is the only man alive with the willpower to resist her, and she has never wanted anything more. The comedy is architectural — it does not rely on one-off gags but on a compounding system where each misunderstanding becomes load-bearing evidence for the next, creating an ever-escalating tower of romantic misreadings that the reader knows will eventually, hilariously, catastrophically collapse. The gap between Lucen's perceived identity (legendary enigma, tortured genius, selfless guardian) and his actual identity (terrified speedrunner with garbage stats begging the universe to let him live) is the foundational comedic engine, and it never runs dry because the heroines are too intelligent — they construct elaborate, internally consistent, completely wrong theories to explain him, and their theories reinforce each other.

### Selling Point 2: "Affection Is a Threat Level" — Yandere Raid-Boss Heroines

The heroines are not decorative love interests. Each one is a walking continental disaster whose Bad End is a named, numbered extinction event the reader has been shown in explicit, horrifying detail. Iris Aurelia's Bad End No. 47, *Sunrise Eternal*, depicts her burning the imperial capital to molten glass when her possessiveness breaks containment. Alice's Bad End No. 63, *Skin for Love*, is a reality-warping event where the Abyssal Witch rewrites the laws of physics to trap her beloved in an inescapable pocket dimension forever. The reader understands at every moment that the adorable scene of Lyra falling asleep on Lucen's shoulder is occurring on the back of a creature that could level a mountain range, and that Rhea's quiet blush when he accidentally compliments her swordsmanship is happening on a woman who bisects fortress walls for morning practice. Romance and mortal danger are chemically bonded. A heroine blushing is a threat. A heroine smiling is an escalation. A heroine saying "I don't mind if you run — I'll always find you" is a Bad End proximity alarm. This is the hook that keeps readers clicking "next chapter" at 3 AM: every fluff scene is laced with the electric knowledge that these women are catastrophically powerful, their love is not safe, and the only thing between the world and annihilation is a man with 0.009 mana desperately trying to keep all of them at exactly the right emotional temperature — warm enough to prevent their despair-triggered Bad Ends, cool enough to prevent their obsession-triggered Bad Ends, in a margin of error that shrinks every chapter.

### Selling Point 3: "The Walkthrough Is Burning" — Speedrunner Knowledge vs. Diverging Reality

Lucen's only weapon is encyclopedic game knowledge, and the story systematically, agonizingly strips it away. His presence has already broken canon — he survived a tutorial death the code didn't account for, his existence is rewriting NPC behavior trees, and heroine routes are beginning to merge and conflict in ways no playthrough ever produced. By Volume 3, events are arriving 40 chapters early. By Volume 5, entire quest chains he memorized have been replaced by scenarios the game never contained. His walkthrough is a depreciating asset, and every chapter the reader watches it lose value while the stakes climb higher. This creates a ratcheting tension engine: the reader is addicted to watching Lucen improvise with decreasing information against increasing danger, knowing that his meta-knowledge advantage — the one thing keeping a 0.009-mana nobody alive in a world of demigods — has an expiration date. When it finally runs out completely in Volume 7 and he faces the Starfall Lament with zero foreknowledge for the first time, the payoff is enormous precisely because the reader has spent hundreds of chapters watching the safety net fray thread by thread.

## 4. The Central Emotional Engine

The emotional engine runs on a three-fuel mixture that must be present in precise ratios in every chapter block to sustain reader engagement across 450+ chapters:

### Fuel 1: Existential Fear (The "Oh God, I'm Going to Die" Current)

Lucen is the weakest sentient entity in the setting. His mana index of 0.009 is lower than a wooden practice dummy's passive mana absorption. His stats are all F-rank. His Luck is not even rated — displayed as a dash, meaning the system itself cannot calculate whether he is lucky or unlucky, which is more frightening than either. He exists in a world where students casually shatter stone in sparring, where professors can flatten buildings as demonstrations, and where the main heroines are each individually capable of ending civilizations. He has no plot armor — the system has explicitly told him he should not exist, and he has seen the 49 named ways he can die. This fear is not abstract. It is specific, numbered, and described. The reader has read Bad End No. 12 and knows exactly what Iris does to people who make her curious. The reader has read Bad End No. 63 and knows what Alice's love looks like when it loses all restraint. Every scene of Lucen walking through the academy hallways carries the ambient dread of a man navigating a minefield where the mines have names, smiles, and strong opinions about where he should sit at lunch.

### Fuel 2: Romantic Fluff (The "My Heart Can't Take This" Warmth)

Against this backdrop of existential terror, the romantic moments hit with magnified intensity precisely because they are earned through danger. When Iris — the Dawn Tyrant, architect of Bad End No. 47 — quietly slides her untouched dessert across the table to Lucen because she noticed he skipped breakfast, the gesture carries the weight of a woman whose canonical response to emotional vulnerability is incinerating everything within a kilometer radius choosing, instead, to do something small and gentle and human. When Alice stops teasing and simply rests her head against his shoulder in silence after he accidentally says something that touches her real trauma beneath the Abyssal Witch persona, the reader feels the fluff ten times harder because they know what she is and what she could become. The formula is simple and devastating: the more dangerous the heroine, the softer the fluff hits. A quiet moment with Lyra — who is an ancient dragon curled up in human form, voluntarily making herself small to fit beside him on a bench — is not just cute. It is a creature of apocalyptic power choosing tenderness, and the reader's heart cannot survive it.

### Fuel 3: Protective Yandere Devotion (The "She Would Burn the World for Him" Thrill)

The third fuel is the dark mirror of the fluff: moments where a heroine's love crosses from warmth into something that makes the reader's spine tingle with a mixture of swooning and genuine alarm. Iris calmly informing a duke's son that if he speaks to Lucen in that tone again she will have his family's territory reclassified as "unclaimed wilderness." Alice cheerfully telling a group of bullies that she has already placed a delayed-activation curse on their bloodlines and it would be such a shame if something triggered it. Rhea silently stepping between Lucen and a senior instructor, hand on her sword, eyes flat and empty, and the instructor backing down because he can feel that she is not bluffing, not posturing, not performing — she has already decided to kill him and is simply waiting to see if it becomes necessary. These moments are the reader's guilty pleasure, the scenes they screenshot and share, the reason the comment sections fill with "I'M SCARED BUT ALSO IN LOVE." The devotion is real. The threat is real. Both exist simultaneously, and the reader wants more of both.

### The Ratio

The three fuels must cycle in a rhythm: Fear establishes stakes → Fluff provides emotional relief and deepens attachment → Protective Devotion validates the attachment while re-establishing danger → which feeds back into Fear. This cycle runs on a micro scale (within each chapter) and a macro scale (across each volume arc). The ratio shifts as the story progresses: early volumes lean heavier on Fear and Comedy, middle volumes deepen Fluff and emotional intimacy, and late volumes escalate Protective Devotion to continental scale as the Calamities force the heroines to reveal the full, terrifying magnitude of what they are willing to do — and destroy — to keep Lucen alive.

## 5. The 450-Chapter Climax Promise

### The Contract with the Reader

By Chapter 1, the story makes an implicit promise: *This man will survive the unsurvivable, solve the unsolvable, and achieve the ending the game's own code declared impossible — and he will do it not with power, but with knowledge, desperation, and the accidental, unearnable, world-rewriting love of women who should have destroyed him.* The 450-chapter climax is the fulfillment of that contract.

### The Seven Calamities

The *Eschaton Hearts* game contains seven Calamity-class extinction events, each tied to a heroine's unresolved emotional trauma and triggered by specific narrative failure conditions. In the original game, a player could only address one heroine's route per playthrough, meaning they could prevent at most one or two Calamities while the others fired in the background as tragic, unavoidable lore events. No playthrough in the game's history — across all difficulty modes, all endings, all secret routes — has ever resolved all seven simultaneously. The game's internal data files contain a commented-out flag labeled `TRUE_END_HAREM` with a developer note: *"mathematically impossible under current affection/crisis architecture. Left in as a joke. —K."*

Lucen, armed with knowledge of all routes, all triggers, and all failure conditions, is the only entity in existence positioned to attempt the impossible: running all five heroine routes concurrently, managing their intersecting crisis flags in real time, preventing each Calamity by resolving its root trauma, and doing it all without the heroines discovering he has meta-knowledge — because if they learn the truth about what he is, it triggers Bad End No. 49, *The Dreamer's Collapse*, in which the world itself rejects him as a foreign object and unravels.

### The Escalation Ladder (Volumes 1-8)

**Volumes 1-3 (Chapters 1-200): "The Juggler."** Lucen establishes his survival framework. He identifies the five heroine routes he must manage, maps the early crisis flags, and begins his delicate balancing act. The stakes are personal — individual Bad Ends, academy-scale threats, social catastrophes. He is juggling five increasingly unstable emotional relationships while maintaining the fiction that he is a nobody. The comedy is at its peak. The reader is laughing and wincing simultaneously.

**Volumes 4-5 (Chapters 201-370): "The Savior."** The stakes escalate to national scale. The Emperor notices Lucen. Heroine routes begin intersecting in ways that produce new, unscripted crisis flags. Lucen is forced to abandon passive evasion and begin actively intervening — saving heroines from their canonical deaths, confronting traumas he only knew as text boxes, and forming genuine emotional bonds that terrify him because they are real, not just game mechanics. The fluff deepens. The reader starts crying. The heroines begin to realize, independently, that they would choose Lucen over the world — and they mean it literally.

**Volume 6 (Chapters 371-410): "The Conspirator."** Continental-scale threats emerge. The Church, the Chancellor's treason, and the first stirrings of the Calamity cascade force Lucen into the political arena. His walkthrough knowledge is now 60% obsolete. He must improvise. The heroines, independently investigating the same threats, begin converging on the truth about each other's feelings for Lucen, and the resulting territorial escalation threatens to crack the academy — and possibly the continent — in half. The protective yandere devotion reaches institutional scale: Iris quietly restructures imperial intelligence to shield Lucen, Alice rewrites the academy's defensive wards to respond to threats against him specifically, Rhea challenges and defeats three knight-captains who were ordered to surveil him.

**Volume 7 (Chapters 411-440): "The Starfall Lament Ignites."** The first Calamity fires. Then the second. Then the third. The cascade has begun. Lucen's walkthrough knowledge is now functionally zero — events have diverged so far from any recorded playthrough that he is flying blind for the first time since Chapter 1. The heroines learn fragments of the truth: not what Lucen is, but that he has been protecting them — all of them — from the beginning, at unimaginable personal cost, with no power of his own. The emotional detonation this revelation produces is the story's most devastating sequence. Each heroine's response to learning the truth is filtered through her specific psychology and trauma, producing five distinct, overwhelming, terrifying declarations of devotion that the reader has waited 400+ chapters to witness.

**Volume 8 (Chapters 441-450): "The True Ending."** Lucen stands at the convergence point of all seven Calamities with 0.009 mana, no combat stats, a burned-out walkthrough, and five women who have each individually chosen to rewrite the rules of reality rather than let him die. The final ten chapters deliver the impossible: the `TRUE_END_HAREM` flag activates. Not through power. Not through some hidden cheat code or late-game power-up that betrays the story's premise. Through the precise, accumulated, compound interest of 440 chapters of desperate survival decisions, each one small, each one seemingly insignificant, each one a thread in a web so vast and intricate that even the System — the game's own architecture — cannot find a valid reason to reject it. Lucen Gray, the person who should not exist, forces existence to make room for him. Not by becoming strong. By being so relentlessly, pathetically, beautifully human that five demigod women and one broken world decide, against all mathematical probability, that he is worth keeping.

### The Final Line the Reader Has Been Waiting 450 Chapters For

The System status screen flickers one last time. Where it once read *"A person who should not exist,"* a new line appears — not generated by the game's code, but written into reality by the combined will of five heroines who refused to accept a world without him:

*Status: A person who was loved into existence.*
